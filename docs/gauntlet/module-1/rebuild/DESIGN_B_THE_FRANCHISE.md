## 1. THE ONE SENTENCE

> **You inherit one real NBA club's actual books — three weeks before training camp — and you spend
> three sessions discovering that your job is not to build the best team, it is to choose which of
> your problems you are willing to keep.**

Teacher's repeatable version, twelve words: *"You run a real club's money, and every fix costs you a
different fix."*

---

## 2. THE CORE FANTASY

**You are handed something real, broken, and yours.**

A ten-year-old leans in because the screen opens on a named place with a named problem and their name
is now on it. Not "Team 4." **Milwaukee**, and the third-biggest line on your payroll is
**$21,311,053 a year to Damian Lillard, who plays for somebody else, until 2031** (§8.1 #21). You did
not do that. It is yours now. That is the oldest and strongest fantasy in the genre — the inherited
mess — and it is free, because the real league supplies it already sourced.

A thirteen-year-old leans in for a different reason: **the room is not fair and the unfairness is
legible.** Detroit has $28.7M of room and seven first-round picks. Denver is $11.35M past the second
apron and *every roster fix it wants is literally illegal*. Oklahoma City just won and has
**$2,820,601** of space (§4.1 #2). A thirteen-year-old can see that the game is asymmetric, and the
first thing they want to do is argue about it. That argument is the lesson.

Both bands lean in on the same beat, which is the point of one shared world: **the moment a desk
learns what its neighbour inherited.** Not "who is winning" — "wait, they had *how much*?"

The fantasy is not GM-as-genius. It is **GM-as-inheritor**: the person who walks into someone else's
decisions and has to live inside them. That is also, exactly, what the economics is.

## 3. THE FALSE INTUITION AND HOW IT BREAKS

The module opens on the intuition the founder named:

> **"My job is to build the best basketball team possible."**

### Where it breaks — one named moment

**Lesson 1, Camp Day 2, THE TOOL CALL.**

By Day 2 the pair has an OPEN job on its roster and knows exactly who fills it. On the board sits
**Cooper Flagg at $14,517,480** — a genuinely top-tier player at a rookie-scale price, because the
rookie scale is an administered ladder and not a market (§8.1 #9, §4.5). The pair can afford him.
The move is right. The move is legal.

And the only tool that reaches $14,517,480 for a team already over the cap is the **non-taxpayer
mid-level exception, $15,044,000** — and using it **draws a wall at $209,015,000 that the club may
not cross for the rest of the league year, for any reason** (§2.3, §2.4, cbaguide high, corroborated).
The product says this in one line *before* the commit, in dollars, with the date the wall lifts.

So the pair sits in front of the best basketball decision available to them and discovers that taking
it **turns on a rule against themselves that will refuse a deal they have not thought of yet, in
February, in a lesson they have not played.** That is the break. It is not a blocked button and it is
not a lecture. It is the best move being *expensive in a currency that is not money*.

The room's own evidence closes it thirty minutes later: at REVEAL, two desks holding the same club
appear side by side — one took Flagg and is walled at $209,015,000; one filled the same job with two
camp contracts and is not. Nobody can say which one is better. **That is the replacement intuition,
and the class produces it before the teacher names it.**

### What replaces it

> **"Running a franchise means allocating scarce resources across competing goals under
> uncertainty."** — arrived at in three steps the room walks itself:
>
> - **L1:** *my money is not the only thing I can run out of.* (Slots, tools, term, the line.)
> - **L2:** *what I did in September decides what I am allowed to do in February.*
> - **L3:** *there is no best team. There are different clubs, and I have to defend which one I built.*

### Why this break and not another

Three candidate break-points were considered and rejected. **"You can't afford him"** is FL2 — it
teaches the cap as a wall, and a blocked button is the easiest thing to build and the wrongest thing
to teach. **"You overpaid"** is FL7 dressed as economics and it is a moral verdict a model cannot
earn. **"He got injured"** is a shrug. The break has to be a *rule the student turns on themselves*,
because that is the only version where attribution survives — and it is the most distinctive
economics in the entire dossier (Contract C8: "a constraint the student creates by their own
transaction is a harder and rarer object than a constraint imposed on them").

#### 4.0.1 The two books that cannot be summed

This is the design's answer to "what is the outcome?", and it is the single most important paragraph
in the document, because it is what keeps the module from being a knapsack.

There is **no team quality scalar anywhere in this module.** No OVR, no rating, no franchise value,
no fan trust, no wins. VISUAL_TARGET's three prohibitions and Contract §8 #1 are honoured by
construction: nothing in the model, the payload or any renderer computes such a number, so nothing
can rank on it.

Instead a club walks out of a session holding two things that cannot be added together:

**BOOK ONE — THE SHAPE OF THE ROSTER.** Five named jobs (two guard slots, wing, forward, centre).
Each job is in one of four states:

```
OPEN  <  THIN  <  COVERED  <  ANCHORED
```

`THIN` means only a camp contract holds it. `COVERED` means a rotation contract. `ANCHORED` means a
player the league pays like a cornerstone. **This is a shape, not a number. Five cells. It never
sums, it is never ranked, and every cell traces to one named signing paid for by one named tool.**

**BOOK TWO — WHAT YOU STILL HOLD.** Also not a number: `{ dollars below your nearest line, tools
still unspent (by name), roster slots still free, whether you drew a wall and where }`.

The two books trade against each other on every single decision and **there is no exchange rate**.
A club that anchors four jobs holds nothing in February. A club that holds everything walks into
February with an OPEN job and a mandate it did not meet. Both are legitimate. Neither is scored.

This satisfies REASONING TEST **(a)** — two non-summable objectives, no action weakly best on both
for all seats — and it is why the class reveal can carry conflicting definitions of success without a
winner: the definitions are *already* incommensurable in the model.

#### 4.0.2 THE TWIN DESK — the answer to this territory's named risk

The risk this territory carries is stated plainly: **inherited difficulty decides outcomes and the
student's own reasoning becomes invisible.** If Detroit ends deeper than Denver, the room learns
"Detroit is better," which is FL4 with extra steps and teaches nothing.

The structural answer is one rule, applied at seat assignment:

> **Every club in play is held by at least two desks.** Sixteen desks, eight clubs. Twelve desks, six
> clubs. Assignment is deterministic from join order, exogenous, visible, and never scored.

This changes what the class evidence *is*. The room can now always show both halves of the
attribution question at once:

- **Same books, different plan.** Desk 3 and Desk 11 both run Milwaukee. Both were handed the same
  $21,311,053 ghost. One anchored a job and drew a wall; one stayed clean and is thin at centre.
  **Everything different between them is a decision.**
- **Same plan, different books.** Desk 3 (Milwaukee) and Desk 7 (Denver) both spent every dollar they
  legally could. One got a player; one could only reach the minimum, because Denver is past the
  second apron. **Everything different between them is a situation.**

Both comparisons are on the projector simultaneously, side by side, and the teacher's question is the
same both times: *"What is different between these two, and is it something they chose?"*

The falsifiable form, which the tuning harness must clear:

> **P-TWIN.** For every club in play and every displayed dimension, the **within-club spread across
> the reachable strategy set** must be **≥** the **between-club spread of any single fixed strategy**
> on that dimension. If a club's identity moves an outcome more than a plan does, the module is
> teaching inheritance, and the seat assignment or the board must be retuned until it does not.

This is brute-forced over the reachable action space, not spot-checked, and it is the property I
expect to be hardest to clear. It is stated here so that failing it is visible rather than deniable.

#### 4.1.1 The signature mechanic — THE CAMP BOARD

**What is on the student's screen.**

Three regions, and only three.

1. **THE FRANCHISE BAND** (top, never scrolls). Real club wordmark, typographic, no logo art. Three
   real facts: the market, the building, the money. Then the **LINE METER** — a single horizontal
   axis carrying the floor, the cap, and (once you draw it) your wall, with your committed salary as
   a filled bar against it. At 5–6 two lines are drawn at all times: **floor** and **cap**. The third
   appears only if you create it.
2. **THE BOOKS** (left, the hero object). A three-year grid — 2026-27 / 2027-28 / 2028-29 — with one
   horizontal bar per contract, real names, real dollars, bars spanning the years they cover. Dead
   money is drawn as a **ghost bar**: same width, same money, hatched, and it carries the person's
   name. Milwaukee's board has Damian Lillard's ghost running off the right edge of the grid, because
   it runs to 2030-31 and the grid only shows three years. **That overflow is deliberate and it is
   the most memorable image in the module.**
3. **THE BOARD** (right). Shared by the whole class, shrinking in real time, and it has two halves.
   The top half is **eight named real players** at their real 2026-27 salaries — each can go to
   exactly one desk, and when he goes he is gone. The bottom half is **contracts at administered
   prices**: a rotation player at the room mid-level ($9,366,000), a rotation player at the taxpayer
   mid-level ($6,064,000), and camp contracts at the minimum. Those are **a price, not a person**:
   several clubs sign an exception-level player every summer, so they never run out. What limits
   them is your *toolbox* — you hold each exception once — not the supply. This split is what makes
   contention real where it should be (the named players) and keeps every seat able to reach
   fourteen contracts (the recoverability invariant). Each is a card carrying exactly: **role chip** (tier + job, one label: `ANCHOR · WING`),
   **price**, **one plain-language strength**, **one plain-language risk**. No scalar. No rating. No
   number a pair can sort on except price — and price is not monotone in tier, because the rookie
   scale is a real administered ladder (Cooper Flagg **$14,517,480** is an ANCHOR; Devin Vassell
   **$27,500,000** is ROTATION — §4.5, §4.1 #10).

**What the student manipulates.** Exactly one thing per camp day: they drag a board card onto a job
on their books, and choose **which tool pays for it**. The tool picker shows only tools that are
legal for their club right now, each with its amount and its term, and each with its consequence
sentence attached. Everything is free and reversible until they press **ENTER THE CLAIM**.

**What commits.** `claim = { boardCardId, jobId, tool }`. One per desk per day. A pair may also enter
`PASS`.

**The live foregone panel** — inherited from the best single idea in the discarded module
(`draftDay.ts:318`, frozen at `:556`), and rebuilt against real prices. As the pair hovers a tool, a
panel names, in dollars and in nouns, **what this claim would make unavailable**:

> *If you sign him with the mid-level exception:*
> - *your wall goes up at **$209,015,000**. You are $16.6M under it today.*
> - *you can no longer use the bi-annual exception this year.*
> - *your books hold **$15,044,000** in 2027-28 and **$15,796,200** in 2028-29.*
> - *these three cards go out of reach: [named, with prices].*

At the moment of commitment that list is **frozen** into `foregoneAtLock` — the exact named
alternatives that were alive at the instant they chose — and it is what the COUNTERFACTUAL phase
draws from. This is the D15 frozen-fact discipline and it exists because live re-derivation once
printed a false projector number.

**What the server computes at day close.** Simultaneous, deterministic, no wall clock:

1. Group all claims by board card.
2. **Uncontested** card → the claim applies.
3. **Contested** card → resolved by an in-model ladder, never by arrival time
   (`tradeDeadline.ts:783` / `freeAgency.ts:838` are the defect this exists to avoid):
   1. **Longer contract wins.** Minimum 1 yr < BAE/taxpayer MLE 2 < room MLE 3 < NTMLE 4 < room-with-
      Bird-rights 5. This is real: the rules pay players for term, and re-signing your own player
      allows 5 years / 8% raises against 4 / 5% elsewhere (§8.1 #10). **Your tool is your bargaining
      power** — the single best thing about this resolution rule.
   2. Tie → the club where his job is `OPEN` beats `THIN` beats `COVERED`. (He wants to play.)
   3. Tie → the club with fewer standard contracts.
   4. Tie → lower desk order, disclosed in the rules card before play.
4. Losers: **tool not consumed, money not spent, day spent, player gone forever.** Permanent loss,
   not benching (`draftDay.ts:286-300`).
5. Apply floor/slot/hard-cap arithmetic. **Cap inviolability runs through the same function that
   validates an ordinary purchase** — there is no second code path (PLATFORM_REALITY §6 #17).

**Why it is not a knapsack.** The candidate must name which of REASONING TEST (a)–(e) it satisfies.
This one satisfies four:

- **(a) Non-summable objectives** — the roster shape and what you still hold; §4.0.1.
- **(b) Unprinted value** — role chip, price, one strength, one risk, no scalar (Design Rule 15).
- **(c) Non-separability** — a centre is worth a great deal when your centre job is OPEN and nothing
  at all when it is ANCHORED. What a piece is worth **depends on what you already own**, so greedy
  provably fails and the pair reasons about a portfolio.
- **(d) A second binding constraint** — dollars and the 15 roster spots bind simultaneously, and
  which one binds first depends on the plan. Denver's slots bind; Detroit's floor binds *upward*;
  Memphis's dollars bind.
- **(e) Another desk** — the shared shrinking pool, with contention resolved by term.

#### 4.1.2 The exact economic model

Per-seat state (`y0` = 2026-27, `y1` = 2027-28, `y2` = 2028-29):

```
committed[y]  : dollars committed in league year y
dead[]        : { name, perYear, lastYear }        (included in committed[])
slots         : standard contracts held
tools         : { ntmle, roomMle, taxMle, bae } -> available|used|illegal
hardCap       : 0 | 209_015_000 | 221_686_000
hardCapCause  : null | "the mid-level exception, Camp Day 2"
jobs          : { G1,G2,W,F,C } -> OPEN|THIN|COVERED|ANCHORED
```

Constants — every one below is quoted, with its dossier section:

```
CAP        = 164_961_000   §8.1 #1  official-nba  2026-06-30
FLOOR      = 148_465_000   §8.1 #1  = 0.90 x CAP exactly (§8.1 #2)
TAX        = 200_428_000   §8.1 #1  = 1.215 x CAP (§8.1 #3)
APRON1     = 209_015_000   §8.1 #1
APRON2     = 221_686_000   §8.1 #1
NTMLE      =  15_044_000   §8.1 #7  4 yrs, 5% raises, $64,689,200 total
ROOM_MLE   =   9_366_000   §8.1 #7  3 yrs
TAX_MLE    =   6_064_000   §8.1 #7  2 yrs
BAE        =   5_477_000   §1.2     2 yrs   [SINGLE SOURCE — §7.4 — non-load-bearing]
MIN_ROOKIE =   1_357_763   §1.2
MIN_VET10  =   3_876_529   §1.2
ROSTER_MAX = 15 ; ROSTER_MIN = 14 ; EMPTY_SLOT_FLOOR = 12   §8.1 #8, §2.5
RAISE      = 0.05          §1.2 (NTMLE raise rate)
```

**Tool legality** (real rules, §2.3 / §2.4, transcribed, not approximated):

```
roomTeam         = committed[0] < CAP  AND  the club has used room this summer
room             = max(0, CAP - committed[0])
ntmle  legal iff  tools.ntmle available AND committed[0] < APRON1 AND NOT roomTeam
bae    legal iff  tools.bae   available AND committed[0] < APRON1 AND NOT roomTeam
taxMle legal iff  tools.taxMle available AND committed[0] < APRON2
roomMle legal iff tools.roomMle available AND roomTeam
minimum          always legal
above APRON2  ->  no mid-level exception of any kind is legal            §2.3
```

**Signing legality** for card `p` at salary `s` with tool `t`:

```
legal(p,t) =
     amount(t) >= s                        (for t = room:  room >= s)
 AND slots < ROSTER_MAX
 AND legal_tool(t)
 AND (hardCap == 0  OR  committed[0] + s <= hardCap)
```

**Apply:**

```
committed[0] += s
for k in 1..term(t)-1 :  committed[k] += round(s * (1+RAISE)^k)     (k <= 2 grid)
slots        += 1
tools[t]      = used
if t in {ntmle, bae} : hardCap = APRON1 ; hardCapCause = <tool>, <day>
if t == room         : tools.ntmle = illegal ; tools.bae = illegal ; tools.roomMle = available
jobs[j]       = max(jobs[j], tierState(p))     ANCHOR->ANCHORED, ROTATION->COVERED, CAMP->THIN
```

**End-of-summer settlement:**

```
if slots < EMPTY_SLOT_FLOOR :
    committed[0] += (12 - slots) * MIN_ROOKIE                          §2.5
shortfall = max(0, FLOOR - committed[0])                               §2.1
    -> rendered as: "$X you must pay, and it goes to players across the league.
       It does not buy you a player."
```

**What must be tuned, and against what property.** Only two things in L1 are tuned rather than
sourced, and both are declared:

| Tuned quantity | Property it is tuned against |
|---|---|
| **The board's composition** (how many ANCHOR / ROTATION / CAMP cards, at which prices, covering which jobs) | (i) **≥2 genuinely different affordable options at every forced decision point in the worst reachable case**, for every seat, brute-forced (PLATFORM_REALITY §6 #16). (ii) **No monotone map from money spent to roster shape** (FL1a). (iii) **P-TWIN** (§4.0.2). (iv) **PASS never weakly dominates the best available claim** (Contract §8 #6). |
| **The starting slot counts** (Detroit 11, Brooklyn 12, Memphis 13, Milwaukee 13, Oklahoma City 13, Denver 14) and each club's opening job shape | Every seat must be able to reach 14 standard contracts using only legal actions in its worst reachable state, including Denver above the second apron with minimums only (FL5b, recoverability). |

Everything else is a quoted CBA figure. **No luxury-tax dollar bill is computed anywhere in Module 1**
— the tax appears as a *line* and as a *price structure in words*, never as a bill, because §8.2 #5
forbids rendering one and §7.7 says the bracket math was never reproduced. This is the Economic Truth
critic's dissent #1 and this design does not test it.

### LESSON 2 — **"THE DEADLINE"**
*February 2027. Concepts C4 · C8 · C5 (the seam) · C6 (the topic).*

#### 4.2.1 The signature mechanic — THE COUNTERPARTY

L1 removed the other team so the only thing between a pair and a player was their own books. **L2
puts the other team back**, and the entire lesson is what the other team will and will not do.

**On screen.** THE BOOKS return, five months older, carrying whatever L1 committed. Beside them:
**THE OFFERS DESK** — three real clubs that are *not* seats, each with one named player available and
one stated need, and each with **its own binding constraint printed on its card**:

> **CHICAGO** wants: a wing. Has: Josh Giddey, $25,000,000.
> Chicago is $3.2M over the cap and holds a **$17,991,071 trade exception that expires** (§4.2).
> *Chicago will not take back more salary than it sends.*

The pair builds a proposal by dragging contracts from their own books onto the table and a
counterparty's contract back. Then they press **MAKE THE CALL**.

**What the server computes.** The counterparty either accepts or **refuses with a legible economic
reason**, and the refusals are the teaching surface, not the friction:

- *"Chicago says no. You are sending $12.0M and asking for $25.0M. In this room a trade has to send
  out at least as much as it takes back."*
- *"You cannot make this call. You are above the second apron, and above the second apron you may not
  put two salaries together in one trade."* — §2.3, uncontested across all three researchers.
- *"You cannot make this call. Your wall is at $209,015,000. This deal puts you at $211,400,000. You
  drew that wall on Camp Day 2 when you signed [player] with the mid-level exception."* — **this is
  the L1→L2 carry, and the refusal names the decision.**

**The registered trade rule.** §8.2 #1 forbids rendering *any* salary-matching percentage — 100%,
110% or 125% — because three researchers produced three incompatible accounts and the only
primary-text reading contradicts both cap databases (§7.1, the most dangerous disagreement in the
corpus). This module therefore does not render one. It states its own rule, in its own voice, on the
rules card, before play:

> **"In this room, a trade must send out at least as much salary as it takes back."**

Registered in the simplifications ledger with what changed, why, and the misconception risk: *the real
CBA permits taking back somewhat more than you send, and the exact allowance is currently disputed
across our sources; we chose the strictest reading, which is also the closest to the only
primary-text reading we have (100% + $250,000). A student may conclude NBA trades must balance
exactly. Correct it by saying so.*

The rest of L2 does not need a percentage, because **the real teaching content is what you may no
longer DO** (Economic Truth's dissent #2, agreed with in full):

| Above **$209,015,000** you may not (§2.3) | Above **$221,686,000** you additionally may not (§2.3) |
|---|---|
| acquire a player by sign-and-trade | use any mid-level exception at all, including the taxpayer MLE |
| use the bi-annual exception | **put two or more salaries together in one trade** |
| use the non-taxpayer mid-level exception | send cash |
| use a trade exception generated in a prior year | use a sign-and-trade player for salary matching |

These are rendered as **struck-through items on your own desk** — the honest visual is confiscation,
and the dossier supplies the real precedent (Phoenix 2024-25's four lost tools, §5.4). The
buyout-market restriction is **omitted** because §7.2 records two of three researchers placing it at
the first apron and one at the second, unresolved. The second-apron pick penalty is **omitted**
because §8.2 #2 forbids rendering its count or horizon.

#### 4.2.2 The exact economic model

L2 adds three things to L1's state and changes nothing else:

```
offers[]      : three counterparties, each { club, out:{name,salary,years}, need:jobId, rule }
calls         : 0..3 made
locked[]      : the deals done, frozen
```

Legality of a proposal `{ send: Contract[], receive: Contract }`:

```
sumOut  = sum(send.salary)
sumIn   = receive.salary
after   = committed[0] - sumOut + sumIn

REFUSE unless sumOut >= sumIn                                     module rule, ledgered
REFUSE if   hardCap > 0 AND after > hardCap                       §2.3   -> names the L1 decision
REFUSE if   committed[0] > APRON2 AND send.length >= 2            §2.3   -> "you may not aggregate"
REFUSE if   the counterparty's own line would be crossed          §2.3   -> its own constraint
REFUSE if   slots after the deal < 14                             §8.1 #8
REFUSE if   receive.job is already ANCHORED and send strips a COVERED job   (counterparty objective)
```

The counterparty is not a compliant money pump (Family 3's named exploit). Each has **its own
binding constraint and its own objective**, printed, and it refuses when either is violated. A pair
that tries the greediest offer is told, in one sentence, why the other club will not do it — and that
sentence is the economics.

**Dead money at the open (C6).** The books arrive carrying the ghost bars. Milwaukee's Lillard row is
still there, still $21,311,053, still running past the right edge of the grid. The module states the
only true thing about it: **the money is gone in every branch; the only live question is the roster
spot.** No sunk-cost vocabulary at 5–6 (CEE places sunk cost at grade 12). The 7–8 extension is the
stretch as an intertemporal transfer, with the clean worked example the dossier supplies: **Joakim
Noah, $19.3M becomes $6.4M × 3** (§8.1 #20, 2018-10-13) — division and addition only, and the total
visibly does not shrink.

**The recoverability invariant.** For every reachable seeded opening — including every dead-money
position and the honestly-labelled stock franchise — **at least one legal call satisfies the lesson's
obligation.** Memphis's $3.9M-of-room against $21.9M-of-dead-money is a real position (§4.1 #9) and it
must be playable. Brute-forced over the whole reachable carry space, not spot-checked (FL5b).

### LESSON 3 — **"THE BOARD"**
*July 2027, the signing window, and then you defend it. Concepts C7 · C14 · C11 · C12, with C2 at its
harder object.*

#### 4.3.1 The signature mechanic — RENOUNCE OR HOLD, then DEFEND IT

This is the module finale (D18) and it does two things in one session: it stages the **cleanest
opportunity cost in the entire CBA**, and then it makes the pair argue for what they did.

**Part one — the two roads (PLAY, three signing days).**

Your own free agents are on your books as **placeholders** — the cap hold, §2.5: *"until he re-signs,
signs elsewhere, or you let him go, he still takes up room."* The placeholder is rendered as a
**dollar figure** at 5–6 (the module computes it; the percentage is never printed, per §8.2 #21) and
as **a percentage of his prior salary** at 7–8 (grade 6 has percent; grade 7 has multi-step percent).

Two roads, both legal, neither dominant, exactly as the CBA built them (§2.4, §2.5):

| | **ROAD A — GO UNDER** | **ROAD B — STAY OVER** |
|---|---|---|
| You must | **renounce** your own free agents — permanently destroying the right to keep them at anything above the minimum | keep them |
| You get | room = $164,961,000 − committed, spendable on anyone | Bird rights: up to his max, **5 years, 8% raises** — the longest contract anyone in the room can offer |
| Your tool becomes | the **room** mid-level, **$9,366,000** | the **non-taxpayer** mid-level, **$15,044,000** |
| The catch | your own player is gone forever, and your biggest tool shrank by $5,678,000 | you can never dip into room this summer, and using the NTMLE **draws the wall again** |

Renouncing is **irreversible by construction**, its irreversibility is stated before commitment, and
the loss appears in the next session's opening state — except there is no next session, so it appears
in the room's own reveal. FL5's requirement is met and FL5(b)'s counter-clause holds: irreversible is
not unrecoverable, and every reachable opening retains a legal path to the lesson's obligation.

The signing days run L1's contention ladder unchanged, with one addition that matters: **Road B's
Bird rights buy five years, which beats every other tool in the room.** A club that stayed over the
cap loses every price war and wins every term war. That is real (§8.1 #10) and it is the sharpest
"there is no dominant strategy" object in the module.

**Part two — THE BOARD (ARGUE).**

The pair is given three minutes and a structured card. Not an essay box — a **defence built from
their own computed state**, with the numbers pre-filled from the model and the reasoning theirs:

- 5–6, **two moves**: *"We chose ______ because ______, and the class evidence is ______."* The third
  blank is a picker over the room's actual REVEAL findings, so the evidence is real and shared.
  (W.5.1 — opinion.)
- 7–8, **three moves**: the same, plus *"The strongest case against us is ______, and our evidence
  still beats it because ______."* (W.6.1 — argument; W.7.1a acknowledge; W.8.1a distinguish.
  C3 D3.4.6-8 counterclaims.)

Two desks' defences go on the projector at the teacher's press, chosen by `/teach` from a list the
module ranks by **how far apart they are**, never by quality.

#### 4.3.2 The exact economic model

```
holds[]   : { name, priorSalary, holdAmount, rights: BIRD|EARLY|NON }
road      : null | "UNDER" | "OVER"      (irreversible once set)
room      = max(0, CAP - committed[0] - sum(active holds))
```

Cap-hold amounts are the CBA's, computed by the module and rendered in dollars (§2.5, CBA, high):
150% of prior salary (Bird, at or above the estimated average), 190% (Bird, below it), 250%/300%
(finishing a rookie-scale contract), 130% (Early Bird), 120% (Non-Bird) — never above his max, never
below his min.

```
RENOUNCE(h):  holds -= h ; rights[h] = destroyed forever ; irreversible
ROAD("UNDER"): requires committed[0] + sum(holds) < CAP after renouncing
               tools.ntmle = illegal ; tools.bae = illegal ; tools.roomMle = available
ROAD("OVER"):  room = 0 ; tools.ntmle = available ; Bird re-signing legal at 5 yrs / 8%
```

**C11 — risk, ordinally only.** Each free-agent card on the market carries one of three words —
`STEADY` / `SOLID` / `SWING` — and a **bar of ten chips** (Design Rule 7). No probability is printed
at 5–6, because grades 5 and 6 have no probability standard at all (§6.2). At 7–8 the chips are
labelled numerically (7.SP.C.5) and the class's pooled results build a long-run frequency
(7.SP.C.6). **No expected value anywhere, in either band** — EV is grade 8 in CEE and it belongs to
M4 (D2, CLAUDE.md §7). The naming collision between M1's world and M4 "Draft Day" makes that an easy
and invisible trespass, and this design declines it explicitly.

Outcomes resolve **visibly, across the room, from the same declared word**, so variance is seen as a
spread of desks rather than asserted by a card. Every outcome traces to an announced cause at
debrief. There is no unexplained variance anywhere.

**C12 — decision quality vs outcome.** The model permits, and the reveal must contain an actual
instance of, both: good reasoning + bad outcome, and weak reasoning + good outcome. **No score,
award, bonus, or reveal phrasing anywhere rewards matching a historical move** — grep-assertable, and
the forbidden-vocabulary harness (`clientClaims.test.ts`) is extended with the FL7 list (*greedy,
reckless, brave, smart, disciplined, cheap, worth every dollar, value signing, trap*).

#### 4.3.5 The four spectacle beats

| Beat | What it is |
|---|---|
| **SIGNATURE DECISION** | **THE ROAD.** One irreversible press. Renounce your own people and get room, or keep them and keep the bigger tool and the longer contract. The screen names, by person, exactly who you are giving up the right to keep, and says the word *forever* before you press. |
| **CONSEQUENCE** | **THE PRICE OF EVERYONE HAVING MONEY.** The room's own signing prices, plotted against the room's own count of clubs that took Road A. When more desks go under the cap, more desks have room at the same time, and the contested cards go to longer terms and bigger tools. Then the 2016 reveal: the cap jumped ~34%, everyone had money at once, and the names of the contracts that followed. **Auction first, 2016 second** (§5.9). |
| **CLASS REVEAL** | **THE FIVE WALLS, TWINNED, WITH THE ARGUMENT ATTACHED.** Same structure as L1 and L2 so the room can read it in three seconds — but this time each desk's shape carries the sentence *they wrote* about it. |
| **INTELLECTUAL REVEAL** | **THE SAME CALL, TWICE.** Two real cases, presented on the information available at the time, in this order: Milwaukee trades for Jrue Holiday (2020-11-17) → title; Milwaukee trades for Damian Lillard (2023) → $21,311,053 a year through 2031. **Same franchise. Same category of bet. Twice.** Then, only then, the room's own two desks that made the same call and got different results. The teacher's line is *"here is what they did and what happened"* — never *"correct."* |

#### 4.3.6 TIME CUT

**Round unit: the signing day.** `noun: "signing day"`. Three.

`fallbackPolicy`: *"A signing day that closes with no offer keeps your money, your placeholders and
your rights exactly as they are. Nothing is renounced by the clock — that press belongs to the desk."*

**The road is never taken by a fallback.** An irreversible, rights-destroying action may not be
applied by a bell. That is a hard rule of this design and it is the single most important thing the
round contract does here.

- `fallback`: *"Desk 9 · Brooklyn — no offer entered. Keeps $18,400,000 of room and both placeholders.
  The two guards it was watching go to the desks that offered four and five years."*
- `selfFallback`: *"No offer from you today. You keep your $18,400,000 and both of your own players'
  placeholders — and the two guards you were watching go to whoever offered them more years."*

#### 4.3.8 `/teach` director

**HOOK** — **NOW:** the empty chair, then the two roads. **ASK:** *"Which road would you take if I told you nothing else?"* Hands up both ways, count them on the board. **DON'T EXPLAIN YET:** do not say which is better. Neither is.

**PLAY** — **NOW:** walk to any desk hovering the renounce control. **ASK:** *"Say his name out loud, and say what you get for letting him go."* **WATCH FOR:** desks that renounce fast and desks that will not renounce at all. You want one of each at ARGUE.

**REVEAL** — **ASK** *(after SAME WORD, DIFFERENT DAY):* *"Everyone here picked the same kind of player. Look at what happened. Was the choice wrong, or did it just not work?"* That is the module's hardest question and this is the only place it can be asked, because it needs the room's own spread behind it.

**CONSEQUENCE** — **NOW:** 2016. **ASK, before the smoothing rule:** *"If you ran the league and you had to stop this happening again, what rule would you write?"* Take three. Then show them the real one. **DON'T EXPLAIN YET:** do not tell them the cap can never fall until they have proposed something.

**ARGUE** — **NOW:** two defences, side by side, chosen for distance not quality. **ASK** *(5–6):* *"Which one of these convinced you, and which sentence did it?"* **ASK** *(7–8):* *"Take the desk you disagree with. What is the strongest version of their case?"* **DON'T EXPLAIN YET:** *(5–6)* you must converge before the bell — one nameable mechanism, one right-ish reading. *(7–8)* you may end in disagreement, but it must be adjudicated by evidence on the wall. Never "everyone has their opinion."

**SYNTHESIS** — name **opportunity cost** again *(it was named in L1; this is where it becomes subjective — two desks, same menu, different costs, both right)*, and **option value** *(7–8 also: **intertemporal choice**)*.

## 5. THE CARRY

### 5.1 What moves, and in what shape

One bounded flat record per desk. No log, no transcript, no history array. Target **under 1KB per
desk serialised**, because every session read deep-clones the whole state through JSON
(`snapshotRepository.ts:45`) and every write re-serialises the entire store (`:222`).

```ts
type FranchiseCarry = {
  v: 1;
  band: "5-6" | "7-8";          // the band lives IN the carry (see §6.4)
  club: ClubId;                  // franchise identity — real club, real market, real building
  books: [number, number, number];        // committed salary, 3 league years
  dead: { name: string; perYear: number; lastYear: number }[];
  jobs: Record<JobId, "OPEN"|"THIN"|"COVERED"|"ANCHORED">;   // 5 entries
  tools: {
    ntmle: "available"|"used"|"illegal";
    roomMle: "available"|"used"|"illegal";
    taxMle: "available"|"used"|"illegal";
    bae: "available"|"used"|"illegal";
    hardCapLine: 0 | 209015000 | 221686000;
    hardCapCause: string | null;          // "the mid-level exception, Camp Day 2"
  };
  slots: { standard: number; twoWay: number };
  lost: { what: string; because: string; when: "L1"|"L2" }[];   // absences WITH reasons
  stock: boolean;                // honestly labelled, never disguised as carried
};
```

Seven fields, and each one is here because it changes a **reachable option set**, not a display:

1. **`books`, by year** — path dependence is about *which years are spoken for*, not a single total.
   Three years, because every real device in the module (stretch, dead money, rookie-scale team
   options in years 3 and 4, contract raises) operates over exactly that horizon.
2. **`dead`, by name and year** — a number without a name is a fee; **the name is what makes it sunk
   cost.**
3. **`jobs`** — determines what a counterparty in L2 will want from you and what an OPEN slot is worth
   in L3's contention ladder.
4. **`tools` + `hardCapLine` + `hardCapCause`** — one flag and one number, and it is the entire
   difference between later options existing and not existing. The cheapest and richest carry
   available.
5. **`slots`** — the second binding constraint. Without it C1's 7–8 object is unreachable later.
6. **`lost`** — irreversible losses as absences *with reasons*, so the carried state can say
   *"you do not have X, and here is the decision that is why."*
7. **`club` + `band` + `stock`** — identity and provenance.

### 5.2 The falsifiable property

> **P1.** In **L1**, a pair signs a board card using the **non-taxpayer mid-level exception
> ($15,044,000, 4 years, 5% raises — §8.1 #7, official-nba)**. In **L3**, the action
> **A = "renounce and sign the $18,000,000 free agent with cap room"** is available to some desks and
> not others.
>
> Take two L1 histories for the same club with the same L2 play:
> - **H1** — used the NTMLE on Camp Day 2. Books carry $15,044,000 in 2027-28 and **$15,796,200** in
>   2028-29.
> - **H2** — filled the identical job with two camp contracts at $1,357,763, one year each. Books
>   carry nothing forward.
>
> Under **H1**, A is **REFUSED**, and the refusal names the decision:
> *"You cannot get under $164,961,000. Your books hold $15,796,200 for [player] through 2029-30 —
> you signed him with the mid-level exception on Camp Day 2."*
> Under **H2**, A is **APPLIED**.
>
> Asserted by replaying the reducer over both branches. **If no such (A, H1, H2) triple exists,
> L1→L3 persistence is narration.**

The shorter-range twin, inside one league year, is the one the room actually *feels*:

> **P1b.** In **L2**, the trade `send $12.0M / receive $18.4M` is **REFUSED** under H1
> (*"your wall is at $209,015,000 — you drew it on Camp Day 2"*) and **APPLIED** under H2. The hard
> cap really does expire with the league year (§2.3), so this property is confined to L1→L2 and P1
> carries the L1→L3 load through contract years, which is where the real persistence lives.

Three companion properties, all required:

- **P2 — no sludge.** For every field in the carry there is at least one reachable L3 state that
  differs on the basis of **that field alone**. A field with no reachable consequence is deleted, not
  documented.
- **P3 — recoverability across the carry.** For every reachable seeded L2 and L3 opening — **including
  every reachable dead-money position and the stock franchise** — at least one legal action satisfies
  that lesson's obligation. Brute-forced over the whole reachable carry space.
- **P4 — attribution, per band.** At 5–6 the causal sentence at the next lesson's opening is
  **computed from the carried field**, never authored per scenario: `"You have $${room} today because
  you signed ${name} with ${tool} on ${day}."` At 7–8 there is no sentence — but every fact needed to
  diagnose it is on the 7–8 surface, or "silent" has become "hidden."

### 5.3 What deliberately does NOT carry, and why

| Not carried | Why |
|---|---|
| **Every historical action** | The runtime already owns the class log (`classEvents` + the WHILE-YOU-WERE-AWAY fold-in, `sessionService.ts:1126-1152`). A seed that is a transcript is a transcript, and it blows the clone budget. |
| **Per-round intermediates** — previews, drafts, reveal beats, UI state | Not state, and the receiving lesson has its own. |
| **Anything derivable from the books** — totals, room, distance-to-a-line, "flexibility" | D15's frozen-fact discipline. Carry the facts, freeze the claims **within** a lesson, never carry a claim **across** lessons. A carried claim has no reducer behind it in the receiving lesson and will eventually contradict that lesson's own arithmetic. This is exactly the class of defect that put three false sentences on a projector through four verification rounds. |
| **Any other desk's data, in any form** | `studentView` must never leak another seat, and a carried blob is the easiest place for that to happen unnoticed. |
| **Outcome labels, strategy classifications, awards, any evaluation of the last lesson** | `classifyStrategy` is on the discard list for a reason, and a carried judgment is a progression system (D4). |
| **The roster's individual players by name beyond the dead-money rows** | Fails the practical test: the module cannot honestly stand in for a named 15-man roster in a stock franchise. `jobs` can be stocked honestly; a roster cannot. |
| **The counterfactual panels** | Frozen within a lesson, discarded at its end. |

**The rule that settles arguments:** *if the module cannot ship an honestly-labelled stock value for a
field, that field is not carryable* — because an absent pair gets a stock franchise, not a broken one
(CLAUDE.md §9), and a stock franchise must never be disguised as a carried one (D18).

**And one thing the chain must not repeat.** The discarded module silently deleted the franchise of
any team that was shocked and did not repair (`tradeDeadline.ts:200`, M11) — destroying the identity
of the pair most affected by the lesson's dramatic beat. **A carry that can blank a pair's franchise
identity is worse than no carry.** `club` is validated first, defended per team, and a malformed
entry produces a labelled stock franchise of the same club, never a missing one.

## 6. THE TWO BANDS

D22 program #2: **one M1 architecture** — shared economic world, shared persistent franchise logic,
shared consequences — plus grade-specific depth and scaffolding. Not M1 for 5–6 followed by a fork.

### 6.1 What is SHARED — identical, byte for byte

1. **The world.** Same league, same six clubs, same 2026-27 thresholds, same board, same dates, same
   cases. Two rooms looking at two leagues is two products.
2. **The engine.** **One reducer.** Given the same actions from the same state, the two bands resolve
   **identically.** If a 5–6 model and a 7–8 model produced different consequences for the same
   choice, the bands would have learned two different economics and D22's shared-consequence
   requirement would be false in the only place it matters.
3. **The consequences.** Every threshold, every tool legality rule, every contention tie-break, every
   floor and slot charge.
4. **The runtime.** Same module id, same phase list, same round contract, same seed shape, same
   `/teach` machinery.
5. **The franchise state.** Same `FranchiseCarry`, same fields, same arithmetic.
6. **Which concepts are true.** Opportunity cost is opportunity cost. The cap is soft in both rooms.
   Dead money persists in both rooms. **Every false lesson in §4 of the contract is false in both
   rooms and every guard binds in both rooms. No band is exempted from a false-lesson guard.**
7. **Decision quality ≠ outcome**, both bands, always. What differs is *who does the separating.*
8. **Irreversibility and recoverability**, both bands, both properties.
9. **Attribution.** Both bands must be able to name the choice that produced today's state.
10. **The real numbers.** Real cap figures stay real in both rooms. What changes is **the arithmetic
    the student must perform.** A 5–6 room that sees a rounded fake cap has been given a different
    world, not a simpler one.

### 6.2 What the grade profile selects, per lesson

| | **Grades 5–6** | **Grades 7–8** |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 · lines carried** | Two drawn at all times: **floor** and **cap**. The cap is **crossable with a tool** — not a blocked button. A third line appears **only if the pair draws it**. Two different kinds of object, and neither of the two standing lines merely blocks (FL2b). | Four: floor, cap, tax, first apron — plus the second apron for Denver and OKC, who sit on it. The tax is introduced as a **price**, structurally, with no bill. |
| **L1 · board width** | 3–5 legal, non-dominated options visible per decision; the rest greyed with a plain reason. No compound moves. | 6–10 options, plus **≥1 compound move**: *waive-and-stretch, then sign with the room it freed* — where the legality of step 2 depends on step 1. Plus ≥1 dominated-looking option that is correct under a constraint. |
| **L1 · variables per card** | **2**: price, and one role chip that carries tier+job as a single label. | **3–4**, as a scannable table: price, tier, job, contract years — plus one deliberately **non-causal** column (the club he last played for) so the debrief can expose it. |
| **L1 · board resolution** | **Open and sequential**, with visible shrinking supply — you see the card go. | **Hidden and simultaneous** — claims resolve at day close, so the pair must hold a belief about what rivals will do. *(Design Rule 12 rates itself **LOW confidence and unsourced** and asks for a playtest of both variants at 5–6. This design treats the split as a hypothesis to test, not a rule to assume, and the Stage-0 prototype runs the open variant so the sequential case is the one under attack first.)* |
| **L2 · trades** | One counterparty at a time, its constraint printed above the table, refusal in one sentence. | Three counterparties live, their constraints inferable rather than printed, and refusal reasons that require reading two cards together. |
| **L2 · dead money** | The line item exists, it names a person, the money is gone either way, the live question is the roster spot. **No sunk-cost vocabulary** — CEE places it at grade 12. | The **stretch as an intertemporal transfer**: same total, different years, with a ceiling on how much may be pushed forward. | **L3 · cap holds** | Rendered as **dollars only**. The module computes the hold; the percentage never appears. | Rendered as **the percentage and the dollars**, and the pair computes one of them. |
| **L3 · risk** | **Ordinal only** — STEADY / SOLID / SWING and a bar of ten chips. Evidence is the room's own spread. | **Numeric** (7.SP.C.5) and long-run frequency built from the class's pooled results (7.SP.C.6). **No EV in either band** — it is grade 8 in CEE and it belongs to M4. |
| **Counterfactual** | The product **runs and displays** it: *"here is your club if you had kept the pick."* The pair notices and reacts. (Rafetseder & Perner: mature counterfactual reasoning is not reliable in all children before ~age 12.) | The product **withholds** it. The pair constructs *"what would have had to be true for the other choice to be right?"* and it is revealed only after they commit an answer. |
| **Cross-lesson state** | Carried, and the causal link **surfaced in plain language, computed from the carried field**, at the next lesson's opening. | Carried **silently**; the pair diagnoses why the room is tight. Plus a **commitment device**: the four-year mid-level deal is the biggest tool available *and* the one that locks the wall — the biggest offer removes the escape hatch. |
| **Argument** | Two moves. | Three moves, including the strongest case against themselves. **The word itself changes at exactly this boundary** — W.5.1 "opinion," W.6.1 "argument." |
| **Debrief scope** | *"What did **your** choice cost **your** club?"* — generalization is personal. | *"Who else in the league was made better or worse off, and would the same choice be right for a different club?"* — generalization is societal (C3 D2.Eco.1.6-8). |
| **Debrief resolution** | Must **converge** on one nameable mechanism and one right-ish reading. **No "both teams were right" endings.** | May end in a **defensible disagreement**, adjudicated by evidence on the wall — never at "everyone has their opinion," which is the multiplist failure mode this exact age is most prone to. |

### 6.3 The scaffolded exploration phase for grades 5–6 — concretely

This is not a UI setting and it is the highest-stakes rule in the pedagogy set. Sinha & Kapur:
problem-solving-before-instruction is **g = +0.50 at grades 6–10** and **g = −0.09 at grades 2–5**,
and the moderator splits at exactly this product's band boundary. Mazziotti et al. found no
Productive Failure advantage in 228 German fifth graders. The 5–6 band **contains grade 5**, so its
discovery phase is scaffolded and the 7–8 band faces the constraint cold. **Both bands keep the
consolidation. Only the front half is released.**

Five concrete pieces, all in-product except the fourth:

1. **CAMP DAY 1 IS RUN WITH THE ROOM.** The product ships a `GUIDED DAY 1` state that the teacher
   triggers. One desk's books go on the projector mirror; the director script walks the four tools
   out loud — amount, term, consequence; the class enters Day 1 together. **Day 2 must be different
   in kind, not the same card with new numbers** (Family 4's "wait for the teacher" exploit): Day 1's
   decision is *which job to fill*; Day 2's is *what to pay with*, which is a different question and
   the one the lesson turns on.
2. **THE CONSTRAINT METER IS ALWAYS VISIBLE AND NEVER NEGATIVE.** Floor and cap drawn at all times,
   the bar filled to your committed salary. **Over a line is stated in words, never as a minus sign**
   (Design Rule 6; §8.2 #21 — grade 5 has no negative-number standard): *"$12.4M past the tax line,"*
   never *"−$12.4M."*
3. **AN IN-PRODUCT PROMPT NAMES THE TRADE-OFF BEING FACED**, computed from the pair's own state and
   never authored per scenario: *"Paying for him with the mid-level means you cannot use the
   bi-annual on the guard you looked at."* This is the foregone panel, phrased as a sentence, and it
   is the single scaffold the evidence most supports.
4. **A MANDATORY TEACHER-LED CONTRAST AT CONSOLIDATION.** THE TWINS beat is not optional at 5–6. The
   director marks it as required and `/teach` refuses to let the teacher skip it without the
   consequence-stating confirm the runtime already requires for irreversible controls.
5. **≤2 DECISION-RELEVANT VARIABLES ON SCREEN AT ONCE**, ever. Sixth graders cannot reliably
   coordinate three simultaneous causal variables even with the causal structure displayed (Kuhn,
   Pease & Wirkala: only ~1/3 do, and a 4th/5th-grade inquiry intervention did not move it). The
   contract's anti-pairing follows: **C3 + C11 + C12 together at 5–6 is forbidden**, which is why
   C11 and C12 live in L3 and C3 lives in L1.

One honest caveat the director must give the teacher, once, at the end of L1: *exploration-first costs
immediate performance and buys transfer.* A flat quiz tomorrow is not evidence of failure.

### 6.4 The cross-band seed — refused, not silently accepted

PLATFORM_REALITY §3.3 names the hole: the seed envelope is exactly `{lessonModuleId, state}`, so a
7–8 room seeded from a 5–6 room's L1 sees a well-formed seed from the right module id, accepts it,
and carries 5–6 franchise depth into a 7–8 lesson **with nothing in the runtime able to notice.**

The economic requirement, not merely the engineering one: **a cross-band carry must be refused or
honestly relabelled as stock.** The two bands do not present the same decision set — 5–6 never had a
compound move and never faced a hidden simultaneous claim. A 7–8 L2 opening seeded from a 5–6 L1
therefore attributes to the pair a constraint they were never given the chance to create. That is an
attribution failure, and attribution is the one thing this module cannot lose.

**This design solves it without a runtime change: the band is a field inside the carried state.**
The receiving module reads `state.band`, compares it to its own session band, and on a mismatch
refuses the carry and boots an honestly-labelled stock franchise of the same club, with the reason
on `/teach`:

> *"Desk 9 · Milwaukee — stock books. The linked session was a grades 5–6 room and this is a 7–8
> room; a 5–6 summer never included the compound move this lesson expects, so carrying it forward
> would blame this pair for a decision they were never offered."*

I still recommend amending D38 to add `gradeBand` (and `sourceSessionId` / `sourceEnded`, so D39's
live-source warning can also be a module-side guard) — but the module must not *depend* on that
amendment landing first, and this one does not.

## 7. THE CLASS REVEAL

### The shape: **THE FIVE WALLS**

Five orderings of the room, on the projector simultaneously, in five columns of equal weight and
equal type size. **No column is labelled better. No column is numbered 1st. There is no sixth column
that combines them, and no such combination exists anywhere in the model.**

| Wall | What it orders by | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **DEEPEST** | how many jobs you **raised**, and the shape you got | `jobs` against the club's opening `jobs` |
| **STILL HOLDING** | tools you did not spend, by name, and whether you drew a wall | `tools`, `hardCapLine` |
| **SPENT THIS SUMMER** | what you **added** to this year's payroll | `books[0] − opening books[0]` |
| **PROMISED LATER** | what you **added** to 2027-28 and 2028-29 | `Δ(books[1] + books[2])` |
| **THE TWINS** | pairs of desks holding the same club, side by side | seat assignment |

**Every ordering is decision-relative, and that is not a stylistic choice.** An absolute
ordering — *most dollars under the line*, *most owed* — ranks what a desk **inherited**, which is
exactly FL4 and exactly this territory's named risk. The Stage-0 prototype's first cut used
absolutes and P-TWIN failed on two of three dimensions: the club moved the outcome more than the
plan did. Switching the class-facing orderings to deltas is the repair, and it is recorded here
rather than quietly fixed. Absolutes still appear where an absolute is the point — on the pair's
own private summary, where *"$8.4M under the tax line"* is a fact about their own February.

Nobody appears at the top of all five. **This is provable, not hoped for:** DEEPEST and STILL HOLDING
are structurally opposed (every dollar committed to depth is a dollar not held), and MOST OWED is
mechanically produced by the same action that produces DEEPEST. The tuning harness asserts it:

> **P-NO-SWEEP.** No reachable end state is top-ranked on both DEEPEST and STILL HOLDING for any
> seat. Brute-forced over the reachable action space.

Which is the same thing as saying: **the argument is guaranteed by the model, not by the teacher's
skill.**

### Why this is not a leaderboard

- Five simultaneous orderings that disagree is not a ranking; it is a disagreement. D4's prohibition
  is on a progression register and a single ranked class leaderboard on one universal franchise
  score. There is no such score here — there is no scalar to build one from.
- **Franchise names, never student names**, on any shared screen.
- No column is ever described with an evaluative word. The vocabulary is *deeper · freer · thinner ·
  tighter · more owed*, and the forbidden-vocabulary harness is extended to enforce it.
- **No trophy, no champion, no confetti, no "best."**

### The three things the reveal is required to contain

Not hoped for — asserted, because the contract requires the class evidence to hold an actual instance
from the room's own play and not a slide:

1. **FL1(b)** — at least one desk that spent less and is deeper than a desk that spent more. Reachable
   by construction: the cheap ANCHORs are rookie-scale contracts and the expensive ROTATION contracts
   are not, so a desk that spends $14,517,480 on Flagg out-anchors a desk that spends $27,500,000 on
   Vassell. If a given room does not produce one, the projector says so honestly and the teacher's
   card carries the dossier's own instance: **New Orleans, ninth-highest payroll in the league at
   $212,290,592, not a contender** (§4.1 #12).
2. **FL4(c)** — at least one small-market path winning on a displayed dimension. Oklahoma City,
   Memphis and Milwaukee are all in play, and the real instance backs it: **OKC won 2025 from the
   smallest market and holds 13 future firsts** (§4.1 #2, §5.8).
3. **C12** — at least one good-decision/bad-outcome and one weak-decision/good-outcome from the
   room's own play, in L3. The model permits both by construction (contested cards resolve on term,
   and a pair can be beaten by a rival's tool rather than by their own reasoning).

### The teacher's question, which is the point of the whole beat

> *"Two desks are running the same club. One is deeper. One is freer. Which one do you want to be in
> February — and say what makes you think so."*

At 5–6 this converges: the teacher names the mechanism and the class agrees on a right-ish reading.
At 7–8 it may not converge, and that is allowed — provided it is adjudicated by the evidence on the
wall.

## 8. THE FINAL DEFENSE

L3's `ARGUE` phase is the boardroom test the founder asked for. It is not a writing exercise bolted
onto a simulation: **the pair defends a strategy using numbers the model computed from their own
play**, and the alternatives they are compared against are real desks in the room, not straw men.

### The shared structure

The defence card pre-fills every quantity from the pair's own state, so nothing they claim can outrun
the model. The blanks are reasoning; the numbers are computed and cited to the beat that produced
them. Then two desks go on the wall, side by side, chosen by `/teach` from a module-ranked list
ordered by **distance between the two positions** — never by quality, and the ranking function is
grep-assertable as containing no evaluative term.

### Grades 5–6 — two moves

```
WE CHOSE  [ the road / the signing, filled from state ]
BECAUSE   [ free text, ≤ 25 words ]
AND THE CLASS EVIDENCE IS  [ picker over the room's actual REVEAL findings ]
```

The evidence picker is the load-bearing part. It offers only findings the aggregate actually
produced this session — *"three desks that went under the cap all lost a contested player to a
longer contract"* — so a fifth grader's third move is real class evidence and not a remembered
slogan. (W.5.1: opinion pieces supporting a point of view with reasons.)

Converge before the bell: the teacher's closing move is to name **one** mechanism the room agrees
produced the pattern. **No "both teams were right" endings.**

### Grades 7–8 — three moves

```
WE CHOSE  [ from state ]
BECAUSE   [ ≤ 40 words ]
THE CLASS EVIDENCE IS  [ picker ]
THE STRONGEST CASE AGAINST US IS  [ picker over the OTHER twin's actual position ]
AND OUR EVIDENCE STILL BEATS IT BECAUSE  [ ≤ 40 words ]
```

The fourth blank is picked from **their twin's real state**, so the counterclaim is a real position a
real desk actually holds, not a hypothetical. That is C3 D3.4.6-8 (claims *and* counterclaims,
strengths and limitations of both) and D4.4.6-8 (critique for credibility), instantiated rather than
asserted.

Two structural guards, both grep-assertable:

- **No score anywhere rewards matching the historical move** (FL3a). There is no matching score, no
  award, no bonus and no reveal phrasing that could be one.
- **Every commit-then-reveal beat presents the decision-maker's information set as it stood at the
  date**, and ships **a paired counter-case where a near-identical decision ended differently**
  (FL3c). The dossier supplies the pair ready-made: **Lakers/Davis 2019 and Clippers/George 2019 —
  same summer, similar price, opposite result** (§5.7). And it supplies the contrarian-heuristic
  breaker (Family 5): **Milwaukee/Holiday 2020 → 2021 title**, a consensus move that was genuinely
  right and indistinguishable ex ante from the ones that failed. Without a right-consensus case the
  module teaches contrarianism.

Adults fail at separating decision quality from outcome (Baron & Hershey). Neither band is relied on
to make the distinction unprompted: at 5–6 **the product does the separating** by showing the
distribution of outcomes for the same decision and the teacher says the sentence; at 7–8 the students
sort the cases and defend the sort.

## 10. THE VISUAL WORLD

Against `VISUAL_TARGET.md`, and against the platform's real ceiling: **only `.html .js .css .json
.svg .woff2` are servable** (`http.ts:87-96`) — a `.png` request 404s. No canvas, no
`requestAnimationFrame`, no WebGL, no animation library. Everything below is SVG built from
arithmetic, CSS, and typography, which is the technique the platform has already proven
(`shared/arena.ts`, 1019 lines of procedural SVG; `shared/m2ui.ts`'s HTML-string component builders).

### The place

**The cap room, at night, three weeks before camp.** Near-black ground, one accent, no photography —
the mockups' cinematic imagery is unavailable and is replaced by **type at scale**. The club is a
**typographic wordmark**: the real club's name, set enormous, letterspaced, in the module's own face.
No logo, no mark, no arena image, no player photograph (§8.2 #22, and CLAUDE.md §3's rights
discipline). This is not a compromise — a wordmark at 96pt on black reads as a franchise and reads
from the back row, and it is unambiguously BOW's visual system rather than the NBA's.

### The composition, per surface

| Surface | Composition | The one hero object |
|---|---|---|
| **`/play`** | Three regions, fixed: the **franchise band** (never scrolls, carries identity + the line meter), **THE BOOKS** (left, dominant, ~55% of the width), **THE BOARD** (right). Designed to 1366×768, first contact 1024×600. Quiet: information density belongs to the teacher surface, not this one. | **THE BOOKS** — a three-year cap sheet drawn as horizontal bars, real names, real dollars, with the lines drawn *through* it as vertical rules. That overflow is the image the module is remembered by. |

Nothing lands on it while a decision is open. |
| **`/teach`** | The mockup's density, delivered through machinery that already exists and is shape-driven: **THE ROOM** (`view.room`), **THE DESKS** (`view.deskStrip`), the director panel (`view.director` / `watchFor` / `revealStages` / `bellNote`), the projector mirror, the class clock, TIME CUT. | **THE TWINS PANEL** — the mockup's "INTERESTING CONTRASTS" done right: two desks holding the same club, one axis, side by side, with the walk-to flag on whichever needs the teacher. It is a §7 affordance and a director tool in one object. |

### What earns motion

Four things. Everything else is static, and every one collapses under `prefers-reduced-motion`
(in-SVG, the technique `arena.ts` already uses):

1. **The wall being drawn.** When a pair commits a tool that hard-caps them, a vertical rule sweeps
   across THE BOOKS to $209,015,000 over ~600ms and then stays there for two more lessons. It is the
   only irreversible animation in the module and it marks the only irreversible action in L1.
2. **A card leaving the board.** When another desk takes a card, it strikes through and dims in place
   — it does not vanish. Permanent loss should be visible as an absence with a shape, not a gap.
3. **The books redrawing at day close.** Bars grow to their new lengths from their old ones, so the
   pair sees *what changed*, not *what is*.
4. **The projector's beat transition.** One object out, one object in, no crossfade of numbers.

### What the model must hold for a drawing to be allowed to exist (D25)

Any drawing whose **size** carries a quantity is measured in a real browser against the model, with a
no-content baseline subtracted, a stated tolerance, and **an instrument that is poisoned before any
pass is believed.** In this module that binds on exactly three drawings: the contract bars in THE
BOOKS (length = dollars), the line meter's fill (length = committed salary), and the ladder on
`/board` (position = dollars). The five-cell job strip encodes an ordinal, not a magnitude, and is
drawn as four discrete states with four distinct shapes — **colour is never the only carrier**.

Two things the mockups show that this design refuses to draw, because the model does not hold them:
a **per-desk sparkline** (there is no per-desk time series in a three-round lesson worth drawing) and
a **10-year franchise outlook** (nothing in a 55-minute lesson can honestly compute one).

## 12. HOW THIS DIES

### Death 1 — *"The inheritance decides the outcome, and the student's reasoning is invisible."*

This is the territory's own named risk and it is the strongest argument against the design. If
Detroit ends deeper than Denver every time, the room learns "Detroit is better," a fifth grader
concludes that front offices are luck, and the module has taught FL4 (*small markets lose because
they are run badly*) with the sign flipped.

**My answer, in three parts, two of which are falsifiable.**

1. **The twin desk** (§4.0.2). Every club is held by two desks, so the room always sees the same books
   with two different plans. That is not a mitigation; it is a change to what the class evidence *is*.
2. **P-TWIN**, asserted by the harness: for every club and every displayed dimension, the within-club
   spread across the reachable strategy set must be ≥ the between-club spread of any single fixed
   strategy. **If a club's identity moves an outcome more than a plan does, the module fails its own
   test and the board is retuned until it does not.** This is the property I expect to be hardest to
   clear, and I have stated it in a form that makes failing it visible rather than deniable.
3. **No cross-club ranking exists.** The five walls are five orderings and one of them is explicitly
   the twins. Each club also carries its own owner's mandate (§4.0), which is not comparable across
   clubs — so there is no axis on which "Detroit beat Denver" is even a sentence the product can say.

**Where the answer is weakest:** P-TWIN is a property I have asserted, not measured. If the board
turns out not to admit a wide enough within-club strategy space at the tightest seats — Denver, above
the second apron with minimums only, is the obvious risk — then Denver is a *spectator seat with a
story*, which is a real failure. The fallback is to drop Denver to an alternate (Sacramento, the
mid-market fork with no comfortable answer, §4.1 #11) and keep Denver as a projector case study
rather than a seat.

### Death 2 — *"The camp board is a fiction dressed as reality."*

Real NBA clubs cannot buy Cooper Flagg off a board. The module removes the counterparty in L1 and
prices real players at their real salaries as if they were purchasable. A Sports Reality critic is
entitled to call that a fandom-flavoured fiction wearing real numbers — the exact thing CLAUDE.md §3
exists to prevent — and to point out that a student could walk out believing NBA teams shop.

**My answer.** The simplification is registered, stated in the product in the module's own voice
before play, and **corrected by the next lesson's mechanic rather than by a footnote**:

> *"Today the other club is not in the room. The only thing between you and a player is your own
> books. In February we put the other club back, and you will find out how much of today was easy."*

Correcting a misconception with a *mechanic* is the strongest correction available, and it makes the
L1→L2 progression pedagogically load-bearing instead of merely chronological. The board's bottom
tiers are additionally honest by construction: a **$6,064,000 taxpayer-mid-level contract** and a
**$1,357,763 camp contract** are real September objects with no counterparty at all, and they are
rendered as *contracts at administered prices*, unnamed, because that is what they are.

**Where the answer is weakest:** the top of the board. Naming Kevin Durant at $43,902,439 as
available is the part I would defend least. **The cheaper alternative, if a critic rejects it:**
restrict the L1 board entirely to exception-priced and minimum-priced contracts — which is genuinely
how a roster gets finished three weeks before camp — and move every named-star acquisition into L2's
counterparty market, where the other club exists and refuses. The lesson survives that cut intact;
only the glamour of the board is lost.

### Death 3 — *"Roster shape is Team OVR wearing a costume."*

`OPEN < THIN < COVERED < ANCHORED` is a quality judgment the model makes about a real player, and the
tier assignment is invented (§11.6). If the room can read it as *"how good is my team,"* then this
design has smuggled in the exact meter VISUAL_TARGET forbids, and every outcome dimension downstream
inherits the fiction.

**My answer.** Four structural facts, each independently checkable:

1. **It is not a scalar and never becomes one.** Five ordinal cells. There is no sum, no average, no
   weight, no combination anywhere in the model, the payload, or any renderer. A grep for a
   combining function is a real test and it will find nothing.
2. **It is never ranked across clubs on any surface** except as one of five simultaneous, disagreeing
   walls.
3. **Every cell traces to one named signing paid for by one named tool** — full attribution, which is
   exactly what a meter cannot offer.
4. **It is ordinal on purpose, because the pedagogy forbids the alternative.** Grade 5 has no percent,
   no ratio and no negative-number standard, and grades 5–6 have no probability standard at all. An
   ordinal four-state coverage word is the *only* quality object those standards permit.

**Where the answer is weakest, and I will not pretend otherwise:** the ANCHOR/ROTATION/CAMP
assignment for named real players is a judgment the dossier does not source, and CLAUDE.md §13
forbids calling it verified. It must be declared as the module's own judgment **on screen**, not only
in a ledger — *"we've sorted these by what the league pays them"* — and the price ladder must be the
stated basis, since that at least is real and sourced (rookie scale, max tiers, the exception ladder).
If a critic will not accept an authored tier, the honest fallback is to derive the tier **entirely
from the contract**: ANCHOR = paid at or above a max tier or on a top-10 rookie-scale slot; ROTATION =
above the minimum; CAMP = the minimum. That is fully sourced, fully mechanical, and it costs the
design its only remaining editorial judgment — and it is probably what should ship.

## 14. WHAT THE STAGE-0 PROTOTYPE ALREADY FOUND

`stage0/b-l1.html` plays the L1 loop end to end for one desk against five modelled desks, and its
STRATEGY PROBE runs five canned plans — spend to the line, hold everything, copy the most expensive,
balanced, and the trap (anchor-hunt, biggest tool first) — across all six clubs with contention live.
It is not a mockup and it found four real defects in this design before any critic touched it. All
four are recorded because a fixed defect that was never written down is a defect that comes back.

1. **State sludge at Oklahoma City.** With OKC starting at fourteen contracts and an owner's mandate
   of "stay under the second apron," *doing nothing was weakly better than every active plan.* That
   is Contract §8 #6, a rejection. **Repair:** OKC starts at thirteen contracts with an OPEN centre,
   and its mandate is a roster obligation, not a line obligation — so crossing the second apron is
   its own modelled consequence (every exception confiscated for the year) rather than a mandate
   failure that decides the question for the pair. The seat now carries a genuine fork: cover your
   centre for $6,064,000 and lose every tool for the year, or stay under the line and stay open.
2. **Two unsatisfiable mandates.** Brooklyn's original mandate ("anchor a second job") and
   Milwaukee's ("cover point guard") could both become **unreachable through other desks' actions** —
   Brooklyn can never reach an anchor with $3.5M of room, and Milwaukee's only reachable rotation
   guard could be taken by a rival. That is FL5(b), the recoverability invariant, failing.
   **Repair:** every mandate is now satisfiable by an always-available action, and the *interesting*
   version of it sits above that floor. Detroit also moved from twelve to eleven contracts, because
   at twelve it could not clear the $148,465,000 floor using camp contracts alone.
3. **The scalar was the defect.** An earlier cut measured roster shape by summing the four coverage
   states into one number. Under that scalar, "balanced" **dominated** at Detroit. Under the vector
   the model actually holds — *how many jobs are ANCHORED* and *how many are COVERED*, which are not
   comparable — nothing dominates anywhere. This is the strongest evidence in the whole exercise for
   §4.0.1's refusal of a team-quality scalar: **the moment the two books were summed, a dominant
   strategy appeared.**
4. **P-TWIN still fails, marginally, on one dimension of three.** *Jobs raised* passes at a ratio of
   1.00 and *spent this summer* at 1.06, but *promised to later years* comes in at **0.97** — the
   widest spread across clubs for one fixed plan is very slightly larger than the widest spread
   across plans at one club. The driver is structural: Detroit has room and can promise $85.7M of
   future money, Denver has none and can promise nothing. This is the territory's named risk arriving
   exactly where §12's Death 1 said it would, and it is **not fixed**. It is 3% short, it is on the
   dimension least central to the reveal, and the two candidate repairs — narrowing the seat set's
   room spread, or dropping *promised later* as a class-facing ordering — both cost something real.
   It is recorded as open.

Current probe verdict, run this session in Node against the prototype's own engine:
**no dominant strategy at any of the six clubs · no state sludge at any club · every mandate
satisfiable at every club · fifteen cases where paying less bought at least as much roster (FL1) ·
P-TWIN 1.00 / 1.06 / 0.97.**
