## 1. THE ONE SENTENCE

> **You run a real NBA front office that already has more problems than money, and every summer
> move you make either buys you a solution now or takes away a move you will need in February.**

Teacher's version, sayable cold: *"You're the GM. You can't fix everything. Pick which trouble
you can live with."*

---

## 2. THE CORE FANTASY

**The 10-year-old leans in because the desk is real and the problem is embarrassing.**
You open Detroit and the league's published payroll says $136,252,755 — the *cheapest team in the
NBA* — and the second published number says $188,615,753, and the difference is $52,362,998 of
chairs you are paying for that have nobody sitting in them (§4.1 #6, §7.6). You open Phoenix and
the fifth-biggest line on your payroll is **Bradley Beal, $19,383,010 a year through 2029-30, who
plays somewhere else** (§4.1 #5). That is not a tutorial. That is an inherited mess with your name
on it, and a ten-year-old's first instinct — *fix it* — is the exact instinct the lesson is going
to break.

**The 13-year-old leans in because the rules are a weapon and they can be turned on you.**
There is a move in this game that costs you nothing today and quietly makes a different move
illegal in February — and *you* are the one who does it to yourself (§2.3, the hard-cap trigger,
cbaguide high, corroborated; 22 of 30 real teams were operating under one as of 2026-07-10). A
13-year-old who notices that before the reveal feels like they broke the game. They did not. They
found the game.

**Both bands lean in for the same third reason: the eight cards are face up.** From the first
minute of L1 the wall shows the eight things that can go wrong this season. You cannot afford to
be safe against all eight. Choosing which three you can survive is the entire strategic layer, and
it is a real front-office conversation held in real front offices in July.

## 3. THE FALSE INTUITION AND HOW IT BREAKS

The module opens on **"my job is to build the best basketball team possible."**

It is broken in three places, in this order, and each break is a different *kind* of failure so
the intuition cannot re-form around a workaround.

### Break 1 — L1, the CONSEQUENCE beat, ~minute 34. *"You built it and it didn't survive contact."*

Camp opens. Each desk's pressure card lands from the published eight. The desk that spent every
pocket answering all five needs with one player each discovers that **THE KNEE** takes their only
rim protector to zero — a need that no minimum contract can rebuild — while the desk beside them,
who answered four needs and put two cheap bodies on the fifth, takes the same card and comes out
still covered.

*Replaced by:* **the best team is the team that is still a team after something breaks.**

### Break 2 — L2, the SIGNATURE DECISION, ~minute 20. *"You are not allowed."*

February. Two mid-sized contracts on your books and one player who fixes you. Combining them is
the move. The desk that used the **non-taxpayer mid-level exception ($15,044,000, §1.2
official-nba)** last July is refused, and the refusal says which July decision did it. The desk
that filled the same hole with minimum contracts makes the identical trade.

Nobody outbid them. Nobody played better. **The rule they triggered themselves took the move off
the table.**

*Replaced by:* **your job is to keep the ability to act.**

### Break 3 — L3, the CONSEQUENCE beat, ~minute 30. *"You were right and it still cost you."*

The room's own summer commitments set the market price for the players still available. A desk
that reasoned perfectly and held its money finds that because eleven other desks committed big,
what is left costs more than it is worth. That is the **2016 cap spike** (§5.9, cap ~$70M →
$94,143,000, official-nba) run as the room's own price distribution, before the room is told
about 2016.

*Replaced by, and this is where the module lands:* **running a franchise is allocating scarce
resources across competing goals under uncertainty — and part of the uncertainty is everybody
else.**

### L1 — **THE SUMMER**

#### 4.1.1 The signature mechanic — THE POCKETS

On screen, three regions.

**LEFT — THE LEDGER.** Your payroll as one tall stacked column, drawn at true proportion, with
five horizontal rules across it at the real thresholds: **floor $148,465,000 · cap $164,961,000 ·
tax $200,428,000 · first apron $209,015,000 · second apron $221,686,000** (all §1.2, official-nba,
high). Each stacked band is a real contract with a real name and a real number — Cade Cunningham
$50,105,628, Devin Booker $57,078,728, Michael Porter Jr. $40,806,150 (§4.1). **Dead money renders
hollow**: Phoenix's Beal $19,383,010 is a ghost bar in the top five (§4.1 #5); Milwaukee's Damian
Lillard $21,311,053 through 2030-31 is the club's third-largest charge (§8.1 #21, corroborated by
the official waiver announcement). Cap holds render as **empty chairs** — outlined, no name, real
dollars.

**CENTRE — THE MARKET.** Per window, a row of contract slots. Each card carries exactly four
things (Design Rule 15): **the role it covers · the price · one plain-language strength · one
plain-language risk.** There is **no rating, no scalar, no stars, nothing sortable.** Prices are
not negotiated — they are the league's administered ladder: full mid **$15,044,000**, room mid
**$9,366,000**, small mid **$6,064,000**, bi-annual **$5,477,000**, veteran minimum **$3,876,529**,
rookie minimum **$1,357,763** (all §1.2). A 7–8 card carries an optional second tier: the real,
dated comparable (e.g. *"Myles Turner signed 4 yrs/$107M with Milwaukee, July 2025"* §5.8) — colour
for the fan, **never a mechanically better outcome**.

**RIGHT — THE POCKETS AND THE TOOLS.** Not a budget bar. A rack of **named, countable pockets**,
each with its own amount, its own eligibility rule and its own side effect:

| Pocket | Amount | Who may use it | Side effect | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CAP ROOM | cap − your salary | only if you are under the cap | using it **destroys** the non-taxpayer MLE and the bi-annual; you get the **room MLE $9,366,000** instead | §2.4 |
| NON-TAXPAYER MLE | **$15,044,000** | over the cap, below the first apron | **hard-caps you at the first apron ($209,015,000) for the rest of the year** | §2.3, §2.4 |
| TAXPAYER MLE | **$6,064,000** | below the second apron | none | §2.4 |
| BI-ANNUAL | **$5,477,000** | below the first apron, room not used | hard-caps you at the first apron | §2.4 (single-source, §7.4) |
| MINIMUM | $1,357,763 / $3,876,529 | anyone, always | none | §1.2 |
| BIRD RIGHTS | any price, over the cap | only on **your own** free agent, only if you have not renounced | one use | §2.5 |

**You get exactly one mid-level.** Taking the non-taxpayer MLE retires the taxpayer MLE and the
room MLE, and vice versa. That single sentence is the whole lesson's engine.

#### 4.1.2 The signature decision — THE ROOM OR THE RIGHTS

Once, before window 1, irreversibly:

> **OPEN THE ROOM** — renounce your own free agents. Their cap holds come off the books. Detroit's
> payroll falls from $188,615,753 to $136,252,755 and **$28,708,245 of real money appears**. You
> permanently lose the right to re-sign any of them over the cap, and your mid-level shrinks from
> $15,044,000 to $9,366,000.
>
> **KEEP THE RIGHTS** — the holds stay on the books and you stay over the cap. No room. But you may
> re-sign **one of your own** at **any price, over the cap**, and you keep the full $15,044,000
> mid-level plus the $5,477,000 bi-annual.

This is §2.5 verbatim — *"to use cap room you must renounce your free agents, which permanently
destroys Bird/Early Bird/Non-Bird rights"*, CBA-tier, high — and §2.4's *"a genuine two-path choice
with no dominant option."* Neither path is better in general; which is better depends on whether
your own free agent covers a need the market cannot.

The moment OPEN THE ROOM is armed, the **FORGONE PANEL** fills with the specific things this
choice destroys, by name and number, and **freezes at commit** (the `foregoneAtLock` pattern,
`draftDay.ts:318`/`:556` — PLATFORM_REALITY calls it the best single idea in the old M1). At the
COUNTERFACTUAL card in REVEAL, that frozen list is what the product hands back.

#### 4.1.3 The exact economic model

Names, formulas and constants. `[MODULE RULE]` = this module's invention, not an NBA fact.

**Needs.** Five, fixed for the world, named in plain language `[MODULE RULE]`:
`RIM` (protects the basket) · `CREATE` (makes shots for other people) · `SHOOT` (spaces the floor)
· `WING D` (guards their best perimeter player) · `DEPTH` (bodies who can play when someone is
out).

**Coverage.** Each rostered player covers 0–2 needs at level 1 or 2 `[MODULE RULE — assigned by
role, printed on screen next to the player, never derived from performance data]`.

```
COVER(need)  = min(3, Σ level over rostered players covering that need)
ANSWERED  ⇔ COVER ≥ 2      THIN ⇔ COVER = 1      OPEN ⇔ COVER = 0
```

The cap at 3 is what makes greedy fail: a third starter at a need you already answer is worth
exactly zero, so what a card is worth depends on what you already own.

**Salary.**

```
SALARY = Σ active contracts
       + Σ dead money                                   (permanent, coverage 0)
       + Σ cap holds        (if not renounced)          (permanent until renounced, coverage 0)
       + 1,357,763 × max(0, 12 − filled roster slots)   (§2.5 anti-gaming backstop)
```

The last term is the reason doing nothing is not free.

**Legality of one move** — the *same* arithmetic that validates every purchase, every repair and
every rescue (hard constraint #17):

```
LEGAL(move) ⇔  pocket unused
            ∧  pocketAmount(pocket) ≥ price
            ∧  filledSlots < 15                                        (§2.10)
            ∧  (hardCapAt = null  ∨  SALARY + price ≤ hardCapAt)        (§2.3)
            ∧  ELIGIBLE(pocket)
```

```
ELIGIBLE(CAP ROOM) ⇔ SALARY < 164,961,000 ; pays up to 164,961,000 − SALARY
ELIGIBLE(NTMLE)    ⇔ 164,961,000 ≤ SALARY < 209,015,000     → hardCapAt := 209,015,000
ELIGIBLE(TAX MLE)  ⇔ SALARY < 221,686,000                   → no hard cap
ELIGIBLE(BAE)      ⇔ SALARY < 209,015,000 ∧ room unused     → hardCapAt := 209,015,000
ELIGIBLE(ROOM MLE) ⇔ cap room has been used
ELIGIBLE(MINIMUM)  ⇔ always
ELIGIBLE(BIRD)     ⇔ holds not renounced ∧ Bird unused ; any price
ONE MID-LEVEL ONLY: using NTMLE, TAX MLE or ROOM MLE retires the other two.
```

**RENOUNCE** (once, irreversible):
`SALARY −= holdsTotal ; holds := 0 ; BIRD destroyed ; NTMLE and BAE destroyed ; ROOM MLE granted ;
append {what: "our own free agents' rights", why: "we renounced them to open $X of room"} to LOST`.

**The second book — ROOM TO MOVE.** Not a meter. The **enumerated list of legal moves you could
still make at the market's standing prices**, computed by the identical `LEGAL()` predicate. The
board and the console show its **count**; the desk sees the list. If the UI says you kept your
options open, the options are literally enumerable (FL8(c)).

**The third book, which the pair cannot see while shopping — WORST OF THE EIGHT.** Re-run the exact
allocation against every card in the deck and take the fewest needs it still answers. It is never
shown during PLAY, because that is the judgment the arithmetic is not allowed to make for them; it
is the counterfactual the product runs and displays at 5–6 in REVEAL, and withholds at 7–8 until
the pair has committed an answer to *"which one of the eight could you not have survived?"*

**What must be tuned, and against what property.** Only three things, and each has a stated bar:

1. **Pool composition per window** — tuned until the property holds: *for every one of the twelve
   seats, in the worst reachable book state, at every forced decision there exist ≥2 genuinely
   different affordable options differing on a dimension other than price* (hard constraint #16,
   C9). Brute-forced over the reachable action space, not spot-checked.
2. **The count of level-2 minimum-tier slots** — tuned until *a pure minimum-stacking strategy is
   strictly beaten by some active strategy for a majority of seats* (FL6(b)) **and** *a pure
   hoarding strategy is strictly beaten by some active strategy for a majority of seats*, both by
   sweep.
3. **The number of open vs thin needs each inherited franchise carries** — tuned until *no
   monotone map exists from money spent to any displayed dimension* (FL1(a)) and *the class
   evidence contains at least one actual instance, from the room's own play, of a cheaper
   allocation beating a more expensive one on a displayed dimension* (FL1(b)).

Nothing else is a free parameter. The prices, the thresholds, the exception ladder, the roster
limits and the empty-slot charge are all the league's.

#### 4.1.7 TIME CUT

**Round unit: the signing window** (`noun: "window"`, three of them). `closeHook:
"teacher:closeWindow"`. `currentKey` = `"w1" | "w2" | "w3" | null`.

`fallbackPolicy`: *"A window that closes with nothing committed signs nobody. The market does not
wait — anything another desk took is gone, and an empty roster spot is still charged $1,357,763."*

Per-desk `unresolved`, both sentences computed:
- `fallback` (teacher): *"Desk 7 has the full mid-level in hand and nothing committed. Closing now
  leaves Detroit with rim protection open and two empty roster spots charged at $1,357,763 each."*
- `selfFallback` (pair, second person): *"You have $15,044,000 in hand and nothing signed. If the
  window closes now you sign nobody, the centre goes to whoever did commit, and your two empty
  spots still cost you $1,357,763 each."*

**Why the fallback cannot dominate** (contract §8 #6): doing nothing preserves ROOM TO MOVE — a
real book — so the fallback is *not* worthless, which is honest. But it (a) leaves needs open,
(b) triggers the empty-slot charge, (c) forfeits pool slots permanently, and (d) leaves the desk
maximally exposed to five of the six personal cards. The property test asserts, over the reachable
state space, that **no seat's fallback outcome dominates that seat's best available action.**

### L2 — **THE DEADLINE**

*February 2027. The season has run. Your July is on your desk. What may you still do?*

#### 4.2.1 The signature mechanic — THE TOOL RACK

The right-hand rack from L1 returns, and **the first thing that happens in L2 is that items on it
get struck through, one at a time, each naming the July decision that did it.**

```
sign-and-trade ......... UNAVAILABLE — you are above the first apron
bi-annual $5,477,000 ... UNAVAILABLE — you are above the first apron
non-taxpayer MLE ....... USED — July, window 1
prior-year trade exc. .. UNAVAILABLE — you are above the first apron
aggregate two salaries . AVAILABLE
cash in a trade ........ AVAILABLE
```

All four first-apron confiscations and all four second-apron confiscations are the real list
(§2.3, uncontested across all three researchers, and therefore **not** blocked by §8.2 #1, which
only forbids matching *percentages*).

The centre is **THE OFFER SHEET**: your contracts as draggable blocks, the incoming player, and a
single running line that is not a meter but a sentence — *"Sending $23,400,000. Taking back
$26,584,164. This trade takes back more than it sends."*

**The matching rule this room uses is the module's own, in the module's voice**, because §8.2 #1
forbids rendering any of the three incompatible NBA answers (§7.1 — the most dangerous
disagreement in the corpus):

> **[MODULE RULE, printed on screen and in the simplifications ledger]** *"In this room, a trade
> must send out at least as much salary as it takes back. The real NBA rule has more moving parts
> and we have not settled which published version is right, so we are telling you ours."*
>
> Misconception risk: students may believe NBA salary matching is exactly one-for-one. Mitigated by
> the printed sentence and by the teacher note. Resolved by reading CBA Article VII, at which point
> this rule is replaced, not patched.

#### 4.2.2 The signature decision — AGGREGATE OR NOT

You need one player. You have two mid-sized contracts. Combining them is the move.

- If a July mid-level use hard-capped you at the first apron and the trade would leave you above
  $209,015,000 → **refused**, and the refusal names the July signing.
- If you are above the **second apron** → **refused**, because a second-apron team may not
  aggregate two or more salaries in a trade (§2.3, uncontested).
- Otherwise → applied, and you have spent both contracts to buy one, which is a real and
  legible cost.

**The counterparty is a modelled club with its own binding constraint and its own objective, and
it refuses with an economically legible reason** — *"they are hard-capped at the first apron and
cannot take back more than they send."* Refusals are the teaching surface, not friction. This is
the breaker for the compliant-counterparty money pump (Family 3).

**Rival desks do not compete for the same asset in L2.** That is deliberate: the contract
anti-pairs C10 (bidding) with C8 (apron) because a pair that loses a player cannot tell whether it
lost to a rival or to a rule, and attribution is the whole product. Instead, L2's inter-desk
economics runs through **one shared object**: a rebuilding club is shopping one genuinely useful
player and has **published what it wants before the window opens** ("a first-round pick, and we
cannot take back more than $20,000,000"). Multiple desks want him. The seller takes the best
package *by its own published criteria*, and the desks that cannot assemble a legal package are
not outbid — they are **not allowed to bid**. That is attribution-perfect, not
attribution-destroying, and there is no arrival-time tie-break anywhere (M13; ties break on the
seller's published preference, then on picks remaining).

#### 4.2.3 The economic model

Inherits L1's `SALARY`, `COVER`, `LEGAL` and pockets unchanged — **one engine, both lessons, both
bands** (contract §6, requirement 2). L2 adds exactly three rules:

```
TRADE_LEGAL(out[], in) ⇔  Σ salary(out) ≥ salary(in)                       [MODULE RULE]
                       ∧  (|out| ≤ 1  ∨  SALARY < 221,686,000)             §2.3 aggregation ban
                       ∧  (hardCapAt = null ∨ SALARY − Σout + in ≤ hardCapAt)
                       ∧  filledSlots − |out| + 1 ≤ 15
                       ∧  counterparty accepts                              (below)

COUNTERPARTY_ACCEPTS ⇔  their SALARY − in + Σout ≤ their hardCapAt
                     ∧  Σout ≤ their published take-back ceiling
                     ∧  the package meets their published want

TAX (7–8 only, [MODULE RULE, labelled as this room's arithmetic]):
  over = max(0, SALARY − 200,428,000)
  bill = a printed schedule of this room's own making, never an NBA figure (§8.2 #5)
```

At 5–6 **the tax does not exist**. See §6.

declining to match ~$22M for Kentavious Caldwell-Pope because matching would have frozen long-term
draft capital — *the cost was not money, it was future optionality* (the projector-safe version) ·
§2.2 timing: **the tax bill is computed on end-of-regular-season team salary**, which is the entire
mechanical justification for a deadline lesson having stakes.

#### 4.2.5 Phases

`LOBBY · HOOK · PLAY · REVEAL · CONSEQUENCE · ADAPT · SYNTHESIS · COMPLETE`

#### 4.2.6 The four spectacle beats

- **SIGNATURE DECISION — AGGREGATE OR NOT.**
- **CONSEQUENCE — THE TOOLS GO DARK.** Items struck through one at a time, each naming its cause.
  Phoenix 2024-25's four confiscated tools as literally struck-through items is the dossier's own
  prescription (§5.4).
- **CLASS REVEAL — WHO COULD AND WHO COULDN'T.** The room sorted by **what was legal for them**,
  not by what they achieved. Two columns: desks who could still aggregate, desks who could not, and
  under each, the July decision that put them there.
- **INTELLECTUAL REVEAL — THE RULE, NOT THE PLAYERS.** Boston, summer 2025: the reigning champion
  traded away Jrue Holiday and Kristaps Porziņģis and its projected combined salary-plus-tax fell
  from ~$540M to ~$280M, landing under the second apron (§5.4, cbssports high). Nobody got worse
  at basketball. *A rule took a champion apart.*

#### 4.2.7 TIME CUT

**Round unit: the deadline hour** (`noun: "hour"`, three of them, counting down to 3 p.m. ET).
`fallbackPolicy`: *"An hour that closes with an unsent offer sends nothing. Your books are exactly
as they were, which is a real answer at a deadline — standing pat is a decision."*

`selfFallback` example: *"Your offer for the centre is not sent. If the hour closes now you keep
both contracts, keep both roster spots, and rim protection stays open into the last hour."*

Honest and non-dominating: standing pat is genuinely legal and genuinely sometimes right — seven
of fourteen projected 2025-26 taxpayers ducked at the deadline (§3.8, high on the list). But it
leaves the need open, and the tax bill is struck on end-of-season salary, so the desk that stands
pat above the tax line pays for standing pat.

#### 4.2.8 Projector, beat by beat

HOOK: one sentence — **"IT IS FEBRUARY. HERE IS WHAT YOU MAY NO LONGER DO."** and nothing else, for
five seconds. · PLAY: the shared seller's published want, and the remaining supply. No comparative
money while any decision is open (R13). · REVEAL: consummated deals only, in full — that is exactly
what a real market discloses. · CONSEQUENCE: the confiscation list, class-wide, as counts. · ADAPT:
the seller's choice and its stated reason. · SYNTHESIS: Boston.

#### 4.2.9 Director

**HOOK — NOW:** do not explain the aprons. Show the struck-through list and let them ask.
**ASK:** "Whose fault is that?" **WATCH FOR:** the desk that says "we did that in July." That is
the whole lesson and a student said it. **PLAY — DON'T EXPLAIN YET:** the words *hard cap.* They
are experiencing it. **ASK at hour 2:** "Two desks want the same player. One of them is not allowed
to want him. Is that fair?" **WATCH FOR:** desks trying the same illegal trade three times — walk
over; the refusal reason is on their screen and they are not reading it. **SYNTHESIS — THE NAMING:**
5–6 gets *constraint* and *trade-off*; 7–8 gets *hard cap*, *marginal cost*, *commitment*.

### L3 — **THE BOARD MEETING**

*April 2027. The season is over. The owner has one question, and you have to answer it out loud.*

#### 4.3.1 The signature mechanic — THE OWNER'S QUESTION, THEN THE DEFENCE

Two halves, and the order is load-bearing.

**Half one — the decision.** Your own best player's next contract is due. Three paths, all legal,
none dominated, each with a real NBA precedent:

| Path | What it does | Real anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **PAY IT** | The biggest offer available. Keeps him. **He cannot be traded for one year after signing** — the biggest offer is also the one that removes the escape hatch. Guarantees the apron problem twelve months out. | Designated Veteran rules, §2.8, CBA high. Boston: Tatum 5 yrs/$314M (§5.10, official-nba) beside Brown up to $304M — two maxes on one team mathematically guarantee the squeeze |
| **THE MIDDLE** | Shorter, smaller. Keeps flexibility. He may reach the market in two years and leave for nothing. | §2.8: 1–2 season contracts are not extendable at all, and genuinely underpaid stars **cannot** be paid fairly by extension and must reach free agency |
| **CASH HIM IN** | Trade him now, for picks and room, at the top of his value. | OKC traded Paul George for five firsts and two swaps in July 2019 and cashed them in 2025 (§5.3, official-nba). **The same trade is taught from the Clippers' desk in the same session** — one transaction, two opposite lessons |

**Half two — the defence, and it happens BEFORE the outcome.** The pair must commit, on their own
device, two things:

1. **Which definition of success were you playing for?** (the five columns of §7 — you pick one)
2. **What would have to be true for the other choice to be right?** (7–8) / **What is the one thing
   that could make this go wrong?** (5–6, choosing from the eight cards)

Only then does the outcome resolve. The pair has staked a claim they cannot revise, which is what
makes the C12 sort in ARGUE a sort of *their own* reasoning rather than a slide.

#### 4.3.2 The consequence — THE ROOM'S OWN PRICE

The market price for the remaining players is **computed from what the room did**:

```
[MODULE RULE, tuned against the properties below]
committedShare = (desks that chose PAY IT) / (desks total)
priceIndex     = 1 + k · committedShare          k tuned, see below
askingPrice(slot) = round(basePrice × priceIndex)
```

When most of the room commits big, everyone still shopping pays more. That is the **2016 cap
spike**, run as the room's own price distribution before anybody is told about 2016 (§5.9's exact
prescription: *run the auction first, reveal 2016 second*). It is also the module's honest source
of *good reasoning, bad outcome*: a desk that held its money on perfectly sound grounds can be
punished by eleven other desks' choices, with a fully announced cause and no probability, no
expected value and no `Math.random` anywhere.

`k` is tuned against exactly two properties: (i) at every reachable `committedShare` there are still
≥2 affordable options for every seat (recoverability), and (ii) the price movement is large enough
to be visible on a projector at the back row and small enough that holding is not strictly
dominated at any share.

**The floor binds here.** A desk that takes CASH HIM IN and strips the books hits **$148,465,000**
and must spend up to it or pay the shortfall to players league-wide, receiving nothing (§2.1,
§8.1 #2). This is the module's one place where the floor is a mechanic, and it is what makes the
cap unmistakably not a wall (FL2(c)).

#### 4.3.3 Phases

`LOBBY · HOOK · PLAY · REVEAL · COUNTERFACTUAL · ARGUE · SYNTHESIS · COMPLETE`

COUNTERFACTUAL and ARGUE earn their declaration here and only here, because the pair has committed
a written claim that the product can hand back to them.

#### 4.3.4 The four beats

- **SIGNATURE DECISION — THE OWNER'S QUESTION.**
- **CONSEQUENCE — THE ROOM'S OWN PRICE.**
- **CLASS REVEAL — THE FOUR QUADRANTS.** Good decision / bad outcome, bad decision / good outcome,
  and the two agreeing cells, populated **from the room's own play**, with at least one real
  instance in each of the two interesting cells (required, and guaranteed by the price mechanism
  plus the eight-card deal).
- **INTELLECTUAL REVEAL — 2016.** The cap went from about $70,000,000 to **$94,143,000** in one
  summer (§8.1 #5, official-nba) because the players' union rejected smoothing. Everyone had money
  at once. **Before the reveal, the class proposes its own smoothing rule.** Then: 2025-26 at
  exactly **+10.0%**, at the CBA ceiling, and 2026-27 at **+6.7%**, under it. Institutions learn.

#### 4.3.5 TIME CUT

**Round unit: the board session** (`noun: "session"`, two: the decision, then the market).
`fallbackPolicy`: *"A session that closes without an answer answers THE MIDDLE and records that the
room made the choice for you, which is the only honest thing a deadline can do."* The desk is told
this in advance, in the second person, and it is not the best path for any reachable seat.

#### 4.3.6 Director (compressed)

**HOOK — NOW:** read the owner's question aloud. **DON'T EXPLAIN YET:** which answer you would
give. **PLAY — ASK:** "What would have to be true for the other answer to be right?" **WATCH FOR:**
desks who pick a definition of success that contradicts their own decision — that is the best
conversation in the module, and it is theirs, not yours. **ARGUE — NOW:** put two desks side by
side who made the *same* decision and got different results. **ASK:** "Who made the better
decision?" and then refuse to answer. **SYNTHESIS — THE NAMING:** 5–6, *risk* and *consequence*;
7–8, *option value*, *commitment device*, *decision quality vs outcome*.

## 5. THE CARRY

### 5.1 What moves, and in what shape

Exactly six fields, a **bounded flat record**, no log, no transcript, no derived value. Sized so
that a three-lesson chain does not accrete — state size is a first-class budget because every
session read deep-clones the whole state through JSON (`snapshotRepository.ts:45`) and every write
re-serialises the store.

```ts
type FranchiseCarry = {
  v: 1;
  band: "5-6" | "7-8";              // provenance, see §5.4
  club: ClubId;                     // franchise identity — real club, real market, real building
  books: { y1: number; y2: number; y3: number };   // committed salary per league year
  dead: { name: string; amount: number; lastYear: number }[];
  lost: { what: string; why: string }[];           // irreversible losses, as absences WITH REASONS
  tools: {
    midLevelUsed: "ntmle" | "taxmle" | "roommle" | null;
    baeUsed: boolean;
    birdUsed: boolean;
    renounced: boolean;
    hardCapAt: 209015000 | 221686000 | null;
  };
  slots: number;                    // roster slots filled
};
```

Field-by-field justification against the contract's §7 list: **1 books by year** (three years,
because every device in the module — stretch, dead money, rookie-scale team options in years 3 and
4, raises — operates over exactly that horizon) · **2 dead money by year and by name** (the name is
what makes it sunk cost) · **3 irreversible losses as absences with reasons** · **4 tool state,
including whether a hard cap was triggered and at which line** (one flag and one number; the
cheapest and richest carry in the dossier) · **5 roster slots** (the second binding constraint) ·
**6 franchise identity**.

### 5.2 The falsifiable property (P1)

> **In L1**, a desk running **Detroit** fills its rim-protection hole with the **non-taxpayer
> mid-level exception, $15,044,000**. Under §2.3 that converts the first apron, **$209,015,000**,
> into an absolute wall for the remainder of the league year. Detroit's L1 salary after the signing
> is $203,659,753.
>
> **In L2**, action **A** = *aggregate John Collins ($17,000,000) and Duncan Robinson
> ($15,992,957) and take back a $34,000,000 centre.* Post-trade salary = $204,666,796 — legal
> against the cap, legal against the tax, **and $204,666,796 ≤ $209,015,000, so it passes.** Now
> the same desk under history **H1** that also used the **bi-annual $5,477,000** in window 2 sits
> at $209,136,753 in February; **A is refused**, and the refusal reads: *"This trade would put you
> at $209,136,753. You may not go above $209,015,000 — you set that wall yourself in July when you
> used the mid-level."*
>
> Under **H2**, the desk that filled the same two holes with two minimum contracts sits at
> $190,331,279 with `hardCapAt = null`, and **A applies**.
>
> Asserted by replaying the reducer over both branches. If no such `(A, H1, H2)` triple exists,
> the persistence is narration.

**Companion properties, all required:** **P2 no sludge** — for every field in the envelope there
exists at least one reachable L3 state that differs on the basis of that field alone; a field with
no reachable consequence is deleted, not documented. **P3 recoverability** — for every reachable
seeded L2 and L3 opening, including every reachable dead-money position (Memphis's $3,926,207 of
room against $21,909,021 of dead money is a real position and must be playable) and including the
honestly-labelled stock franchise, at least one legal action satisfies the lesson's obligation.
The minimum contract is the universal escape hatch and it is a real rule — that is what makes P3
provable rather than tuned. **P4 attribution per band** — at 5–6 the causal sentence at L2's
opening is **computed from `tools` and `lost`**, never authored per scenario; at 7–8 it is silent,
and the information needed to diagnose it is present on the 7–8 surface.

### 5.3 What deliberately does NOT carry

| Not carried | Why |
|---|---|
| The pressure cards drawn, and every per-window intermediate | The runtime already owns the class log and the WHILE-YOU-WERE-AWAY fold-in. A seed that is a transcript is a transcript. |
| Coverage, ROOM TO MOVE, totals, distance-to-a-line, cap room | All derivable from `books` + `tools` + `slots`. D15's frozen-fact discipline is precise: carry the **facts**, freeze the **claims** inside a lesson, never carry a claim across lessons. A carried claim has no reducer behind it in the receiving lesson and will eventually contradict that lesson's own arithmetic. |
| Any other desk's data, in any form | `studentView` must never leak another seat, and a carried blob is the easiest place for that to happen unnoticed. |
| Any strategy label, award, outcome grade or evaluation of how the last lesson went | `classifyStrategy` is on the discard list for a reason. A carried judgment is a progression system (D4). |
| The season's on-court results | There are none. This module does not simulate basketball, and a fake standings table that gates nothing is what killed the old L2 (`tradeDeadline.ts:575`). |

**The practical test that settles the rest:** *if the module cannot ship an honestly-labelled stock
value for a field, that field is not carryable* — because an absent student gets a stock franchise,
not a broken one, and a stock franchise is never disguised as a carried one (D18). Every field
above has one.

### 5.4 The cross-band guard

`PLATFORM_REALITY §3.3` is right that the seed envelope is the missing D38 attachment point. This
design **requires** `band` inside the carry record itself, so that even if the runtime envelope is
not amended, the receiving module can refuse. A 7–8 L2 seeded from a 5–6 L1 is **refused or
honestly relabelled as stock, never accepted silently** — the two bands do not present the same
decision set (5–6 never had a compound move and never triggered a hard cap by its own
transaction), so a silent cross-band carry attributes to the student a constraint they were never
given the chance to create. That is an attribution failure, and attribution is the one thing this
module cannot lose.

## 6. THE TWO BANDS

### 6.1 What is SHARED — non-negotiable

**One world** (same league, same 2026-27 thresholds, same twelve clubs, same cases, same dates).
**One reducer** — given the same actions from the same state, the two bands resolve identically; if
they did not, the bands would have learned two different economics. **One consequence engine, one
runtime, one franchise-state shape, one pressure deck, one market.** **Every false-lesson guard
binds in both rooms — no band is ever exempted from one.** **Decision quality ≠ outcome in both
bands**; what differs is who does the separating. **Irreversibility and recoverability in both.**
**Attribution in both.** **Real numbers real in both** — what changes is the arithmetic the student
must perform, never the world.

### 6.2 What the grade profile selects

| Dimension | 5–6 | 7–8 |
|---|---|---|
| **Needs shown** | 3 (the ones this club has OPEN or THIN) | all 5 |
| **Decision-relevant variables per card** | 2 — price and role. Strength and risk are single icons with words. | 3–4 in a scannable table, plus one deliberately non-causal column so the debrief can expose it |
| **Choice-space width** | 5 slots per window, all legal, none dominated, **no compound moves** | 9 slots per window, **plus the compound move**: `renounce → use room`, where the legality of step 2 depends on step 1; plus one dominated-*looking* option that is correct under a constraint (taking the **$6,064,000** taxpayer mid-level instead of the **$15,044,000** non-taxpayer one, on purpose, to avoid the hard cap) |
| **Thresholds carried** | **two, of two different kinds**: the **cap** blocks with a plain-language reason; the **first apron** **confiscates** — sign-and-trade and bi-annual are struck off the rack, visibly, no arithmetic. *(Both lines blocking would teach FL2 by construction and is rejected.)* | **three**: cap, first apron, second apron, plus the **hard-cap trigger** — a conditional constraint the student **creates** by their own transaction |
| **The tax** | **absent** | present as a **price** on the marginal dollar, using this room's own labelled arithmetic — never an NBA tax bill (§8.2 #5) |
| **Bidding** | open, sequential, **visible shrinking supply** | hidden simultaneous offers, requiring a belief about what rivals will do *(Design Rule 12 rates itself LOW and unsourced; this is a hypothesis the prototype must playtest at 5–6, not a rule to assume — §7.11)* |
| **Risk** | ordinal only: SAFE / SOLID / BOOM-OR-BUST, and a durability flag. The evidence is the **room's own spread of outcomes for the same card**. | the class's own pooled results as a long-run frequency. **Expected value stays out — it is grade 8 in CEE and it belongs to M4** (D2) |
| **Marginal reasoning** | absent. No ratios, no per-unit figures, no percentages, no negative numbers | the **rising cost of the next dollar** on the mid-level ladder: **$15,044,000 → $9,366,000 → $6,064,000 → nothing.** Your tool shrinks as you spend more. Grade-8 CEE benchmark with no tax bill in it (§8.1 #7) |
| **Counterfactual** | the product **runs and displays** it: *"here is your bench if you had kept the rights"*, from the frozen forgone list | the product **withholds** it; the pair constructs *"what would have had to be true for the other choice to be right?"* and it is revealed only after they commit an answer |
| **Argument** | two moves: "I chose X because Y, and here is the class evidence" (W.5.1, *opinion*) | three moves: the same, **plus the strongest case against their own decision and why their evidence still beats it** (W.6.1 *argument*, W.7.1a, W.8.1a) |
| **Debrief scope** | "What did **your** choice cost **your** team?" — generalization is personal | "Who else in the league was made better or worse off, and would the same choice be right for a different club?" — generalization is societal |
| **Debrief resolution** | must **converge** on one nameable mechanism. No "both teams were right" endings | may end in a **defensible disagreement**, adjudicated by evidence, never at "everyone has their opinion" |
| **New terms per lesson** | **≤2**, named only after the phenomenon was produced | 3–4, may include grade-8 CEE terms; one may be pre-taught to read the interface |
| **Cross-lesson state** | carried, and the causal link **surfaced in plain language** at the next lesson's opening, **computed from the carried field** | carried **silently**; the pair diagnoses why the room is tight |

### 6.3 The one asymmetry that is not a depth setting — and the 5–6 scaffold, concretely

Problem-solving-before-instruction is **g = +0.50 at grades 6–10 and g = −0.09 at grades 2–5**, and
the moderator splits at exactly this product's band boundary (§6.3, Sinha & Kapur, high; direction
well-supported, magnitude uncertain per §7.11). The 5–6 band contains grade 5. **Running the 7–8
front half on a grade-5 room runs the exact condition the meta-analysis found negative.**

So 7–8 faces the constraint cold for windows 1–2 and gets the teacher-led contrast only at
consolidation. **5–6 gets a scaffolded front half, and here is exactly what that is:**

1. **A worked first window, run with the class, on the projector, on a thirteenth franchise nobody
   is sitting at.** The teacher makes one signing live. The class watches the pocket empty, the
   tool disappear from the rack, and one need turn from OPEN to ANSWERED. Roughly four minutes.
   **Window 2 then presents a card that is different in kind, not the same card with new
   numbers** — otherwise the room copied a demonstration and only the consolidation happened.
2. **A visible constraint state that never scrolls away.** The pocket rack and the ledger are
   pinned in the header at all times, and the running total is **never negative** — going over a
   line is a **blocked action with a plain-language reason**, not a minus sign.
3. **An in-product prompt naming the trade-off being faced, at the moment it is faced**, computed
   from the pair's own books: *"Using the $15,044,000 mid-level here means you cannot use it on the
   centre in window 3, and it sets a wall at $209,015,000 you cannot cross for the rest of the
   year."* Named trade-off, no verdict, no recommendation.
4. **A mandatory teacher-led contrast at consolidation** — the director script hands the teacher two
   named desks with the same card and different outcomes, computed.
5. **A one-tap UNDO until the window closes.** Reversible until commit; only an actual retraction
   costs.

Both bands keep the consolidation stage in full. Only the front half is released. One honest note
for the teacher, in the director panel: *exploration-first costs immediate performance and buys
transfer, so a flat quiz right afterwards is not a failure signal.*

## 7. THE CLASS REVEAL — THE FIVE COLUMNS

The projector shows **five columns at once**, each a different, defensible definition of success,
each with a **different desk at the top**, and **no aggregate anywhere**.

| Column | What it means | How it is computed |
|---|---|---|
| **STILL COVERED** | Which needs you answer *after* your pressure card landed | the coverage strip, drawn as five named cells — **never a number, never sorted** |
| **STILL ABLE** | How many legal moves you have left going into February | `|ROOM TO MOVE|`, by the same `LEGAL()` predicate that validated every purchase |
| **WHAT IT COST** | Total 2026-27 committed salary | `SALARY`. **Displayed, never ranked, and the board carries no payroll ordering anywhere** (FL1(c)) |
| **WHAT YOU COULD HAVE SURVIVED** | The **worst of the eight**: re-run your exact allocation against every card in the deck and take the fewest needs it still answers. This is the third book, and it is the one you could not see while you were shopping. | `min` over the deck of `ANSWERED` after applying that card, by the same reducer |
| **WHAT YOU GAVE UP** | The frozen forgone list, by name | `foregoneAtLock`, frozen at commit |

**The columns disagree by construction.** The desk on top of STILL COVERED is spent out and near
the bottom of STILL ABLE. The desk on top of STILL ABLE answered three needs. The desk on top of
THE INSURANCE THAT PAID did it by buying two cheap bodies nobody thought were interesting, and
they are mid-table on everything else. The class cannot resolve this by looking, which is the point.

**Three hard rules on this surface.** No universal franchise score exists in the model, the payload
or the renderer. No column is a ranking of people — the board shows the shape, and where it shows
an order it shows an order *within a single named column* with the column's definition printed
beside it. No trophy, no champion, no confetti, no XP, no level, no rank (D4, and VISUAL_TARGET's
three hard prohibitions).

Below the columns, one row: **HOW DESKS PLAYED IT** — the room clustered by the *strategy they
actually pursued*, named from their own committed actions (SPENT THE TOOLS / OPENED THE ROOM /
STAYED CHEAP AND DEEP / STOOD PAT), with the count of desks in each and each cluster's outcome
across all five columns. VISUAL_TARGET calls this the single best idea in the founder's three
mockups, and it is strictly better than a leaderboard because the unit of comparison is a
*decision*, not a person.

## 8. THE FINAL DEFENCE

L3's ARGUE phase, and it is the only phase in the module where the product mostly stops and the
room talks.

### 8.1 Grades 5–6 — the product does the separating

The projector puts up **two desks that made the same decision and got different results**, computed
from the room, side by side, with the card each drew. The teacher says the sentence for them, and
the director script gives it verbatim: *"These two made the same call. One of them got card 1 and
one got card 6. The call was the same. The result was not."*

Then each pair answers two moves on their own device, and the answers go nowhere near a score:

1. **"We chose ___ because ___."**
2. **"Here is the class evidence: ___."** (a picker over the five columns, so the evidence is the
   room's, not an opinion)

Convergence is required (Rule 5). The debrief must land on one nameable mechanism, and the
mechanism is: **you cannot judge the decision by the result, because the result had a second
author.** No "both teams were right" ending.

### 8.2 Grades 7–8 — the students do the sorting

A **four-quadrant sort**, populated from the room's own play, with real instances in each of the
two interesting cells guaranteed by the price mechanism and the eight-card deal:

```
                   GOOD OUTCOME        BAD OUTCOME
GOOD REASONING     [desks…]            [desks…]   ← the cell that matters
WEAK REASONING     [desks…]   ← the other cell    [desks…]
```

Pairs drag desks (their own included) into cells and **must defend the sort**, in three moves:
their claim, the strongest case *against their own decision*, and why their evidence still beats it.

Then the historical calibration, in this order, because the order is what stops the contrarian
heuristic from replacing the outcome bias:

1. **Portland drafts Greg Oden over Kevin Durant, 2007-06-28.** Run as a two-stage reveal on 2007
   information only. Oden was the widely accepted pre-draft consensus No. 1 (§5.6, high). **Most
   classes will also pick Oden.** That is the point.
2. **Milwaukee trades for Jrue Holiday, 2020-11-17 → the 2021 title** (§5.7, team-official, high).
   The consensus move, genuinely right, and **indistinguishable ex ante** from the ones that failed.
   Without this case the module teaches contrarianism instead of outcome bias.
3. **Milwaukee's Lillard bet, 2023 → waived and stretched 2025-07-01, $21,311,053 a year through
   2030-31** (§5.6, §8.1 #21). *The same franchise, the same category of aggressive bet, twice.*
4. **Dallas trades Luka Dončić for Anthony Davis, 2025-02-01/02**, with Nico Harrison's actual
   quoted rationale on screen (§5.6, high). Students evaluate the *reasoning at the time*. **The
   product does not editorialize the verdict, and no score anywhere rewards matching the historical
   move** — grep-assertable.

7–8 may end in a **defensible disagreement**, adjudicated by evidence, never at "everyone has their
opinion."

## 10. THE VISUAL WORLD

**The place is the cap room at 2 a.m. in July** — not an arena, not a court. A working room with a
wall of numbers, in the Cap Room register: near-black ground, one accent, evidence-tier type,
colour never the only carrier of meaning. Real clubs appear as **typographic wordmarks only** — no
logo, mark, arena image or player photograph anywhere (§7.12 #8, and `http.ts:87-96` serves no
image MIME type at all, so this is enforced by the platform as well as by policy).

**One hero object per surface, and only one.**

- **`/play` — THE LEDGER.** A single tall stacked column, one band per contract, real names, real
  numbers, with the five thresholds drawn across it as horizontal rules at **true proportional
  height**. Dead money is hollow with a rule through it. Cap holds are outlined chairs with no
  name. This drawing's *size carries a quantity*, so under D25 it is measured in a real browser
  against the model, with a stated tolerance and a **poisoned instrument** that must reject a
  deliberately wrong bar before any pass is believed. It may encode only quantities the model
  holds: dollars, yes; anything about how good a player is, never.
- **`/teach` — THE PRESSURE MAP.** THE DESKS, re-shaped for this module: each desk as its club
  wordmark, the card it is about to draw, and one computed word for whether it can absorb it. It
  answers the only question a standing teacher has: *who do I walk to.* Beside it, THE ROOM: the
  spread of the room over the dial this lesson turns on — **which pocket desks are spending
  from** — binned on the pocket ladder itself, committed decisions only, undecided pockets ghosted.
- **`/board` — THE FIVE COLUMNS.** Enormous, sparse, one thing readable from the back row before
  anything populates. It must fit 1366×768 **and** 1920×1080 with no scroll, because a projector
  cannot scroll and overflow is a failure.

**What earns motion — three things, and nothing else.**

1. **A threshold being crossed.** The column grows past a rule and the rule changes from a thin
   line into a wall. One transition, ~400ms.
2. **A tool being confiscated.** The item on the rack gets struck through, once, at the moment the
   cause fires, with the cause appearing beside it.
3. **THE LINE MOVED.** All five rules descend $1,000,000 together, slowly enough that the room sees
   them move. This is the only motion in the module that carries an economic claim, and it is the
   intellectual reveal.

Every motion collapses under `prefers-reduced-motion`. No canvas, no rAF, no WebGL, no animation
library — CSS keyframes and procedural SVG from arithmetic, which is a proven technique on this
platform (`shared/arena.ts`).

**Against VISUAL_TARGET explicitly.** Taken: the franchise identity band (real club, real market,
real building, real financial position); constraint state pinned in the header as *several distinct
resources, never one bar* — which is exactly the pocket rack; INTERESTING CONTRASTS as a first-class
panel; AT TIME CUT as an enumerated pre-close checklist; the grade band visible on both student and
teacher surfaces; status never colour alone; HOW STRATEGIES COMPARED. **Refused:** Team OVR, Fan
Trust, Franchise Value, the 10-year outlook, the single ranked leaderboard, CLASS CHAMPION, the
trophy, Level 1 / 150 XP, "Team 4", the four generic decision cards, the fake "6 Days Left"
deadline, and the nine-item franchise-app nav.

premise — *every pair runs a different real NBA club with a different cap sheet, different
inherited coverage and a different set of legal pockets* — is precisely the premise
`PLATFORM_REALITY §9.2` names as most likely to collide with the runtime. `initialState` runs once

checkpoint and nothing ever pruned. Mitigation: the carry is a bounded flat record by design (§5.1);
the roster is stored as contract references into a module-scope table, not as inlined objects; and
`latency-harness.cjs` plus `e2e-full-room.cjs` at 32 seats run against the new module **early**.

## 12. HOW THIS DIES

### Attack 1 — "It is a crisis-management treadmill with no strategy. You have just built whack-a-mole with real salaries."

**The strongest form of the attack:** each lesson hands the pair a problem, they patch it, a new
problem arrives, they patch that. There is no plan, only reaction. Three lessons of that is
exhausting and teaches learned helplessness, not economics.

**My answer.** The treadmill exists when shocks are independent and the only response is local
repair. Three structural properties break it, and each is falsifiable:

1. **The eight cards are face up from minute one.** The pair is not reacting to surprises; it is
   *choosing which surprise it can survive.* You cannot be safe against all eight — the property
   test asserts that no reachable allocation absorbs more than four — so the whole of PLAY is a
   portfolio decision about exposure, taken **before** any pressure lands. That is strategy in the
   strict sense: a commitment made under uncertainty that determines your response set later.
2. **Slack is four non-substitutable resources, not one meter.** Dollars, roster slots, pockets and
   picks cannot be freely converted. So "keep some slack" is not a decision — *which kind* of slack
   is. A desk with money and no roster spots and a desk with roster spots and no money are exposed
   to different cards, and neither can become the other.
3. **The pressure the desk gets is the exposure the desk chose, and the whole room can see the
   attribution.** Card 1 costs one desk everything and another desk nothing, and the difference is
   legible on the coverage strip. Nothing is a shrug.

And the honest concession: **L1's ADAPT beat is the one place this attack lands.** One repair move
after camp is closer to patching than to planning. It is bounded to one move for exactly that
reason, and if the playtest shows it reads as whack-a-mole it should be cut to zero moves and
replaced with the counterfactual card alone.

### Attack 2 — "Your two books are both derivable before you commit, so a pair can sweep, and the whole thing collapses to arithmetic sorting."

**The strongest form:** COVERAGE and ROOM TO MOVE are deterministic functions of the action. A pair
that tries every combination finds the frontier in three minutes without meaning to. That is the
knapsack failure the contract calls the single most likely M1 failure, and it *feels* like
engagement.

**My answer, in two parts, and the first part concedes ground.**

*Conceded:* both books are derivable, and a pair can and will explore. That is fine and it is not
the defect. The defect would be a **single summable objective with a printed per-item scalar** —
which this does not have, because (a) there are two books that cannot be added and that pull in
opposite directions, (b) there is no scalar on any card, (c) coverage caps at 3 so what a piece is
worth depends on what you already own and greedy provably fails, (d) money and roster slots bind
simultaneously and which one binds depends on the plan, and (e) the market is shared, so what you
can get depends on another desk. Five of five limbs of the reasoning test, not one.

*And the real answer:* **sweeping tells you nothing about the two things that decide the lesson** —
which of eight cards lands on you, and what the rest of the room does to the price. The frontier is
computable; the *right point on the frontier* is not, because it depends on an exposure judgment
the arithmetic cannot make. A pair that sweeps has found the frontier and still has to decide
whether to be the covered-and-stuck desk or the thin-and-free desk. That is the decision, and it is
the decision a real front office makes.

*Falsifier I accept:* if the strategy probe finds one allocation that is weakly best on both books
for a majority of seats, this design is dead and I do not get to argue about it.

### Attack 3 — "You have built a rules simulator. The student is learning CBA trivia, and a kid who doesn't follow basketball is lost."

**The strongest form:** pockets, exceptions, aprons, hard caps, renouncing, Bird rights. That is a
lot of institutional vocabulary for a fifth grader, and the fun is in knowing the rules, which is
exactly the fandom test CLAUDE.md §3 forbids.

**My answer.** The rules *are* the economics here — the cap is the module's title concept, and a
lesson about a constraint that never shows you the constraint's actual shape is the FL2 wall. But
the accessibility claim has to be earned mechanically, not asserted:

- **Every decision is fully decidable from the card alone**: role, price, one strength, one risk.
  Domain knowledge adds colour on an optional second tier and **never a mechanically better
  outcome**. The two named at-risk cases in the dossier (§7.12 #7) are avoided outright: there is no
  Gobert trade and no supermax vocabulary at 5–6.
- **The pockets are never named as CBA jargon on the 5–6 surface.** They are a rack of physical
  things with amounts and rules printed on them. A fifth grader reads *"$15,044,000 — this one sets
  a wall you can't cross"* and does not need the phrase "non-taxpayer mid-level exception" to use
  it. The phrase appears at 7–8 and at SYNTHESIS.
- **5–6 carries two lines, 7–8 carries three.** Not five. The rest of the ladder exists in the world
  and appears only in the SYNTHESIS as facts, never as mechanics.
- And §7.12 #7 records that the fandom-accessibility claim **has never been tested against a
  non-fan reader.** So this design owes a test and names it: **before Stage 1, put the L1 market
  cards and the pocket rack in front of three adults who do not follow basketball and ask them to
  make a legal signing that answers an open need. If any of them cannot, the surface is wrong, not
  the reader.**

## 14. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THIS DESIGN

Stated in advance so the prosecution has targets rather than impressions.

1. A strategy sweep finds one strategy weakly best on **all three** books — needs answered, moves
   left, and worst-of-the-eight — at a majority of seats. *(The Stage-0 prototype runs exactly this
   check, plus falsifiers 2 and 4, and prints the verdict; at 2026-09-03 none of the three fire.)*
2. A sweep finds any reachable state where **doing nothing** is weakly better than every active
   strategy, or where a TIME CUT fallback dominates that seat's best available action.
3. A brute force finds a reachable seeded L2 or L3 opening — including every dead-money position
   and the stock franchise — with **no legal action** satisfying that lesson's obligation.
4. A monotone map is found from money spent to any displayed column.
5. No `(A, H1, H2)` triple as in §5.2 survives implementation.
6. A non-fan adult cannot make a legal signing that answers an open need from the L1 surface alone.
7. Grade 5 playtest shows the scaffolded front half is still the unscaffolded condition in
   disguise — i.e. window 2's card is window 1's card with new numbers.
8. The five columns produce the same desk on top of three or more of them, in which case the reveal
   has an accidental winner and the argument does not happen.
