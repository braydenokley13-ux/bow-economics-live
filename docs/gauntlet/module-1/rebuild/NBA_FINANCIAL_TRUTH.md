## 2. THE RULE SYSTEM
*Written for a product designer who has to model it.*

### 2.1 Five lines, not one

The system is a ladder, and each rung changes the *kind* of constraint, not just the amount:

1. **Floor ($148.465M)** — you must spend at least this. Shortfall is paid to the league and redistributed to *all players league-wide* pro rata by salary, not to your own roster (2023 CBA change) ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/07/hoops-rumors-glossary-minimum-salary-floor-2.html), medium).
2. **Cap ($164.961M)** — a soft cap. You may exceed it only by using an exception (Bird rights, MLE, BAE, TPE, minimum).
3. **Tax ($200.428M)** — a *price*, not a prohibition. Escalating brackets.
4. **First apron ($209.015M)** — a *tool confiscation* line. Money no longer buys you out of it.
5. **Second apron ($221.686M)** — severe tool confiscation plus a seven-year draft penalty.

**Design note:** thresholds 3, 4, 5 are the whole reason this system is teachable. A fee, a prohibition, and a delayed penalty are three genuinely different economic objects sitting on one axis.

### 2.2 The luxury tax (a progressive penalty on the marginal dollar)

Bracket width ≈ the taxpayer MLE for that year ($6,064,000 in 2026-27; $5,685,000 in 2025-26).

| Band above tax line | Standard rate | Repeater rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1st $6.064M | $1.00 / dollar | $3.00 / dollar |
| 2nd | $1.25 | $3.25 |
| 3rd | $3.50 | $5.50 |
| 4th | $4.75 | $6.75 |
| each further band | +$0.50 | +$0.50 |

Sources: [hoopsrumors glossary](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/08/hoops-rumors-glossary-luxury-tax-penalties-5.html) (medium) and [cbaguide.com/thresholds/luxurytax](https://cbaguide.com/thresholds/luxurytax/) (medium). **The entry rate is $1.00, not $1.50** — Researcher 1 flagged $1.50 as a common error.

- **Repeater status:** taxpayer in 3 of the previous 4 seasons ([cbaguide](https://cbaguide.com/thresholds/luxurytax/)).
- **Worked example (single source, medium):** $24M over the 2026-27 tax line ≈ **$62.5M** in tax at standard rates, ≈ **$110.5M** at repeater rates ([stocksandbuckets](https://stocksandbuckets.com/insights/nba-luxury-tax-and-two-aprons-explained/)). The ~2.6× ratio is the memorable number.
- **Where the money goes:** the NBA may distribute 50% of tax penalties to non-taxpaying teams (excluding teams below the floor); the remainder goes to "League Purposes" ([cbaguide](https://cbaguide.com/thresholds/luxurytax/), high). 2024-25: $11.5M each to non-taxpayers. 2025-26: 23 non-taxpaying teams projected at ~$4.9M each ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/04/projected-nba-taxpayers-for-2025-26-2.html), medium — projections, not confirmed).
- **Timing:** the bill is computed on **end-of-regular-season** team salary. That is the entire mechanical justification for a Trade Deadline lesson having real stakes ([stocksandbuckets](https://stocksandbuckets.com/insights/nba-luxury-tax-and-two-aprons-explained/), high).

### 2.3 The aprons (constraints paid in options, not dollars)

**Above the FIRST apron, a team cannot:**
- acquire a player via sign-and-trade
- use the bi-annual exception
- use the non-taxpayer MLE
- sign a player waived during the season whose pre-waiver salary exceeded the NTMLE (the "buyout market" restriction — **Researcher 1 flags that this begins at the FIRST apron and that several outlets wrongly place it at the second**; Researcher 3's cbaguide-derived list does place it at the second — see §7.2)
- use trade exceptions generated in a prior year, or the Expanded TPE, or the Transition TPE

Researcher 2, reading the CBA's Transaction Restrictions Table directly, counts **seven** first-apron-restricted transactions (rows A–G) ([2023 CBA PDF](https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/06/2023-NBA-Collective-Bargaining-Agreement.pdf)). Researcher 1's list from [hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/01/hoops-rumors-glossary-tax-aprons-2.html) is substantially the same set.

**Above the SECOND apron, a team additionally cannot:**
- use *any* mid-level exception, including the taxpayer MLE
- **aggregate two or more player salaries in a trade** (the most teachable rule: you can only swap roughly one-for-one)
- send cash in a trade
- use a TPE generated via sign-and-trade, or use a sign-and-trade player for salary matching

Researcher 2 counts **four** second-apron-restricted transactions (rows H–K) in the CBA table. Researchers 1 and 3 agree on the substance.

**The hard-cap trigger — the single best mechanic in the system for a classroom.** A team is not "assigned" a hard cap. Using any apron-restricted transaction *converts that apron into an absolute wall* for the remainder of the salary cap year, which the team may not exceed under any circumstance ([cbaguide.com/thresholds/apron](https://cbaguide.com/thresholds/apron/), high; corroborated by [hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/07/nba-teams-with-hard-caps-for-2026-27.html)). Your own July choice silently removes your February options. That is consequence and path dependence in one rule, with no vocabulary required.

**Scale of the effect:** as of 2026-07-10, **4 teams were hard-capped at the second apron (GSW, HOU, MIN, PHX) and 18 at the first** — 22 of 30 teams operating under a wall they cannot cross for any reason ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/07/nba-teams-with-hard-caps-for-2026-27.html), high).

**The seven-year draft penalty.** A team over the second apron at the end of the regular season has its own first-round pick **seven drafts in the future** frozen (untradable). If it finishes over the second apron in **2 or more of the next 4 seasons**, that pick is moved to the end of the first round. It unfreezes if the team is under in 3 of 4. Sources: [hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/01/hoops-rumors-glossary-tax-aprons-2.html) (high, corroborated by overtheapron.com) and Researcher 2's direct CBA read (high). **Conflicting version:** Researcher 3's cbaguide-sourced entry and reporting around the Celtics teardown state **3 of 5 years**. See §7.2.

### 2.4 Exceptions (the escape valves)

| Exception | 2026-27 amount | Max term | Who may use it | Hard-cap effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-taxpayer MLE | $15,044,000 | 4 yrs, 5% raises | over-cap teams below 1st apron | hard-caps at 1st apron |
| Taxpayer MLE | $6,064,000 | 2 yrs | teams below 2nd apron | — |
| Room MLE | $9,366,000 | 3 yrs | teams that went under the cap and used their room | — |
| Bi-annual | $5,477,000 | 2 yrs, once every 2 yrs | below 1st apron | hard-caps at 1st apron |
| Minimum | scale by service | — | anyone | — |

The MLE tier structure is the cleanest "your tool shrinks as you spend more" image available: $15.0M → $9.4M → $6.1M → nothing.

The **room MLE trade-off** is a genuine two-path choice with no dominant option: using cap space costs you the bigger MLE.

### 2.5 Bird rights and cap holds (path dependence, formalized)

All from Researcher 2's direct read of the [2023 CBA PDF](https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/06/2023-NBA-Collective-Bargaining-Agreement.pdf), tier `cba`, confidence high.

| Right | Requirement | What the prior team may offer | Term / raises |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bird** (Qualifying Veteran FA) | 3 preceding seasons under contract, no FA team change or waiver clearance after season 1 | up to the player's max, over the cap | 5 yrs / 8% raises |
| **Early Bird** | 2 preceding seasons (team changes only by trade or waiver claim) | greater of 175% of prior salary or 105% of prior-year league average salary | min 2 yrs / 8% raises |
| **Non-Bird** | any other veteran FA | greater of 120% of prior salary, 120% of applicable minimum, or the QO amount | 4 yrs / 5% raises |

**Cap holds ("Free Agent Amounts") — the empty chair still costs money.** Until a free agent re-signs, signs elsewhere, or is renounced, he occupies the cap sheet at: 150% of prior salary (Bird, earned ≥ estimated average salary), 190% (Bird, below it), 250%/300% (finishing a rookie-scale contract), 130% (Early Bird), 120% (Non-Bird) — never above his max or below his min.

**The cleanest opportunity cost in the entire CBA:** to use cap room you must **renounce** your free agents, which permanently destroys Bird/Early Bird/Non-Bird rights to them for that free agency. Afterward you can only re-sign them with remaining room, the minimum, or a two-way. Irrevocable except in a narrow offer-sheet-rescission window.

**Anti-gaming backstop:** a team with fewer than 12 players counted in team salary between July 1 and opening night has team salary increased by one rookie-minimum charge ($1,357,763) per empty slot below 12. You cannot manufacture cap space by emptying the roster.

**Incentive designed on purpose:** re-signing with your own team allows 5 years / 8% raises; signing elsewhere allows 4 years / 5%. The rules pay players to stay.

### 2.6 Restricted free agency (a bargaining-power switch)

- A veteran FA with **≤3 years of service** on June 30 (or one completing a two-way) becomes restricted **only if** the prior team tenders a qualifying offer by **5:00 p.m. ET June 29**; otherwise he is unrestricted July 1.
- **Qualifying offer:** one year, fully guaranteed. For first-round picks, the 4th-year salary increased by the scale percentage (adjusted upward if starter criteria met); for others, 135% of prior salary or the applicable minimum + $200,000, whichever is greater. Withdrawable through July 13; player may accept until October 1 (extendable, never past March 1).
- **Offer sheets:** must cover >1 season (>2 if a max QO was tendered), cannot be two-way, and the signing team must have room continuously while it is outstanding. The incumbent has ~1–2 days to match (or until July 7 for moratorium-period sheets). A matched contract cannot be amended, and the player cannot be traded to the offering team, for one year.
- **The Arenas provision (an engineered poison pill):** an offer sheet to an RFA with 1–2 years of service is capped at the NTMLE ($15,044,000) in year one but permits a balloon third year. The **offering team** is charged the multi-year average for room purposes; a **matching incumbent** must carry the actual spiky year-by-year amounts unless it has room for the average.
- **A second, distinct poison pill (trades):** if a rookie-scale contract is extended and the team tries to trade it before the following July 1, the **acquiring** team counts the average of the last original-term year plus every extended year for matching, while the **trading** team counts only current-year salary. The same player is two different numbers on the two sides of one trade.

All from the CBA PDF (high).

### 2.7 Trades — **the most contested area in this dossier**

There are **three incompatible accounts** of standard salary matching in the corpus. Read §7.1 before modeling anything here.

**Researcher 2 (direct CBA read, high confidence):**
- Standard and Aggregated Standard TPE: take back **100% of outgoing salary + $250,000**.
- Under-cap team: absorb up to cap room + $250,000.
- **Expanded TPE:** greater of (a) the lesser of {200% of outgoing + $250,000} or {100% of outgoing + $9,096,000}, or (b) {125% of outgoing + $250,000} — **only if** post-trade apron salary stays at or below the first apron; triggers a first-apron hard cap.
- Above the first apron the **$250,000 allowance drops to $0** in every TPE, so first-apron teams must match dollar-for-dollar (effectively 100%).

**Researcher 1 (hoopsrumors, medium):** standard over-the-cap matching is **125% + $100,000**; first-apron teams are limited to **100%**. Notes a reputable source (NBC Sports Boston, Jun 2025) says **110%** for the first apron.

**Researcher 3 (cbaguide apron page, high on the restriction list):** second-apron teams are "restricted to **110%** instead of 125%."

**Other trade rules (uncontested):**
- **Traded Player Exception:** created when a team sends out more salary than it takes back. Absorbs up to the traded player's pre-trade salary + $250,000, valid **one year**, and — unlike other exceptions — is **not** reduced daily after January 10. A store of purchasing power with an expiry date. (CBA, high)
- **Sign-and-trade:** player must have finished the prior season on that roster; contract 3–4 seasons with year 1 fully protected for lack of skill; executed before opening night; no NTMLE or room MLE use; acquiring team must have room for year-1 salary and is **hard-capped at the first apron**. (CBA, high)
- **Cash in trades:** $8,495,000 each direction per league year; second-apron teams may not send cash at all. ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/06/salary-cap-tax-line-set-for-2026-27-nba-season.html), medium)
- **Newly signed FAs** generally cannot be traded until the later of 3 months after signing or **December 15**; an over-cap Bird/Early Bird re-signing raising salary >120% pushes that to **January 15**. Rules police intent, not just arithmetic. (CBA, high)

### 2.8 Draft picks as assets

- **Seven-draft trading horizon.** Picks may be traded only through the seventh draft following the trade; immediately after the 2026 draft, 2033 picks became tradable. ([cbaguide](https://cbaguide.com/transactions/trades/traderules/), medium)
- **Stepien Rule** (NBA Constitution & By-Laws, *not* the CBA): no trade may leave a team without a first-round pick in any two consecutive future drafts **under any possible outcome**, including outcomes created by protections. Owning another team's first in one of those years satisfies it; **pick swaps are exempt** because the team retains a selection. ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2024/05/hoops-rumors-glossary-ted-stepien-rule-5.html), medium — **secondary sourcing only; the primary Constitution text was never obtained**)
- **Protections** condition conveyance on draft position; unconveyed picks typically roll forward or convert to second-rounders. Protection is insurance. (medium)

**Rookie-scale contracts — administered prices, not a market.**
- 4 years: years 1–2 guaranteed, years 3 and 4 are **team options** (each exercisable from the day after the prior season through October 31).
- Amount is set by draft slot and the cap; teams may sign at **80%–120%** of scale, and 120% is essentially universal.
- 2026: No. 1 pick $14,748,000 → 4-yr total $66,914,053; No. 30 pick $2,926,800 → 4-yr total $15,031,531. A **~5× price gap set purely by slot**.
- **Rookie-scale extension window:** last day of the moratorium before the player's 4th season through 6:00 p.m. ET the day before opening night of that season; up to 6 seasons total from signing; only for players who will qualify for Bird rights.
- **Designated rookie ("designated rookie max"):** starts at 25% of cap, escalating to 27% (All-NBA 2nd Team), 28% (All-NBA 1st Team), or 30% (MVP) if the Higher Max Criteria are met in the 4th season.
- **Designated Veteran ("supermax"):** 8–9 years of service, all with the team that first signed him (or team changes only by trade in his first four seasons), plus Higher Max Criteria; up to 35% of cap, **exactly six seasons**, and the player **cannot be traded for one year** after signing. The biggest available offer is also the one that removes the escape hatch.
- **Veteran extensions:** 3–4 season contracts extendable no sooner than the 2nd anniversary; 5–6 season contracts the 3rd; 1–2 season contracts **not extendable at all**. First extended year capped at the greater of 140% of the last original-year salary or 140% of estimated average player salary, 8% raises, five-season total. Consequence: genuinely underpaid stars **cannot** be paid fairly by extension and must reach free agency.

All CBA-sourced, high confidence.

### 2.9 Dead money, the stretch provision, and set-off

- A waived player's remaining guaranteed salary stays on the cap sheet in the years it was owed. (CBA, high)
- **Stretch provision:** re-attributes it evenly over **(2 × seasons remaining after the current year) + 1**. Election must be made before the **September 1** preceding the contract's final season. The total does not shrink — it spreads.
- **Ceiling:** a team may not stretch if total waived-player charges in any future season would then exceed **15% of the cap in the year of election** ($24,744,150 in 2026-27 — *researcher arithmetic, not a published figure*). A team that stretches a player may not re-sign or reacquire him until the July 1 after his original contract would have ended.
- **Set-off:** when a waived player signs elsewhere, the original team's remaining **cash obligation** is reduced by 50% of the amount by which the new salary exceeds the 1-year-of-service minimum. Not required to be enforced against non-NBA earnings. **Whether set-off also reduces team salary is unresolved** — see §7.5.
- **Recent small-dollar worked example:** Denver stretched Jonas Valanciunas on **2026-08-27**, spreading ~$2MM over three seasons at roughly **$667K/year** through 2028-29 ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/08/nuggets-to-use-stretch-provision-on-jonas-valanciunas.html), high). This conflicts with a team-page figure — see §7.6.

### 2.10 Roster construction

- **15 standard contracts max** in the regular season, plus up to **3 two-way**; offseason roster max **21**. ([cbaguide.com/eligibility/rosters](https://cbaguide.com/eligibility/rosters/), high)
- **Minimum 14 standard contracts**; may drop to 13 for up to 2 consecutive weeks and 28 total days per season. (cbaguide, **medium — primary CBA article not read**)
- **Two-way contracts:** $678,882 (50% of rookie min); max 3 per team; each limited to **50 active-list games** of 82; players with 4+ years of service ineligible; no more than 3 salary cap years on two-way deals with the same team; postseason-ineligible unless converted. (CBA + cap-database, medium–high)
- **10-day contracts:** from **January 5**; run the longer of 10 days or 3 games; minimum salary; max twice per player per team per season; limited by roster size (0 allowed at 12 players, up to 3 at 15). A cheap information purchase before commitment. (CBA, high)

**Design consequence:** roster *slots* are a second scarce resource alongside money. Two simultaneous binding constraints is what makes allocation interesting rather than arithmetic.

## 3. THE BUSINESS

### 3.1 BRI — defining the pie before splitting it

- **BRI** is essentially all income generated by the NBA and League-Related Entities: tickets, concessions, parking, jersey sales, naming rights and suites (suites/naming counted at **50%** because they are co-licensed with other sports), and league/team licensed products not co-licensed by current or retired players. ([cbaguide.com/finances](https://cbaguide.com/finances/), high)
- The 2023 CBA **added league and team licensing revenue to BRI for the first time** since the cap began — roughly **$160M** added for 2023-24, with total CBA changes projected to add ~**$250M** ([sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2023/nba-players-new-cba-shared-revenue-250-million-income-1234720330/), medium). The definition of the pie is itself bargained over.
- **Players' share: never less than 49%, never more than 51%.** Reduced from 57% in the 2011 lockout; players have received 51% in every season except 2020-21. ([cbaguide.com/finances](https://cbaguide.com/finances/), high)
- **Cap = (44.74% of Projected BRI − Projected Benefits) ÷ 30.** Projected BRI = next year's national TV revenue + all other BRI increased by 4.5%.
- **Withholding replaced escrow (2023 CBA):** standard 10% of player salaries withheld and trued up. If salaries exceed the players' share, the difference goes to owners; if under, the withheld money returns to players.
  - **2024-25 true-up:** BRI came in at **$10.25B**, below projection; players forfeited **more than $480M** and retained **90.9%** of salaries. Stephen Curry, at $55.8M, forfeited **$5.1M** ([sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2025/nba-player-salaries-cut-escrow-system-1234858905/), high).
  - **2025-26:** reported **$580M** split **45%/55%** between players and owners ([sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2026/nba-escrow-players-salaries-owners-million-1234937798/), **low — headline only, article body paywalled. Do not build a lesson beat on this.**)

### 3.2 National media rights — the number that explains everything downstream

- **11 years, ~$76 billion**, with Disney (ABC/ESPN), NBCUniversal (NBC/Peacock) and Amazon (Prime Video), running **2025-26 through 2035-36** ([nba.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-and-nbpa-reach-tentative-labor-deal), high).
- Package split: Disney ~$2.6B/yr for ~80 games; NBCUniversal ~$2.5B/yr for up to 100 regular-season games; Amazon 66 regular-season games per season, **annual fee not confirmed** ([cbssports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/nba-signs-new-tv-deal-details-on-11-year-76-billion-deal-with-espn-nbc-amazon-as-tnt-gets-left-out/), medium).
- **The equality fact:** national TV revenue per team rose from **$103M (2024-25) to $143M (2025-26)** — a $40M jump — growing ~7%/yr to a projected **$281M per team by 2034-35**. Memphis and the Lakers receive the identical check ([sportspro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/nba-revenue-2025-2026-projection-november-2025/), high). This isolates what is *not* unequal and sets up the market-size lesson.

### 3.3 Local media — the collapsing half

- **Main Street Sports Group** (successor to Diamond Sports, operating FanDuel Sports Network) announced **2026-04-03** it would wind down after the 2025-26 regular season, affecting **13 NBA teams** including OKC, San Antonio, Cleveland, Minnesota, Memphis, Indiana, Milwaukee, the Clippers and Miami. None received rights-fee payments in 2026; each could recover **up to ~60%** of lost TV money ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/04/nba-tells-13-teams-to-look-for-new-regional-tv-deals.html), medium — **live and unresolved**).
- Before-and-after for teams that already left the RSN model: **Utah $34M → $16M**; **Phoenix $37M → $15M**; teams that renegotiated with FanDuel after the 2023 Diamond bankruptcy were already receiving $7–14M less per year ([forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nicolekraft/2026/01/31/nba-nhl-teams-could-lose-local-tv-in-fanduel-sports-network-collapse/), medium).
- The NBA is pushing teams toward one-year deals or exit clauses, targeting a **centralized streaming platform for 2027-28** bundling local rights for as many as **22 teams**; YouTube is the leading candidate, DAZN also in contention, potentially >$1B ([forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johncassillo/2026/08/19/nba-consolidating-local-rights-doesnt-streamline-viewing-experience/), medium).
- **This shock reached the cap.** The 2026-27 cap came in **$1M below** the league's own earlier projection (~$165M rather than ~$166M) because local media revenue fell; the reduction flowed through to the floor, tax line, and both aprons ([awfulannouncing](https://awfulannouncing.com/nba/lowerssalary-cap-projection-local-media-revenue-craters.html), medium). Local trouble in a handful of cities moved the spending limit for all 30 teams.

### 3.4 Revenue, gate, attendance

- League team revenue **2024-25: $12.25B**, ~$408M per club. **2025-26 projected $14.3B**, up 12% from $12.75B. Team revenues 2024-25 ranged **$833M (Golden State) to $301M (Memphis)** — a 2.8× spread while both got the same $143M national check ([sportspro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/nba-revenue-2025-2026-projection-november-2025/), high).
- **Gate and arena operations: $3.4B in 2024-25, ~28% of league revenue** ([statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/193410/percentage-of-ticketing-revenue-in-the-nba-since-2006/), medium). The intuitive student guess — "teams make money from tickets" — is about a quarter right, not mostly right.
- Individual gate receipts 2024-25: **Knicks $193M** (record), **Lakers $101M**, **Heat $88M** ([statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/285532/gate-receipts-of-the-new-york-knicks-national-basketball-association/), medium). "Big market" is not one thing.
- Attendance 2024-25 averaged **18,147/game**, second-best in NBA history; >22.3M total for only the second time; Bulls led at 20,138 ([thescore](https://www.thescore.com/nba/news/3268109/nba-set-2nd-highest-regular-season-attendance-mark-in-2024-25), medium). **Buildings are full while local TV money collapses** — an important corrective.

### 3.5 Franchise valuations and sale prices

Forbes, as of **2026-01-08** ([wikipedia compilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_most_valuable_NBA_teams), medium): league average **$5.4B**. Top: Warriors $11.0B (rev $880M, op income $409M), Lakers $10.0B ($551M / $170M), Knicks $9.75B ($532M / $98M), Clippers $7.5B, Celtics $6.7B. Bottom: Hornets $3.8B, Pistons $3.65B, Timberwolves $3.6B ($320M / $41M), Pelicans $3.55B, Grizzlies $3.5B ($306M / $28M). **~3× spread in value, ~15× in operating income.**

Sale prices ([yahoo](https://sports.yahoo.com/nba/article/how-the-los-angeles-lakers-125b-sale-compares-to-the-price-tags-of-other-north-american-sports-franchises-171424922.html), medium):
- **Lakers $12.5B** — agreed August 2026, Josh Kushner and Bob Iger. **Board of Governors approval NOT confirmed as of 2026-09-03.** Treat as "agreed/reported."
- Lakers **$10B** (June 2025, Mark Walter, from the Buss family) — a ~$2.5B / 25% gain in ~14 months.
- **Celtics $6.1B+**, approved by the NBA ([nba.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-approves-sale-of-boston-celtics), **official-nba, high**), rising to $6.5B on a 2028 tranche.
- Trail Blazers ~$4.1B blended (approved 2026-03-31, Tom Dundon group). Suns/Mercury $4B (2022). Mavericks $3.5B (2023).

**Asset appreciation vs operating profit** is the real lesson: the Lakers made more from being resold than from a decade of ticket sales.

### 3.6 Revenue sharing — teach the shape, not the formula

- Each club contributes a fixed percentage of local revenue (reported around **48–50%**) to a central pool; each club then receives an equal share sized around the league's average team payroll. Small-market clubs must reach roughly **70%** of average club local revenue to qualify as recipients. ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/20747413/a-confidential-report-shows-nearly-half-nba-lost-money-last-season-now-what), **low — the formula is confidential and reported inconsistently. Do not put a specific percentage on a projector.**)
- **Actual flows, 2021-22 (label as historical):** ten teams paid **$163.6M** into the pool; Warriors and Lakers alone contributed >$88M, eight other large-market teams added $75.6M. Reported recipients: Pacers $42.2M, Nuggets $35.5M, Trail Blazers $32M, Grizzlies $28M ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/35882999/warriors-9-other-teams-paid-1636m-revenue-sharing-pool), medium).
- **The system is contested from the top.** In a July 2024 letter to the Board of Governors, Knicks owner James Dolan criticized the league retaining "$6 billion (or 8 percent) of the total-NBA related fees" from the media deal without sufficient justification or transparency, noted league retention rising from $15M (0.5%) in 2024-25 to a proposed $358M in 2025-26, and warned the national streaming package "risks rendering the entire [RSNs] unviable," citing a 45% decline for MSG Network ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/40571512/knicks-james-dolan-criticizes-nba-revenue-sharing-new-media-deal), high).

### 3.7 What a GM actually optimizes

Not wins. **Wins subject to an owner's spending mandate.** Three on-the-record cases:

- **Joe Lacob (Warriors, 2024-02-15):** "Our Plan 1, or 1A, is that we'd like to be out of the tax" and "we need to be out of the tax two years out of the next four in order to get this repeater thing off our books." Warriors had paid roughly $350M in combined salaries and taxes the prior season ([cbssports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/warriors-offseason-plan-1a-is-to-avoid-luxury-tax-entirely-owner-joe-lacob-says/), high).
- **Pacers ownership (2025-07):** reported "fully prepared" to go deep into the tax to re-sign Myles Turner; after Tyrese Haliburton's Game 7 Achilles tear, Indiana never offered more than ~$23M/yr ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/45690444/pacers-were-fully-prepared-go-tax-myles-turner), high).
- **Celtics (summer 2025):** projected combined salary-plus-tax cut from ~$540M to ~$280M ([cbssports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/why-celtics-are-trading-away-key-players-money-not-the-only-reason-for-jrue-holiday-kristaps-porzingis-deals/), high).

GM tenure ~6 years, average compensation ~$2.5M/yr with top earners to $10M, contracts typically 4–5 seasons with incentives ([frontofficesports](https://frontofficesports.com/highest-paid-nba-general-managers/), **low — weakly sourced, do not present as precise**).

### 3.8 2025-26 taxpayers (real named bills)

Only **seven** teams finished 2025-26 as taxpayers, totalling **$223.1M**: Cavaliers $68.7M, Warriors $67.9M (repeater), Knicks $44.4M, Lakers $22.2M (repeater), Rockets $7.2M, Clippers $6.7M (repeater), Timberwolves $6.0M. **Fourteen** teams were projected taxpayers before the deadline; **seven ducked it** at the deadline — Celtics, Nuggets, Suns, Mavericks, 76ers, Magic, Raptors ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/04/projected-nba-taxpayers-for-2025-26-2.html), high on the list, medium on the projections).

Half the projected taxpayers flinched. That is a class prediction exercise with a real answer.

## 5. TEACHING CASES

Every case below is public names, dates, transaction terms, and at most one on-the-record quotation. No photography, logos, marks, or proprietary datasets are implicated.

### 5.1 Opportunity cost

**Phoenix trades for Bradley Beal — 2023-06-24** ([nba.com](https://www.nba.com/news/wizards-suns-trade-bradley-beal-chris-paul), official-nba, high). Suns acquired Beal for Chris Paul, Landry Shamet, four future first-round **swaps** (2024, 2026, 2028, 2030) and six future seconds. Beal kept his no-trade clause — the only one in the NBA at the time. Combined with the Durant trade four months earlier, Phoenix had the league's highest payroll entering 2024-25 and could add players essentially only at the veteran minimum.
*Teach:* spent the whole budget on three expensive things; when a fourth was needed, only the clearance bin remained. The no-trade clause is the bonus — they gave away not just money but the right to undo the decision.

**Denver cannot re-sign Bruce Brown — July 2023** ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2023/06/nuggets-bruce-brown-declining-2023-24-player-option/), reporting, medium). Weeks after winning the 2023 title, Denver could offer only ~$7.7M (Non-Bird rights from a prior one-year deal) while projecting over the tax. Brown signed with Indiana for a reported 2 yrs / ~$45M.
*Teach:* the constraint does not care that you won. Violates the kid intuition that winning solves money.

**Renouncing free agents** (CBA, high — §2.5). To spend cap room you must destroy the right to keep your own player. The cleanest opportunity cost in the entire rulebook.

### 5.2 Path dependence

**Brooklyn trades for Garnett and Pierce — 2013-07-12** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/new-york/nba/story/_/id/9473403/kevin-garnett-paul-pierce-jason-terry-trade-brooklyn-nets-official), reporting, high). Nets acquired Kevin Garnett, Paul Pierce, Jason Terry and D.J. White for Gerald Wallace, Kris Humphries, MarShon Brooks, Keith Bogans and **unprotected** firsts in 2014, 2016 and 2018 plus a 2017 swap right. Brooklyn never reached a conference final with that group; Boston used the 2017 swap to hold the No. 1 overall pick while being the East's No. 1 seed.
*Teach:* "unprotected" means the worse you get, the more you pay. Draw the timeline 2013 → 2014 → 2016 → 2017 → 2018.

**Phoenix waives-and-stretches Beal — 2025-07-16** ([gophnx](https://gophnx.com/suns/bradley-beal-waive-and-stretch-suns-stretch-provision/), reporting, medium). Beal had ~$111M and two years remaining; he gave back just under $14M in a buyout, and Phoenix stretched the remainder over five years at ~$19.4M/season **through 2029-30**.
*Teach:* pair it with the 2023 acquisition — same player, two class periods apart, and the second is the bill for the first. Confirmed by Phoenix's 2026-27 dead cap of $19,383,010 (§4.1).

**Minnesota trades for Rudy Gobert — July 2022** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/insider/insider/story/_/id/34775510/the-trade-left-nba-stunned-skeptical-why-wolves-went-all-in-with-rudy-gobert), reporting, high). Sent Malik Beasley, Patrick Beverley, Jarred Vanderbilt, Leandro Bolmaro, the No. 22 pick in 2022 (Walker Kessler), unprotected firsts in 2023, 2025 and 2027, a 2026 swap, and a top-5-protected 2029 first. **47% of NBA general managers** voted it the most surprising move of the offseason in NBA.com's GM survey.
*Teach:* mocked, then two conference finals, and still owes picks into 2029. Breaks the "win/lose trade grade" habit.

**The hard-cap trigger** (§2.3). A July exception use removes February options. The purest path-dependence mechanic in the CBA and the one a Trade Deadline lesson should be built on.

### 5.3 Option value and flexibility

**Oklahoma City trades Paul George for picks — July 2019, cashed 2025-06-22** ([nba.com](https://www.nba.com/news/clippers-thunder-paul-george-shai-gilgeous-alexander-trade-review), official-nba, high). Clippers sent Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, Danilo Gallinari, five firsts (LAC 2022, 2024, 2026; Miami 2021, 2023) and two swaps (2023, 2025) for Paul George. OKC used that capital to win the **2025 championship** over Indiana in seven games, with Gilgeous-Alexander winning MVP and Finals MVP; one pick became Jalen Williams.
*Teach:* lottery tickets over a sure thing, held six years. **Teach the same trade from the Clippers' desk in the same session** — one transaction, two opposite lessons, and the Clippers had a real reason (a championship window with two stars), which makes it defensible-but-wrong rather than stupid.

**Houston spends its stockpile on Kevin Durant — 2025-06-22** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/45683702/keven-durant-rockets-part-complex-seven-team-trade), reporting, high). Acquired Durant from Phoenix for Jalen Green, Dillon Brooks, the No. 10 pick in 2025, and five seconds; later expanded into the **first seven-team trade in NBA history**. Houston had finished 2024-25 as the West's No. 2 seed.
*Teach:* an option is only worth something if you eventually exercise it. Saving is not the goal.

**Brooklyn converts Mikal Bridges into five firsts — 2024-06-25** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/40433116/sources-knicks-acquire-mikal-bridges-nets), reporting, high). Knicks sent Bojan Bogdanovic, five firsts (unprotected NYK 2025, 2027, 2029, 2031; a top-4-protected 2025 via Milwaukee), an unprotected 2028 swap, and a 2025 second; Brooklyn sent back a 2026 second. Tied the Paul George trade for the most firsts in a two-team deal — **for a player who was not an All-Star**.
*Teach:* the same player is worth wildly different amounts to different buyers. Note the buyer was in the biggest market.

**Traded Player Exceptions** (§2.7) and Chicago's $17,991,071 TPE (§4.2) — a coupon that expires.

### 5.4 Apron consequences

**Boston dismantles a title core — June/July 2025** ([foxsports](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nba/celtics-kristaps-porzingis-jrue-holiday-trade-second-apron), medium; [cbssports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/why-celtics-are-trading-away-key-players-money-not-the-only-reason-for-jrue-holiday-kristaps-porzingis-deals/), high). Traded Jrue Holiday to Portland for Anfernee Simons and two seconds, and Kristaps Porzingis to Atlanta in a three-team deal returning Georges Niang. **Salary savings of only ~$4.7M produced ~$35M in tax savings**; projected combined salary-plus-tax fell from **~$540M to ~$280M**, landing ~$4.5M under the second apron.
*Teach:* the 2024 champion took itself apart because of a **rule**, not because the players got worse. The $4.7M → $35M leverage ratio is the lesson. Students find this genuinely shocking.

**Phoenix's confiscated tools above the second apron, 2024-25** ([brightsideofthesun](https://www.brightsideofthesun.com/2024/5/9/24150719/second-apron-phoenix-suns-a-review-of-what-restrictions-are-in-play-for-the-2024-25-season), reporting, medium). Could not aggregate salaries (e.g. could not combine Jusuf Nurkić and Nassir Little to match a ~$24.8M incoming salary), could not take back more salary than sent out, could not use any MLE, could not sign buyout-market players above the NTMLE. **Phoenix's 2032 first-round pick was frozen.**
*Teach:* list the four lost tools as literally confiscated items. A rule triggered in 2024 takes something away in **2032** — a year most fifth graders will be adults. **⚠ the ~$300M tax figure from this fan-site source is unverified; do not print it.**

**Denver lets Kentavious Caldwell-Pope leave** ([si.com](https://www.si.com/nba/thunder-sam-presti-clever-move-forced-nuggets-second-apron), reporting, medium). Declined to match ~$22M because doing so would have frozen long-term draft capital under second-apron rules; KCP signed with Orlando (3 yrs / $66M).
*Teach:* the cost was not money. It was future optionality.

### 5.5 Sunk cost and dead money

**Milwaukee waives and stretches Damian Lillard — 2025-07-01** ([nba.com](https://www.nba.com/news/bucks-waive-damian-lillard), official-nba, high). Two years and $113M remaining, stretched over five seasons at ~$22.5M/year — the largest waive-and-stretch in NBA history. Created >$30M of immediate flexibility, used to sign **Myles Turner, 4 yrs / $107M**. Lillard later signed with Portland. Confirmed in Milwaukee's 2026-27 books at **$21,311,053 through 2030-31** (§4.1).
*Teach:* a loan — money now, more later. And the sunk-cost move: the question was never "we already paid Dame so we must play him," it was "given the money is gone either way, what is the best use of the roster spot?"

**New York waives and stretches Joakim Noah — 2018-10-13** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/24973763/new-york-knicks-waive-joakim-noah), reporting, high). Two years and $37.8M remaining. Cut his 2019-20 cap charge from **$19.3M to $6.4M** (saving $12.9M that season) but cost $6.4M of cap space in summer 2020 **and** 2021.
*Teach:* the clean worked example — $19.3M becomes $6.4M × 3. Small enough for a grade 5-8 student to compute and see that the total does not shrink, it spreads. Use this **before** the bigger Lillard/Beal cases.

**Portland buys out Deandre Ayton — late June 2025** ([nba.com/blazers](https://www.nba.com/blazers/news/trail-blazers-and-deandre-ayton-reach-buyout-agreement), team-official, medium). Ayton gave up ~$10M of the $35.6M owed in the final year, leaving Portland paying **>$25M**. He then signed a reported 2 yrs / $16.6M (player option) with the Lakers.
*Teach:* paying someone $25M to play for another team. Kids find this outrageous, which makes it stick. Also the natural intro to set-off and to negotiated exit.

**Denver stretches Jonas Valanciunas — 2026-08-27** ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/08/nuggets-to-use-stretch-provision-on-jonas-valanciunas.html), reporting, high). ~$2MM remaining, spread over three seasons at roughly **$667K/year** in 2026-27, 2027-28, 2028-29.
*Teach:* recent, named, small-dollar, and the arithmetic is grade-appropriate — students can compute the stretch themselves before "sunk cost" is ever spoken. **⚠ conflicts with the Denver team page showing $2,000,000 of 2026-27 dead cap — see §7.6.**

### 5.6 Decision quality vs outcome

**Portland drafts Greg Oden over Kevin Durant — 2007-06-28** ([wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_draft), reporting, high). Portland took Oden No. 1; Seattle took Durant No. 2. Oden was the widely accepted pre-draft consensus No. 1, projected as a top pick since high school. He missed all of 2007-08 after microfracture surgery on his right knee; repeated knee injuries derailed his career.
*Teach:* run it as a two-stage reveal — show only the 2007 information, have the class vote, then reveal. **Most classes will also pick Oden.** That is the point.

**Toronto trades DeMar DeRozan for Kawhi Leonard — 2018-07-18** ([cbc](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.4752018), reporting, high). Sent DeRozan, Jakob Poeltl and a protected 2019 first for Leonard and Danny Green. Leonard had one year left and was reported to prefer Los Angeles. **Toronto won the 2019 championship; Leonard then left for the Clippers.**
*Teach:* ask before the reveal — would you trade your most popular player for someone who might leave in twelve months? Then reveal *both* facts. Students must hold two truths at once.

**Milwaukee's Lillard bet, 2023 → 2025** ([hoopsrumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/07/bucks-to-waive-damian-lillard-stretch-remaining-salary.html), reporting, medium). Acquired 2023 to pair with Giannis; waived 2025-07-01 with two years and $113M left. Ownership publicly defended the decision.
*Teach:* pair with Milwaukee's own 2020 Jrue Holiday trade — **the same franchise, the same category of aggressive bet, twice**: once a title, once $22.5M/year of dead money. Far more persuasive than comparing two different teams.

**Dallas trades Luka Dončić for Anthony Davis — 2025-02-01/02** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/43659380/sources-mavericks-trading-doncic-lakers-anthony-davis), reporting, high). Dallas sent Dončić, Maxi Kleber and Markieff Morris to the Lakers for Davis, Max Christie and the Lakers' 2029 first; Utah took Jalen Hood-Schifino and two 2025 seconds. GM Nico Harrison's stated rationale to ESPN: *"I believe that defense wins championships. I believe that getting an All-Defensive center and an All-NBA player with a defensive mindset gives us a better chance."* First midseason trade of two reigning All-NBA players for each other.
*Teach:* the hardest and most valuable defensibility case — the criticism was that the **reasoning** was weak at the time (age gap, no bidding war, minimal draft compensation), not merely that the outcome was bad. Use it as the contrast that sharpens the Oden lesson. Give students the actual quoted rationale and have them evaluate it. **Do not editorialize the verdict.**

### 5.7 Draft capital — worked and did not work

**Worked — Lakers trade for Anthony Davis, 2019-06-15/07-06** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/26981805/sources-lakers-reach-deal-pelicans-davis), high). Sent Lonzo Ball, Brandon Ingram, Josh Hart and three firsts — the No. 4 pick in 2019, a top-8-protected 2021 (unprotected 2022 if unconveyed), a 2023 swap, and an unprotected 2024 (deferrable to 2025). **Won the 2020 title.** Teach next to the Clippers' Paul George trade — same summer, similar price, opposite result — and force students to explain the difference without saying "one team was smarter."

**Worked, small market — Milwaukee trades for Jrue Holiday, 2020-11-17** ([nba.com/bucks](https://www.nba.com/bucks/news/milwaukee-bucks-acquire-jrue-holiday-new-orleans-pelicans-part-four-team-deal), team-official, high). Acquired Holiday and the No. 60 pick for Eric Bledsoe, George Hill, three firsts (a 2020 via Indiana, unprotected 2025 and 2027) and two swaps (2024, 2026). **Won the 2021 title**; Antetokounmpo then signed a five-year supermax reported at $228M in December 2020. Essential counterweight to the Thunder/Harden case so students do not conclude small markets always lose. A successful bet still has a cost — the picks are still owed.

**Did not work — Clippers, five firsts and two swaps for Paul George, July 2019** ([nba.com](https://www.nba.com/news/clippers-thunder-paul-george-shai-gilgeous-alexander-trade-review), official-nba, high). Reportedly a condition of Kawhi Leonard signing. No Finals appearance with that pairing; Gilgeous-Alexander became MVP and Finals MVP elsewhere.

**Did not work — Phoenix, four unprotected firsts and a swap for Kevin Durant, 2023-02-09** ([nba.com](https://www.nba.com/news/suns-nets-kevin-durant-trade), official-nba, high). Four-team deal; Brooklyn received Mikal Bridges, Cam Johnson, firsts in 2023, 2025, 2027, 2029 and a 2028 swap. Phoenix did not reach a conference final and traded Durant to Houston on 2025-06-22.
*Teach:* chains directly to the Beal trade and the Beal stretch — **acquire → get stuck → pay for years**. The richest multi-lesson narrative in the corpus, entirely recent, one team across three class sessions.

### 5.8 Market size

**Oklahoma City trades James Harden — 2012-10-27/28** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/8562868/oklahoma-city-thunder-trade-james-harden-houston-rockets), reporting, high). Thunder traded Harden (with Cole Aldrich, Daequan Cook, Lazar Hayward) to Houston for Kevin Martin, Jeremy Lamb, two firsts and a second. Harden rejected a four-year offer paying roughly $53–54M, which would have pushed OKC's payroll to about **$95M against a 2012-13 cap of just over $58M and a tax line of $70.3M**; Thunder officials were unwilling to go higher.
*Teach:* the numbers subtract cleanly. Frame it fairly — OKC was not cheap for fun; the tax bill scales with how far over you are. Then reveal the **2025 title** as the epilogue.

**Indiana vs Milwaukee on Myles Turner, one player, one week, July 2025** ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/45690444/pacers-were-fully-prepared-go-tax-myles-turner), high). Indiana's ownership was prepared to enter the tax for the first time since 2005-06 to retain Turner, then pulled back after Haliburton's Game 7 Achilles tear ruled him out for 2025-26. Turner signed **4 yrs / $107M** (Yr-4 player option, 15% trade kicker) with Milwaukee, which created the room by waiving and stretching Lillard's $113M.
*Teach:* the single best paired case in the dossier — one free agent, two teams, opposite decisions, same week. Indiana's change of mind was **rational once its star was injured**, not evidence of cheapness. One injury changed the expected value of an entire payroll decision. Excellent for assigned-side debate.

**The 2025-26 taxpayer list and the 2024-25 vs 2025-26 distribution swing** (§3.8) — when more teams duck the tax, the payout to those who stayed under shrinks from $11.5M to ~$4.9M each. A genuine collective-action structure students can play and then name.

**Oklahoma City wins from the smallest market — 2025 and after** ([yahoo](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/thunder-offseason-224-million-saved-114000575.html), reporting, medium). Won the 2025 Finals (Gilgeous-Alexander: scoring title, MVP, Finals MVP). In the 2026 offseason the Thunder were reported to have saved **$224M in payroll and luxury tax**, added seven future seconds, and created a $17M trade exception, while owning at least three picks in each of the next five drafts.
*Teach:* directly refutes "the richest team wins." Draft capital as an alternative currency is the cleanest small-market strategy for grades 5-8.

### 5.9 Winner's curse and auctions

**The 2016 cap spike and the contracts that followed.** The cap jumped from ~$70M to **$94,143,000** ([pr.nba.com](https://pr.nba.com/nba-salary-cap-2016-17-season), official-nba, high) because the NBPA formally rejected the league's "smoothing" proposal, which would have phased the increase in at the same 51% BRI share ([espn](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/12461874/players-union-rejects-salary-cap-smoothing-historic-cap-increase-nba-set)). Nearly every team had cap space simultaneously with too few quality free agents; Kevin Durant signed with the 73-win Warriors, and teams that missed handed out max contracts they later regretted ([washingtonpost](https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/a-2016-salary-cap-spike-helped-the-warriors-and-hurt-the-nba-worse-nobody-saw-that-coming/2018/08/06/9851692e-95c2-11e8-80e1-00e80e1fdf43_story.html), high). Named examples: Timofey Mozgov 4 yrs/$64M (Lakers), Joakim Noah 4 yrs/$72.6M (Knicks, coming off season-ending shoulder surgery), Bismack Biyombo 4 yrs/$72M (Magic) ([cbssports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/ranking-12-worst-nba-contracts-of-infamous-2016-free-agency-with-most-finally-expiring-this-offseason/), medium).
*Teach:* run a sealed-bid auction in class **first**, then reveal 2016. When everyone suddenly has more money, prices rise faster than value and the "winner" is often whoever overestimated most. Then show the 10% smoothing rule (§1.3) and the 2025-26 +10.0% / 2026-27 +6.7% outcomes — **institutions learn**. Let students propose their own smoothing rule before you tell them the real one.

### 5.11 Tax counterexamples — pay it or duck it

- **Golden State paid $170.33M in luxury tax for 2021-22** (tax line $136.6M), the highest ever; prior individual record was the Lakers' $45.1M — **and they won the championship that season** ([forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/markdeeks/2022/07/01/a-complete-history-of-nba-luxury-tax-payments-20012022/), high per Researcher 3). **⚠ Researcher 4 could not verify this and found season attribution inconsistent across outlets — see §7.8.**
- **Milwaukee's first-ever tax payment: $794,721 in 2020-21**, triggered when Jrue Holiday's $1M championship incentive pushed the team over the line; the bill grew to ~$42M the next year and >$70M by 2023 ([nbcsports](https://nba.nbcsports.com/2021/07/22/championship-pushes-bucks-into-luxury-tax/), medium). A small-market team crossed by less than $1M and then found the commitment escalating.

Pair "paid it and it worked" against "ducked it" (Boston 2025) so students argue both sides rather than learning a single moral.

### 6.3 The cognitive evidence

- **Mature counterfactual reasoning is not reliable in all children before ~age 12** (Rafetseder & Perner, [Child Development Perspectives 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/cdep.12061); McCormack, Feeney & Beck 2020). Regret is understood at 6-9, but its use in complex tasks keeps increasing into adolescence. Confidence: medium.
- **Sixth graders cannot reliably coordinate three simultaneous causal variables even with the causal structure displayed.** With a chart identifying causal and non-causal effects pictorially and in text, **only ~1/3 of sixth graders** consistently implicated the three causal variables; more considered only one or two, inconsistently across successive predictions. An extended 4th/5th-grade inquiry intervention "did not affect their multivariable prediction skill appreciably." (Kuhn, Pease & Wirkala, [JECP 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096509000253), high)
- **Working memory is still climbing.** ~3 chunks at 11-12 vs ~1.5 at age 7; growth from ~2 chunks at 5 to ~4 at 14, roughly linear. A 10-11-year-old holds about one chunk fewer than a 13-14-year-old. (Cowan, [Current Directions 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2864034/), medium)
- **⚠ THE MOST CONSEQUENTIAL FINDING FOR BOW'S CORE LOOP.** Problem-solving-before-instruction meta-analysis (53 studies, 166 comparisons): overall **g = 0.36 [0.20, 0.51]** favoring problem-solving-first — **but by grade band: 2nd-5th graders g = −0.09 (favoring instruction-first); 6th-10th graders g = 0.50; undergraduates g = 0.28.** Higher fidelity to Productive Failure principles predicted higher effects for 6th-10th (β = 0.0190, p < .0001) but **not** for the youngest band. (Sinha & Kapur, [Review of Educational Research 91(5) 2021](https://janfasen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Sinha-and-Kapur-PS-I.pdf), high)
  - **The moderator splits at exactly BOW's target boundary (grades 5-6, D6).** The experience-first loop is well-evidenced at 6-8 and **unproven-to-negative at 5**, unless the exploration phase is scaffolded — which the authors explicitly recommend.
- **Direct test with fifth graders:** 228 German fifth graders (ages 10-12), **no significant difference** between Productive Failure and direct instruction on conceptual knowledge; no benefit of collaborative over individual problem solving; students generated only 4.36 solution ideas on average. Named prerequisites: lower working memory/executive function, weaker frustration self-regulation, less developed collaborative dialogue skills. (Mazziotti et al., [npj Science of Learning 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6435728/), high)
- **Exploration-first costs immediate performance and buys transfer** — with an interactive simulation of multivariable causality, instruction-first showed immediate gains in *applying* the concept; both converged; **exploration-first showed better transfer**. (Saba, Kapur & Roll, [Instructional Science 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-025-09726-7), medium — **undergraduate sample; extrapolation to 5-8 is inference**.) *Tell the teacher this so a flat immediate quiz is not read as failure.*
- **Argument skill needs sustained dialogue, not one session.** 13-14-year-olds, **16 sessions** of collaborative goal-based argumentive activity, produced increased counterargument use. (Kuhn & Udell, [Child Development 74(5) 2003](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/dk100/faculty-profile/files/Kuhn_Udell_2003_Thedevelopmentofargumentskills.pdf), high) *A 7-15 minute debrief cannot teach counterargument; it can require one from students who already have the standard behind them.*
- **Epistemological stance shifts across exactly this window.** Absolutism (knowledge is certain, needs no justification) predominates among schoolchildren; multiplism (it's all subjective because people disagree) among middle adolescents; evaluativism adjudicates by argument and evidence. (Kuhn, Cheney & Weinstock, [Cognitive Development 2000](https://www.educationforthinking.org/sites/default/files/pdf/03-02DevelopmentOfEpistemology.pdf), medium)
- **Outcome bias is an adult failure.** Subjects rated an identical pre-operation surgical decision worse when the outcome was bad; replicated in adults through 2025. (Baron & Hershey, [JPSP 1988](https://www.sas.upenn.edu/~baron/papers/outcomebias.pdf), medium)
- **Encouraging:** 10-11-year-olds' decision-making competence (framing problems, decision matrices, risk-perception consistency, confidence calibration) conformed to a **one-factor solution comparable to older populations**. Weak points are self-regulation and processing relevant information — **not the decision itself**. (Weller, Levin & Rose, [JBDM 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/bdm.744), medium)
- **Reading budget.** Median oral reading fluency: Grade 5 fall **121 WCPM**, winter 133, spring 146; Grade 6 fall 132, winter 145, spring 146. **10th percentile: Grade 5 fall 64**, winter 84, spring 102; Grade 6 fall 89, winter 91, spring 91. ([Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017](https://www.readingrockets.org/sites/default/files/2023-08/2017_ORF_NORMS.pdf), high) Silent reading rate gains only **~10-20 wpm per grade**, so 7-8 read roughly 20-40 wpm faster than 5-6 — real but modest ([Hiebert, Samuels & Rasinski 2012](https://textproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Hiebert-samuels-rasinski_Comprehension-based-silent-reading-rates.pdf), medium).
- **No external test to teach to.** NAEP assesses economics **only at grade 12** (2006, 2012); never at grades 4 or 8. ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/economics/whatmeasure.aspx), high) Design freedom is high; the constraint is developmental, not assessment-based.
- **Grade 5 is often not a standalone economics year.** Michigan's grade 5 is "INTEGRATED U.S. HISTORY" with economics embedded and no standalone 5-E expectations; discrete economics expectations exist at grade 3 and return at grade 6 (6-E1.1.1: "Explain how incentives and disincentives in the market economy can change the decision-making process"). ([Michigan K-12 Social Studies](https://www.misocialstudies.org/Standards/5th.pdf), high) *Do not assume prior vocabulary; do assume grade-4 CEE concepts were once covered and need reactivation.*

### 6.4 The design rules (grades 5-6 vs 7-8)

These are **derived** by Researcher 6 from the evidence above, not directly sourced. Confidence noted per rule.

| # | Rule | Grades 5-6 | Grades 7-8 | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Simultaneous variables** | ≤ **2** decision-relevant variables visible per choice (e.g. PRICE and FIT); everything else pre-summarized into one label or icon; prefer a two-column compare | **3-4**, as a scannable table, plus one deliberately **non-causal** variable so the debrief can expose it. Never exceed 4 at either band. | high |
| 2 | **Text density** | Blocking instruction ≤ **40 words** (~25-35 sec at 64 WCPM); cards ≤ 15 words + numbers; no paragraph >3 lines; every number carries its unit inline ("$12M", never "12") | Blocking instruction ≤ **70 words**; cards may carry a ~20-word scouting note; one short context paragraph per phase. **Buy depth with conceptual density, not word count.** | high |
| 3 | **Guidance during the experience phase** | **SCAFFOLDED**: a worked first round run with the class, a visible constraint meter, an in-product prompt naming the trade-off being faced ("spending here means you can't sign a center"), and a mandatory teacher-led contrast with the canonical solution | **OPEN**: face the constraint cold for 1-2 rounds, generate own approaches, teacher-led contrast only at consolidation. **Both bands keep consolidation; only the front half is released.** | **high — highest-stakes rule in the set.** Running the unscaffolded 7-8 loop on a grade-5 class is running the exact condition the meta-analysis found *negative*. |
| 4 | **Choice-space width** | 3-5 discrete options, all legal, none dominated, differences legible at a glance. No compound moves. | 6-10 options plus ≥1 **compound move** (sign-and-trade, cut-and-re-sign, take-back-salary-for-a-pick) and ≥1 dominated-looking option that is correct under a constraint. **Widen options before you widen attributes.** | medium |
| 5 | **Ambiguity and resolution** | Uncertainty during play is fine; the debrief **must converge** on one nameable mechanism and one right-ish reading of the class evidence. **No "both teams were right" endings.** | May end in a **defensible disagreement** (CEE grade-8: evaluation "is subjective") but **must be adjudicated by evidence** — never left at "everyone has their opinion," the multiplist failure mode this age is most prone to. | medium |
| 6 | **Quantitative burden** | Whole dollars in millions, one decimal max ($12.4M); addition and subtraction only; running total always displayed and **never negative** — over-cap is a **BLOCKED action with a plain-language reason**, not a minus sign. No percentages, ratios, or student-computed rates. | Percentages readable at 6; signed balances readable at 6, computable at 7; multi-step percent (escalators, tax multipliers) fair at 7; "dollars per win" efficiency ratios only at 8. | high |
| 7 | **Probability and risk** | Ordinal or physical only — "safe / solid / boom-or-bust," a bar of 10 chips, a colored risk band. Outcomes resolve visibly and the class sees the **spread**, but no student computes an expected value. | Numeric probability legitimate (7.SP.C.5); long-run frequency built from the class's own pooled results (7.SP.C.6); simple EV as a **grade-8 extension, flagged as above-grade** per CEE. | high |
| 8 | **Counterfactual demand** | The product **RUNS and DISPLAYS** the counterfactual ("here's what your team looks like if you'd kept the pick"). The student notices and reacts. | The product **WITHHOLDS** it; the student constructs it ("what would have had to be true for the other choice to win?"), and it is revealed only after they commit an answer. | medium |
| 9 | **Argument burden** | Two moves: "I chose X because Y, and here is the class evidence." | Three moves: the same, **plus** the strongest case against their own decision and why their evidence still beats it. | high |
| 10 | **Vocabulary** | ≤ **2** new economic terms per lesson, each named **only after** students produced the phenomenon, each anchored to a lived moment ("that thing that happened when Maya's table ran out of room — that's scarcity"). Eligible pool: grade-4 CEE terms. | **3-4** terms, may include grade-8 CEE terms (marginal cost/benefit, equilibrium price, relative price, substitute, predictable incentive response); one term may be pre-taught if needed to read the interface. | high |
| 11 | **Decision quality vs outcome** | The **product** does the separating — show the class distribution of outcomes for the **same** decision so students see identical choices produced different results; the teacher says the sentence for them ("good call, bad luck"). | Students **sort** cases into good-decision/bad-outcome and bad-decision/good-outcome themselves, then defend the sort. | medium. **Never rely on either band to make the distinction unprompted — adults fail at it.** |
| 12 | **Peer interaction** | Shared shrinking pool with **visible** remaining supply and **open, sequential** bids | **Hidden simultaneous** bids, requiring reasoning about what rivals will likely do | **low — worth playtesting both variants at 5-6.** |
| 13 | **Debrief scope** | "What did YOUR choice cost YOUR team?" Generalization is **personal** — to the student's own life. | "Who else in the league was made better or worse off, and would the same choice be right for a different team?" Generalization is **societal** — to policy and other people's constraints. | high |
| 14 | **Cross-lesson state** | Carry the roster forward, but the product must **surface the causal link in plain language** at the start of the next lesson ("you signed Player X last time, which is why you only have $6M today"). | Carry state forward **silently**; require students to diagnose why their room is tight. | medium |
| 15 | **Fandom neutrality** | Every decision-relevant fact readable off the card: role, price, one plain-language strength, one plain-language risk. No decision may improve because a student follows the NBA. | Same core on the card, plus an optional second tier (real contract structure, team context, historical comparable) that rewards domain knowledge with **perceived richness — never with a mechanically better outcome**. | **low — the band split is asserted, not sourced.** |

### 7.10 Volatility

Every team currently within ~$3M of a threshold — SAS $37K under tax, ATL $373K under tax, CLE $152K over 1st apron, MIA $1.26M over 1st apron, PHX $1.74M under 2nd apron, ORL $1.7M over 2nd apron, OKC $2.82M under 2nd apron, IND $3.2M over 1st apron, NYK $3.27M under 2nd apron — **is likely to cross that line during the season.** Do not hard-code threshold proximity as a lesson beat without a refresh mechanism.

Also unresolved and live: whether the **Lakers' $12.5B sale** has Board of Governors approval (reporting pointed to a September 2026 BOG meeting in New York; not confirmed as of 2026-09-03), and the **13 RSN-affected teams'** actual 2026-27 local media revenue (the "60% recovery" figure came from unnamed sources; dissolution agreements were not finalized).

### 7.11 Pedagogy-specific gaps

- **Schema mismatch:** Researcher 6's `sourceTier` values (`standards-official`, `peer-reviewed`, `gov-official`, `design-rule`) were **not drawn from the stated vocabulary.** Any downstream tooling that parses tiers will choke.
- **CEE standards are 16 years old** (2010, 2nd edition, never revised). A 2026 lesson is being calibrated against a 2010 document.
- **Common Core is no longer branded as such in several states** (Texas, Virginia, Florida, Indiana, Oklahoma). The grade-placement facts hold for CCSS-aligned states; a **NY Next Generation Learning Standards check is owed** given the Ramaz target.
- **The Sinha & Kapur grade-band CI is internally inconsistent as printed** — Table 4 gives the 2nd-5th estimate as g = −0.09 with CI [−0.92, −0.16], an interval that does not contain the point estimate. **Direction well-supported, magnitude uncertain.**
- **Developmental research on outcome bias specifically in 10-14-year-olds is thin.** Baron & Hershey is an adult study; the developmental literature found was about optimism bias and moral judgment, not decision-quality evaluation. Design Rule 11 is built on the safe assumption, not a measured age threshold.
- **Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017 covers grades 1-6 only.** The 7-8 text budget (~70 words) is **extrapolated**, not measured.
- **No peer-reviewed evidence found for the "name the term only after the experience" rule at middle-school level specifically** — sources returned were practitioner/curriculum (explore-before-explain, 5E). Defensible by inheritance from productive failure, but weaker than the rest of the set.
- **Design Rules 12 (bidding transparency) and 15 (fandom two-tier cards) have no source at all.** Both are inferences from adjacent findings. **Resolve by playtest, not by literature.**

### 7.12 Product decisions that have not been made

Flagged by researchers, not resolvable by research:

1. **Roster salary or cap hit including holds?** The two definitions differ by up to $52M on a single team. Pick one and apply it uniformly.
2. **How many of the five thresholds does a grades 5-8 lesson carry?** The real system has floor / cap / tax / 1st apron / 2nd apron plus exceptions, holds, TPEs and Bird rights. A grades 5-8 lesson can probably carry **three lines at most.** Which two get dropped, and what misconception that creates, is an unmade design decision **that should be logged in PRODUCT_DECISIONS.md** (per CLAUDE.md §3: record what changed, why, and the misconception risk).
3. **Does the Jokic case teach one number or the range?**
4. **Draft-pick protections are almost entirely unmapped.** Pick ownership was captured only at a summary level; there is **no verified per-year, per-team ledger of owed picks, swap rights, and protections.** Protections matter enormously for expected value — a top-4-protected pick is a genuinely different asset from an unprotected one. **A dedicated pass on a pick-tracker source is needed before Module 4.**
5. **Dead money is only partially mapped.** Firm figures exist for PHX ($24.20M), MIL ($21.98M), MEM ($21.91M), ORL ($8.00M), DEN ($2.00M), MIN ($2.06M), CLE ($0.42M). **The remaining ~23 teams were never checked** and several almost certainly carry some. HoopsHype does not separate dead money from active salary at all — it appears inline (Beal's $19.38M shows as a Suns roster line).
6. **No research exists on the "where the money comes from" half at team level for 2026-27** — R2's brief was scoped to transaction rules; R3 covered league aggregates but could not obtain a per-team revenue breakdown.
7. **Fandom-accessibility check is owed.** Every teaching case asserts "no basketball knowledge required," but **that claim has not been tested against a non-fan reader.** Most at risk: the Gobert trade (why a defensive center was worth that) and the SGA supermax (why "supermax" exists as a category).
8. **Rights escalation.** Player names, salaries, dates, transaction terms and cap rules are public facts and safe. **Team logos, marks, arena imagery, and player photography are not**, and per CLAUDE.md §3 this needs a founder call before any visual treatment is designed around real franchises. This dossier deliberately contains none.

## 8. WHAT IS SAFE TO PRINT

### 8.1 Safe — render to students today

These are official-NBA-sourced or multiply-corroborated, and no researcher dissented.

1. **The five 2026-27 thresholds:** cap **$164,961,000**, floor **$148,465,000**, tax **$200,428,000**, first apron **$209,015,000**, second apron **$221,686,000**.
2. **Floor = 90% of the cap, exactly.** Students can compute it themselves and get a real league rule.
3. **Tax = 121.5% of the cap.**
4. **The 2025-26 thresholds** for a year-over-year comparison: cap $154,647,000, tax $187,895,000, floor $139,182,000, aprons $195,945,000 / $207,824,000.
5. **The cap grew 6.7% in 2026-27 and exactly 10.0% in 2025-26** (the CBA ceiling), against **~34% in 2016-17** when no ceiling existed.
6. **The three max tiers:** 25% / 30% / 35% of cap = **$41,240,250 / $49,488,300 / $57,736,350**. One 35% max is over a third of the budget.
7. **The three mid-level exceptions:** $15,044,000 / $9,366,000 / $6,064,000 — your tool shrinks as you spend more.
8. **Roster limits:** 15 standard contracts max, 3 two-way, 21 in the offseason.
9. **Rookie scale is a fixed price ladder by draft slot:** No. 1 pick $14,748,000, No. 30 pick $2,926,800 — a ~5× gap set by slot, not negotiation, with years 3 and 4 as team options.
10. **Re-signing your own player allows 5 years / 8% raises; signing elsewhere allows 4 years / 5%.** The rules pay players to stay.
11. **Players receive 51% of Basketball Related Income** (band: never below 49%, never above 51%).
12. **The national media deal: 11 years, ~$76 billion, 2025-26 through 2035-36, with Disney, NBCUniversal and Amazon.**
13. **Every team receives the identical national TV check: $143 million in 2025-26** (up from $103M in 2024-25).
14. **Gate and arena operations were $3.4B in 2024-25, about 28% of league revenue** — not most of it.
15. **Team revenues ranged from $833M (Golden State) to $301M (Memphis) in 2024-25**, while both got the same national check.
16. **The NBA approved the Celtics sale at a valuation above $6.1 billion** (official NBA source).
17. **Seven teams paid luxury tax in 2025-26, totalling $223.1 million**, and seven of fourteen projected taxpayers ducked it at the trade deadline.
18. **Joe Lacob's quote** ("Plan 1, or 1A, is that we'd like to be out of the tax… two years out of the next four to get this repeater thing off our books") and **Nico Harrison's quote** on the Dončić trade — both on the record.
19. **Every teaching case in §5 as narrative** — names, dates, and transaction terms. These are public facts, well-sourced, and each is attached to a specific date and URL.
20. **The 2018 Noah stretch arithmetic:** $19.3M becomes $6.4M × 3 seasons. The total does not shrink, it spreads.
21. **Milwaukee pays $21,311,053 per year to Damian Lillard, who plays for another team, through 2030-31** — corroborated by the official NBA waiver announcement plus the current cap sheet.
