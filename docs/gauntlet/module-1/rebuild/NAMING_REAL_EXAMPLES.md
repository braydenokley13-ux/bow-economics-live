## 1 — SCARCITY (`l1.ts:564`, both bands, written for 5–6)

**Instantiates** *N desks needed a big, the board had M, everybody saw the same board* — from the
buyers' side: one league-wide list, almost everyone shopping it with the same small amount.

> **`real`:** "In the summer of 2026 only three NBA teams — Brooklyn, Chicago and the Lakers — had
> spending room under the cap. Every other club in the league was shopping the same list of free
> agents, and the rules capped what most of them could offer a player who was not already theirs at
> about $15 million a year. Same board for thirty teams, and not enough of it to go round."

**7–8 DEPTH LAYER (do not read at 5–6).** The $15,044,000 is the non-taxpayer mid-level exception; a
club over the first apron ($209,015,000) may offer only the taxpayer version, $6,064,000, and one over
the second apron ($221,686,000) may not use it at all — so the scarce thing is not only players but the
*permission* to bid, and the clubs with least permission are usually those that already spent most.

**WATCH FOR (teacher).** *"So the rich teams just buy everyone."* The opposite is what the sheet
shows: the clubs with money to spend were three, and two of them were not contenders. Second
misreading: *"scarcity means the league is poor."* Scarcity is not poverty — the league had never had
more money; there was still only one of each player.

**Source / date.** Hoops Rumors, *How Teams Are Using 2026/27 Mid-Level Exceptions*
(`https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/07/how-teams-are-using-2026-27-mid-level-exceptions.html`),
**read in full 2026-09-04** — page states the exception values and lists exactly three clubs under
**Room Exception** (Brooklyn $0, Chicago $0, Lakers $9,366,000 on Collin Sexton), and says in its own
prose *"now that most of the NBA's teams have used up their cap space."* **VERIFIED THIS SESSION**
for the values and the three-club list. **INFERRED:** that those three are the *only* clubs that
operated with cap room — the page's structure and its own sentence support it; no source says the
number "three" out loud. Corroborating repo row: `world.ts` `LINE`/`TOOL` (NTMLE $15,044,000, pr.nba.com,
asOf 2026-06-30) and `NBA_FINANCIAL_TRUTH.md` §1.

## 2 — OPPORTUNITY COST (`l1.ts:599`, both bands, written for 5–6)

**Instantiates** *"Nobody took them away. No rival outbid it. Its own signing did that."*

> **`real`:** "On July 4, 2016, Kevin Durant chose Golden State. To fit his contract under the cap,
> the Warriors traded Andrew Bogut to Dallas and let five of their own free agents go, Harrison
> Barnes among them. Nobody made them do it and nobody outbid them — signing one player is what made
> keeping the others impossible."

**7–8 DEPTH LAYER (do not read at 5–6).** The rule underneath it is still live: to spend cap room a
club must first **renounce** its own free agents, which permanently destroys the Bird rights that
would have let it re-sign them over the cap (`NBA_FINANCIAL_TRUTH.md` §2.5 — "the cleanest
opportunity cost in the entire CBA"). Room is never free; it is bought with the players you already
had.

**WATCH FOR (teacher).** Guaranteed: *"They won the title, so it didn't cost them anything."* The cost
was paid on July 4 and did not depend on what happened in June — this is the decision/outcome split
the settle screen makes elsewhere. Second misreading: *"Barnes was bad, that's why he left."* Dallas
signed him immediately. He was not taken; he was let go, and the reason was arithmetic.

**Source / date.** Wikipedia, *2016–17 Golden State Warriors season*, "Preseason": Durant announced
July 4; "To clear cap space for Durant, the Warriors traded Andrew Bogut to the Dallas Mavericks and
didn't match offers for five of their out of contract free agents, losing Harrison Barnes to the
Dallas Mavericks, Brandon Rush to the Minnesota Timberwolves, Leandro Barbosa to the Phoenix Suns,
Festus Ezeli to the Portland Trail Blazers…" **Read 2026-09-04 — VERIFIED THIS SESSION, single source
(encyclopedia tier);** one reporting-tier corroboration owed before release. NOT VERIFIED and
excluded: Bogut's role, Barnes' Finals starts, contract values.

## 3 — COMPETITION SETS PRICE (`l1.ts:644`, 7–8 only)

**Instantiates** *"He never raised his price. The other desks in this room raised it."*

> **`real`:** "On July 7, 2025, Myles Turner signed with Milwaukee for four years and $108.9 million.
> He had spent his whole career in Indiana and Indiana did not keep him. The day before, Milwaukee had
> waived Damian Lillard and taken on the $103 million still owed to him — that is what a second
> interested club was willing to do, and a second interested club is what set Turner's price."

**7–8 DEPTH LAYER.** The market-wide version: the salary cap went from $70.000 million in 2015-16 to
$94.143 million in 2016-17 in one summer. Nothing about the players changed; every club's ability to
bid changed at once, and Mike Conley — who had never been an All-Star — signed the largest contract
in NBA history to that date, five years and $153 million. Prices are made by bidders, not by sellers.

**WATCH FOR (teacher).** *"So Turner is worth $108.9 million"* — no. The number measures what the
second-most-eager club would pay, not the player. And *"Indiana was cheap / made a mistake"*: Indiana
faced a different bill for the same player (Week 3's subjective value, arriving early). Do not
adjudicate who was right; the point is that neither of them, and not Turner, chose the number.

**Source / date.** Wikipedia, *Myles Turner* ("On July 7, 2025, Turner signed a four-year, $108.9
million contract with the Milwaukee Bucks", citing NBA.com 2025-07-07 and Hoops Rumors 2025-07-08)
and *Damian Lillard* ("On July 6, 2025, the Bucks announced they had waived Lillard, stretching the
remaining $103 million left on his deal"); *Mike Conley Jr.* (July 2016, five years/$153 million,
"the greatest contract by total value in NBA history at the time", citing ESPN 2016-07-02 and NBA.com
2016-07-14); *NBA salary cap* (cap by year: 15-16 $70.000M, 16-17 $94.143M). **All read 2026-09-04 —
VERIFIED THIS SESSION.** Corroborating repo row: `NBA_FINANCIAL_TRUTH.md` §4 Milwaukee — waived
Lillard carried at $21,311,053 a year through 2030-31 (salaryswish, asOf 2026-06-30). **INFERRED, and
labelled as such:** that the second bidder is what raised the number. No source states the causal
claim; the facts state that two clubs wanted him and the one that paid more got him.

## 4 — RATIONING (`l3.ts:1050`, both bands, written for 5–6)

**Instantiates** the inbox cap of three — a count rule, not a price, decides who gets in.

> **`real`:** "NBA rules let a club keep no more than 15 players once the season starts. On September
> 3, 2026 — three weeks before camp — Memphis had 19 players under contract, and three other clubs
> were also over the limit. No amount of money buys a sixteenth spot, so every one of them had to cut
> somebody who had done nothing wrong."

**7–8 DEPTH LAYER.** Rationing runs the other way too: the same rulebook sets a 14-man *minimum*, and
on that same day Sacramento had twelve players and New York thirteen — clubs sitting over the tax line
and required to add people they would rather not pay. And the scarcest thing in the sport, the first
pick of a draft, is not sold at all: it is drawn for. Price is one rationing device among several,
and leagues pick which one on purpose.

**WATCH FOR (teacher).** *"Then the rule is unfair."* Ask what the alternative allocates by — money,
queue, lottery, merit, need — and who wins under each. Rationing is not the exception to a market;
every scarce thing is rationed by something, and the interesting question is by *what*. Second
misreading: *"a cut player is a bad player."* The 16th man is cut by arithmetic, not by evaluation.

**Source / date.** Roster limits (15 standard, 3 two-way, 14 minimum, 21 in the offseason):
`NBA_FINANCIAL_TRUTH.md` §8.1 #8, cbaguide.com/eligibility/rosters, asOf 2026-06-30 — **REPO-SOURCED**,
high confidence. September counts (Memphis 19, Milwaukee 17, Charlotte 17, Detroit 16; Sacramento 12,
New York 13): `FRANCHISE_STATES.md` §4.2, SalarySwish, **asOf 2026-09-03 — REPO-SOURCED**. Draft-lottery
line: **NOT VERIFIED this session**; only that Wembanyama went first overall to San Antonio on 22 June
2023 is (Wikipedia, 2026-09-04). Past tense on purpose: a dated snapshot survives camp cuts.

## 5 — ROOM (CONSTRAINT) (`l3.ts:1062`, 7–8 only)

**Instantiates** a wall drawn in July binding a February trade.

> **`real`:** "On July 1 and July 3, 2026, Utah agreed two-year deals with Jaxson Hayes and Josh
> Okogie at $6 million a year each. Together those two signings spent more than the taxpayer part of
> Utah's mid-level exception, and the rule attached to that spending hard-caps a club at the first
> apron for the rest of the league year — the same wall is still standing at February's trade
> deadline. Nobody imposed that line on Utah. Utah drew it, in July, by signing."

**7–8 DEPTH LAYER.** The wall does not only forbid payroll; it forbids *transactions*. A club that has
used the taxpayer mid-level may not do any of the trade rows in the Transaction Restrictions Table for
the remainder of that salary-cap year (CBA Art. VII §2(e)(2)(iii)(B)), and an offseason trade can
hard-cap a club through the *following* season's deadline (§2(e)(2)(ii)). Sequencing, not just size:
in July 2026 Phoenix could not sign Luke Kennard at all until the Miles Bridges trade completed,
because it needed $29 million of salary to leave before $22.8 million arrived.

**WATCH FOR (teacher).** *"The league punished Utah."* Never let the wall be called a penalty
(`W3_THE_DEADLINE_SPEC.md` §R3, do-not-render #20) — every hard cap in the NBA is self-inflicted, and
that is the whole point of the card. Second misreading: *"then just don't sign anyone in July."* Utah
got two rotation players for it; the constraint is the price of the thing it bought, not a mistake.

**Source / date.** Signing dates and terms: `FREE_AGENT_BOARD_RESEARCH.md` ADDENDUM (2026-09-04),
items 2 and 3, from Hoops Rumors' Jazz reports and the 2026/27 mid-level tracker — **REPO-SOURCED,
and the tracker page itself was re-opened 2026-09-04** (Utah: "Used $6,000,000 (Jaxson Hayes);
$6,000,000 (Josh Okogie)"; taxpayer MLE $6,064,000; first apron $209,015,000): **VERIFIED THIS
SESSION.** The Hoops Rumors line "using more than the taxpayer portion of the MLE will hard-cap the
Jazz at the first tax apron" is quoted in the repo addendum — **REPO-SOURCED.** Rule text
(§2(e)(2)(ii)–(iii), §2(e)(4)): `NBA_TRADE_TRUTH.md` §2.1–2.2, primary-text OBSERVED — **REPO-SOURCED.**
Phoenix/Kennard sequencing: `NBA_TRADE_TRUTH.md` §7 (Hoops Rumors 2026-07-10) — **REPO-SOURCED.**
League-wide scale (22 of 30 hard-capped, 2026-07-10) is already spent on L1's AN INSTITUTION card;

**The NBA figure asked for is NOT VERIFIED.** Per-club *road* attendance is published (ESPN's season

**Safest true sentence a teacher can read aloud (no unverified number):** *"When a club with a big
draw plays away, the extra tickets are sold in somebody else's building — and the home club keeps
that gate. The visiting club is not paid for the crowd it brought."* Gate as a local revenue stream
belonging to the home club: `module-2/W4_BILL_RESEARCH.md` §4 — **REPO-SOURCED.**

**If the card needs a dated figure now, the sourced one is the WNBA's, and it is behavioural, which is
stronger than a percentage:** in 2024 six clubs moved Indiana Fever games out of their own arenas and
into bigger buildings to sell more tickets (Front Office Sports; repo `module-2/GATE_L1_SR.md` F8,
verifiedAsOf **2026-08-31** — REPO-SOURCED). One instance: Washington hosted Indiana at Capital One
Arena on September 19, 2024 and drew **20,711** (Wikipedia *2024 Indiana Fever season* game log, read
2026-09-04 — **VERIFIED THIS SESSION, single source**). The Fever were paid nothing out of that gate.
Cautions: (a) that game-log column mixes announced attendance with arena capacity — three Dallas
rows repeat 6,251 exactly — so trust only figures that differ between visits, as Washington's do
(20,333 on June 7, 20,711 on September 19); (b) the claim that Washington *moved* the game is
**INFERRED** from the venue, not sourced.

**Caution on the card's wording.** *"Not one of those buildings paid him or the Cavaliers for it"* is
right about the gate and wrong if a student hears "no money ever moves between NBA clubs": the league
redistributes roughly $400 million a year in revenue sharing (`W4_BILL_RESEARCH.md`, formula
confidential — never print a percentage). Say *"nobody paid the visiting club for the crowd it drew."*
