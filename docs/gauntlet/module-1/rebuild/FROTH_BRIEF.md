# Shared brief for every Module 1 direction author (paste into each froth agent)

You are designing MODULE 1 of BOW Economics Track 101 — "Sports Business I" — from the
ground up. Three ~50-60 minute live classes, run for real by a teacher, in a real classroom,
with real students on Chromebooks. The existing Module 1 is being discarded as a product
direction; nothing survives because it exists.

## The founder's thesis for Module 1

Module 1 opens with a seductive FALSE INTUITION —

    "My job is to build the best basketball team possible."

— and progressively breaks it, until the student arrives at:

    "Running an NBA franchise means allocating scarce resources across competing goals
     under uncertainty."

The student inherits a REAL NBA franchise and discovers that every exciting basketball
decision has consequences elsewhere.

## Non-negotiables

1. **The actual NBA.** Real franchises, real markets, real buildings, real cap rules,
   real players where pedagogically useful, real historical situations in synthesis.
   Never an invented league. Never "Team 4". Never "San Antonio Summit".
   BUT: no implication of NBA endorsement; no logos, jersey art, photographs, broadcast
   graphics, or proprietary datasets. Clubs appear as typographic wordmarks. The
   EXPERIENCE is NBA-authentic; the VISUAL SYSTEM is distinctly BOW.
2. **Reality is never a fandom test.** A student who has never watched a game must fully
   succeed. A fan may perceive extra strategic depth. Accessible core + optional depth.
3. **Economics lives in the choices**, not in a meter that blocks purchases.
4. **No decision-card menus.** A row of four cards titled "Invest in Roster / Shape Our
   Identity / Grow the Brand / Build the Front Office" is exactly what is forbidden. The
   student must OPERATE something, not pick from abstractions.
5. **No XP, levels, badges, leaderboards, progression, trophies, "champion"** (D4).
   The class reveal is the reward system.
6. **Outcome ≠ decision quality.** The model must permit good reasoning + bad outcome and
   weak reasoning + good outcome where economically appropriate. Not deterministic moral
   feedback.
7. **Two grade bands from birth**: grades 5-6 and grades 7-8, separate classes, ONE shared
   NBA world / economic engine / consequence engine / classroom runtime / franchise state,
   with a GRADE PROFILE selecting depth. Not two products. 7-8 is NOT 5-6 with harder
   arithmetic.
8. **Strong persistence, selective mechanical carry.** L1's decisions must constrain L2;
   L1+L2 must constrain L3. Carry franchise identity, important commitments, selected
   resources, remembered strategies. Do NOT carry every number. Avoid state sludge.
9. **Uncertainty must become interpretable afterward, never a shrug.**
10. **Every experience must eventually connect**: experienced moment → class result →
    real NBA example → formal economic term → outside-sports generalization.

## The intellectual demands, per band

- **Grades 5-6:** identify the tradeoff, explain the decision, interpret the consequence,
  adapt. Stronger visual scaffolding, shorter reading, fewer simultaneous variables,
  bounded choice spaces, concrete consequences, guided reasoning, teacher prompts that
  NAME the economics after it is experienced.
- **Grades 7-8:** defend a strategy using evidence, compare realistic alternatives, reason
  under uncertainty, explain what would make the original decision good even if the outcome
  was bad. More information, less guidance, wider decision spaces, more ambiguity,
  competing evidence, counterfactuals, risk analysis, quantitative interpretation.

## The three-lesson shape (directional, not binding)

- **L1** — you inherit the franchise; scarcity is unavoidable immediately; saying YES to one
  strategy means saying NO somewhere else.
- **L2** — the consequences arrive; a changed condition makes the earlier mental model
  insufficient; prior commitments matter; adapt rather than restart.
- **L3** — the boardroom test; an integrated high-stakes franchise decision, defended,
  compared against alternatives, confronting the difference between best outcome, best-looking
  roster, best financial result, and best decision given information available at the time.

You may propose a different shape if it is genuinely stronger. Three lessons is fixed.

## Four spectacle beats per lesson

Each lesson should earn exactly four: SIGNATURE DECISION · CONSEQUENCE · CLASS REVEAL ·
INTELLECTUAL REVEAL. Everything else is restrained.

## The class reveal must NOT produce one fake winner

Rank by ONE universal franchise score only if the economics truly justifies that objective —
it almost certainly does not. The founder wants CONFLICTING DEFINITIONS OF SUCCESS on the
board simultaneously (strongest roster / most future flexibility / most balanced / highest
upside / lowest downside / best financial position / best decision quality given initial
conditions) so the class ARGUES. A student may have the best result while another made the
most defensible decision.

## The classroom substrate you are designing against (do not redesign it)

Three surfaces, one session, all already built and browser-proven:
- `/play` — student device, private, pairs on one device by default. Never shows another
  seat's data.
- `/teach` — the teacher's control room, gated by a per-session bearer key. Already carries:
  a per-phase DIRECTOR script (NOW / ASK / DON'T EXPLAIN YET / TRIGGER / WATCH FOR / TIME CUT),
  THE ROOM (live private read of spread/shape/movement on the dial this lesson turns on),
  THE DESKS (who is sitting where, what they are doing, who to walk to), a live inert mirror
  of the projector, a class clock on the server's clock, per-desk device-health buckets,
  a cold REHEARSAL walk with stand-in desks, bounded undo/restore, and a link picker for
  seeding the next lesson from the previous session.
- `/board` — the projector, public, never handed a seat identity.

The runtime gives you free: teacher-paced phases from a canonical vocabulary
(LOBBY HOOK PLAY REVEAL CONSEQUENCE ADAPT COUNTERFACTUAL ARGUE SYNTHESIS COMPLETE — pick an
ordered subset); a TIME CUT round contract (FINAL CALL + CLOSE NOW, server-clocked, with a
per-desk economically honest fallback the MODULE names); an opaque cross-session SEED for
carry; snapshot persistence surviving refresh/rejoin/mid-class restart; exactly-once action
application with an explicit refusal reason; a per-desk "while you were away" recap the
module writes in its own words; teacher-staged reveal beats where the student device is
structurally forbidden from reading ahead of the projector.

## The economic truth harness you must design against

Every important rendered quantitative claim must be traceable to a researched source, an
explicit model rule, computed state, or a registered simplification. Narrative prose may
never outrun the model. If the UI says "this uses most of your flexibility", the model must
support it. If it says "you gave up future options", a real future option must be constrained.
If it says "safer", "safer" must be defined and computed.

## Your output

Design ONE coherent module direction. Be specific enough to build from and specific enough
to be attacked. Vague directions lose.
