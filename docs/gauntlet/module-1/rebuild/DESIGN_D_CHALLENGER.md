## 1. THE ONE SENTENCE

> **You and every other desk are one league under one rule, the same five lines are drawn across
> all of you in the same place, and the only club you can build is the one your position lets you
> build while everybody else is building theirs.**

**Teacher's repeatable version, twelve words:**
*"Same rule for everyone. It does something different to each of you."*

**The sentence a student says at the end of L1, unprompted, which is the design target:**
*"Wait — they couldn't even offer that much?"*

---

## 2. THE CORE FANTASY

**The ten-year-old leans in because the room is one league and they can watch it empty.**

There are seven real people on the board. Every one of them will end up at exactly one desk. When
another desk signs Myles Turner, his card leaves the board — for everyone, forever, and the desk
that spent its day offering on him has nothing to show for the day. That is not a meter going down.
That is a person walking out of the room while you were deciding. Ten-year-olds understand this
instantly because it is musical chairs, and musical chairs is a genuine economic mechanism: a
shared, conserved, shrinking pool with permanent loss.

**The thirteen-year-old leans in because the rule is visibly unfair and the unfairness is legible.**

The board shows five lines: floor **$148,465,000**, cap **$164,961,000**, tax **$200,428,000**,
first apron **$209,015,000**, second apron **$221,686,000** (§8.1 #1, official-nba, as of
2026-06-30). Every desk is measured against the *identical* five numbers. And Detroit at
**$136,252,755** and Denver at **$227,422,947** (§4.1 #6, #3, cap-database, medium, as of
2026-09-03) are not playing the same game at all. Detroit cannot legally spend little enough;
Denver cannot legally spend at all. Same rule. Opposite prison. A thirteen-year-old wants to argue
about that within ninety seconds, and the argument is the lesson.

**Both bands lean in on the same beat, and it is the beat this direction is built to produce:**
the moment a desk discovers *what its neighbour was allowed to offer*. Not "who is winning" — there
is no winning — but **"how come they could only bid $6 million?"** That question has an answer, the
answer is an institution, and the institution is the module's title.

**The fantasy is not GM-as-genius and it is not GM-as-inheritor. It is GM-as-one-of-thirty.** You
are inside a system that was designed on purpose, by people, to do something specific to you, and
you find out what it was designed to do by being on the receiving end of it while twelve other
desks are on the receiving end of a different part of it.

## 3. THE FALSE INTUITION AND HOW IT BREAKS

The module opens on the founder's named intuition:

> **"My job is to build the best basketball team possible."**

It is broken **twice in Lesson 1, in opposite directions, inside four minutes**, so it cannot
re-form as "spend well" or as "spend carefully." Then it is broken a third time in L2 as a
structural matter, so it cannot re-form as "be clever."

### Break 1 — L1, CONSEQUENCE, ~minute 31. The desk that spent everything.

Signing day 3 resolves. The desk that chased Myles Turner (real: **4 years / $107M, July 2025**,
§5.8, espn, high) got him at **$26.75M a year for four years**, and in doing so used its
non-taxpayer mid-level exception — **$15,044,000** (§8.1 #7) — plus room. Its BIG job is closed
through year 4. Its WING job is open, it has no tool left that reaches a wing, and it has drawn a
wall at **$209,015,000** it may not cross for the rest of the league year (§2.3, cbaguide high,
corroborated by hoopsrumors as of 2026-07-10).

Two desks down, a club closed the same BIG job with **Deandre Ayton at $8.3M for two years**
(real: reported 2 yrs / $16.6M with the Lakers, 2025, §5.5, team-official/medium), still holds its
mid-level exception, and closed its WING job as well.

The board prints the two rows next to each other under the heading **CHEAPEST JOB CLOSED**. Nobody
scored anything. Nobody won. The room can see that the expensive club is not the better club, and
it can also see that the cheap club's BIG job re-opens in two years and the expensive club's does
not.

*Replaces:* **best is not a thing you can point at. There are different clubs.**

### Break 2 — L1, CONSEQUENCE, ~minute 33, immediately after. The desk that spent nothing.

The **FLOOR TABLE** goes up. Detroit's committed salary is **$136,252,755** (§4.1 #6, §4.4). The
floor is **$148,465,000** — exactly 90% of the cap, and a real league rule a fifth grader can check
by subtraction (§8.1 #2). Detroit is **$12,212,245 short**, and under the 2023 CBA that shortfall
is paid out **to players league-wide, pro rata by salary — not to Detroit's own roster** (§2.1,
hoopsrumors, medium).

The pair that held everything back, kept every tool, and felt clever about it pays twelve million
dollars and receives **nothing at all**. Not a player. Not a pick. Not a tool. The money leaves and
goes to players on other teams.

*Replaces:* **"careful with money" is not a strategy either. Underspending is illegal here, and it
is the only move in the lesson that buys literally nothing.**

This is the beat this direction exists for. It kills **FL6** ("spending is bad, saving is good") in
the first lesson, with a real rule, before the student has had time to build the moral frame.

### Break 3 — L2, SIGNATURE DECISION, ~minute 22. "You are not allowed."

February. Your club needs one thing and there is one obvious way to get it: combine two mid-sized
contracts on your books into one trade. The desk that used the **non-taxpayer mid-level exception**
back in July is refused, and the refusal names the July decision by date: *"You drew a wall at
$209,015,000 on signing day 2 when you signed Turner with the mid-level. This trade would put you
at $211.4M. It is refused."*

Nobody outbid them. Nothing went wrong. **They did it to themselves, in a previous lesson, with a
move that was correct at the time.**

*Replaces:* **your job is to keep the ability to act — and the thing that takes it away is usually
your own earlier good idea.**

### The arrival point

> **"Running an NBA franchise means allocating scarce resources across competing goals under
> uncertainty."**

Walked in three steps the room produces itself:

- **L1** — *the line is the same for everyone and it does something different to each of us.*
- **L2** — *what I did in July decides what I am allowed to do in February, and the only fix is a
  desk whose problem is the opposite of mine.*
- **L3** — *the line itself is a decision somebody made, it can move, and when it moves everybody
  gets richer at once — which is when prices stop meaning what they meant.*

### LESSON 1 — **THE WINDOW**

*July. One league, one board, three signing days. The floor pushes you up and the cap holds you
down and everyone else is reaching for the same seven people.*

#### 4.1.1 The signature mechanic

**On screen (/play).** One column, no scrolling.

1. **THE LADDER** — a persistent vertical scale, top of screen, never scrolls away. Five ruled
   lines at their real dollar values. Your club's committed salary is a filled bar rising from the
   bottom, with its dollar figure at the top of the bar. Two numbers, always visible: **the distance
   to the floor above you** or **the distance to the cap above you**, whichever is the live one.
2. **YOUR JOBS** — two named open jobs (`A BIG`, `A WING`), each an empty chair glyph with the
   words under it. When one closes, the chair fills with the player's name and the words
   *"covered through 2029-30."*
3. **THE BOARD** — the seven free agents, as cards. Each card carries exactly four things:
   **role**, **what he is asking (annual dollars)**, **one plain-language strength**, **one
   plain-language risk**. **There is no rating, no grade, no stars and no number a student can
   sort on.** A card whose ask is beyond your legal reach is dimmed with a one-line reason
   (*"the most you can legally offer anyone is $6,064,000"*).
4. **THE OFFER COMPOSER** — appears when you tap a card. Two controls at 5–6 (who, how much); three
   at 7–8 (who, which tool, how much). Above the commit button: **THE FORGONE PANEL**, live —
   *"If you sign him at $18.0M: you can no longer reach Turner ($26.8M), Brown ($22.5M) or
   Caldwell-Pope ($22.0M). You will have one roster slot and $2.4M left."* Names, not categories.
   Recomputed on every keystroke, **frozen at commit** (`foregoneAtLock`).
5. **THE WALL WARNING** — when the composed offer would use the non-taxpayer mid-level exception or
   the bi-annual exception, one sentence in the commit button's own frame, before commit:
   *"Using this draws a wall at $209,015,000. You may not cross it for the rest of this league year
   — including in February."*

**What the student manipulates.** One offer per signing day: a player, a tool (7–8) or an
auto-selected tool (5–6), and a dollar amount. Editable freely until commit; commit is per-day and
irreversible for that day. You may also **pass** — passing is a real choice with a real cost (the
day is gone and the board keeps emptying).

**What commits.** `offer:{ playerId, tool, annual }`, one per desk per day, replaceable until the
day closes.

**What the server computes, in order, deterministically:**

1. Collect all committed offers for the day across all live desks.
2. For each player with ≥1 offer, award to the highest `annual`.
   **Tie-breaks, in model, never by arrival time** (contract §8 #9; `tradeDeadline.ts:783` is the
   defect this replaces):
   a. **the incumbent's advantage** — a club re-signing its own player may offer 5 years / 8%
      raises where everyone else may offer 4 years / 5% (§8.1 #10, cba, high), so at equal annual
      dollars the incumbent's *total* is larger and the player takes it;
   b. **remaining room after signing** — the player takes the club that can still build around him;
   c. **club id order**, documented, asserted by property test to be reachable in under 1% of
      resolutions and never load-bearing.
3. Apply the winning signing: `committed += annual`; `slots += 1`; the tool is consumed; a wall is
   drawn if the tool draws one; the player's future-year books are written for his real term.
4. **The player leaves the board permanently.** Every losing desk's day is spent and its books are
   unchanged (`draftDay.ts:286-300`'s permanent-loss shape, which the platform audit calls a
   repair worth keeping).
5. Publish **only the consummated deal** — player, club, price. **Losing offers and the amounts
   inside them stay private forever, before and after any reveal** (`tradeDeadline.ts:1207`'s
   honest publication rule). This is also exactly what a real market discloses.

**At window close (after day 3), the same arithmetic that validates an ordinary signing:**

- if `slots < 12`, `committed += (12 − slots) × $1,357,763` — the real anti-gaming backstop; you
  cannot manufacture room by emptying the roster (§2.5, cba, high);
- if `slots < 14`, minimum contracts are auto-signed to 14 and charged (§2.10, cbaguide, medium);
- if `committed < $148,465,000`, the club pays `$148,465,000 − committed` and **the money goes to
  players league-wide, not to this roster** (§2.1).

#### 4.1.2 The exact economic model

**Constants — all sourced (§1.2, §8.1, official-nba, as of 2026-06-30 unless noted):**

```
CAP        = 164,961,000
FLOOR      = 148,465,000          (= 0.90 × CAP, exactly)
TAX        = 200,428,000          (= 1.215 × CAP)
APRON_1    = 209,015,000
APRON_2    = 221,686,000
NTMLE      =  15,044,000   ≤4 yrs, 5% raises   → draws a wall at APRON_1
ROOM_MLE   =   9,366,000   ≤3 yrs
TAX_MLE    =   6,064,000   ≤2 yrs
BAE        =   5,477,000   ≤2 yrs               → draws a wall at APRON_1
                                                 (cap-database, high; not in the official release, §7.4)
MIN_TEAM   =   2,449,421   the team-cost cap on a 3+-yr vet minimum, 1 yr (§1.2)
ROOKIE_MIN =   1,357,763   the empty-slot backstop charge (§1.2)
SLOT_MAX   = 15 ; SLOT_MIN = 14 ; BACKSTOP_AT = 12   (§2.10)
```

**Legality — one predicate, used by every path including repairs (contract §8 #17):**

```
reach(club) = the largest annual the club may legally offer =
  max over available tools t of  ceiling(t, club)

ceiling(ROOM,     c) = max(0, CAP − c.committed)      available while c.committed < CAP
ceiling(NTMLE,    c) = NTMLE                          available if unspent AND c is not over APRON_1
ceiling(ROOM_MLE, c) = ROOM_MLE                       available only AFTER a ROOM signing
ceiling(TAX_MLE,  c) = TAX_MLE                        available if c is at/over APRON_1 and under APRON_2
ceiling(BAE,      c) = BAE                            available if unspent AND c is not over APRON_1
ceiling(MIN,      c) = MIN_TEAM                       always available while slots < SLOT_MAX

legal(c, amount, t) ⇔  amount ≤ ceiling(t, c)
                    ∧  c.slots < SLOT_MAX
                    ∧  (c.wall === null  ∨  c.committed + amount ≤ c.wall)
```

`[MODULE RULE]` **The room-versus-exception fork.** The moment a club makes a ROOM signing, its
NTMLE and BAE are marked spent and the ROOM_MLE becomes available. This is the real trade-off in
§2.4 ("using cap space costs you the bigger MLE") modelled as a single switch rather than as the
CBA's full eligibility test. Recorded in §11.3 with its misconception risk.

`[MODULE RULE]` **Taxpayer-MLE eligibility** is modelled as *between the first and second aprons*.
The real test is finer. Recorded in §11.3.

**Books.** A signing at `annual A` with real term `T` writes
`booksY1 += A`, `booksY2 += A × 1.05`, `booksY3 += A × 1.05²` for `T ≥ 2, 3`. The student never
computes this; the product prints the three years. (5% is the real non-incumbent raise, §8.1 #10.)

**Job coverage.** A job closes when a signed player's role matches it. `coveredThrough = 2026-27 + T
− 1`. **A job closed by a two-year deal re-opens in L3.** This is the module's non-summable pair
and it is the whole anti-greedy device: *cover more jobs now, or cover one job for longer.*

**What must be tuned, and against what property.** Exactly three quantities:

| Quantity | Tuned against |
|---|---|
| **Pool size** (7 named + unlimited minimums) as a function of live desks | **Contention property:** over the whole reachable action space, ≥⅓ of desks lose at least one contested offer, and no desk faces a day with fewer than 2 legal, non-dominated, materially different options. Brute-forced, not spot-checked. |
| **Number of signing days** (3) | **Recoverability property (FL5b):** from every reachable opening — including the honestly-labelled stock franchise — at least one legal action sequence reaches the stated obligation: *at or above the floor, ≥14 players, ≥1 open job closed.* |
| **The per-club open-job assignment** | **FL1(a):** no monotone map from money spent to any displayed dimension — for a majority of seats there exists a cheaper allocation that beats a more expensive one on `jobsClosed`. And **FL6(b):** a pure hold strategy is strictly beaten by some active strategy for a majority of seats. |

Nothing else is tuned. This is D29's method: assert the property near where the pair actually
decides, find one setting that clears it, record the trade-off, stop.

### LESSON 2 — **THE FEBRUARY PROBLEM**

*The same league year, five months later. The line has not moved. You have. And the only club that
can fix you is the one whose problem is the reverse of yours.*

#### 4.2.1 The signature mechanic — **THE LEAGUE BOARD**

This is where the challenger axis becomes the mechanic rather than the framing.

You cannot solve your February problem alone. **You can only solve it with a desk whose constraint
is different from yours** — and the difference is not a personality, it is a position on the same
ladder. Denver has talent and no legality. Detroit has legality and no urgency. Detroit's *room* is
worth something to Denver, and that is a market.

**Not a two-party handshake.** A desk **posts** a standing, self-consistent, legal offer to the
league board; any other desk may **take** it. This is deliberate: it dodges the two-party
synchronisation problem (§11.2), it dodges M4's negotiation territory (contract anti-trespass #1),
and it is closer to how the real market works — clubs shop, and a deal is done when somebody says
yes.

**A post is:** `{ out: [contracts from my roster], in: "up to $X of salary", sweetener: pick | none }`.
**Taking a post** applies it if and only if it is legal **for both sides** at the instant of taking.

**The rule the product states, in the module's own voice, with no percentage** (§9.1's ruling
applied verbatim — §8.2 #1 forbids rendering 100%, 110% or 125% as NBA law):

> *"Once you are over the cap, a trade has to be roughly even — you send out about as much salary
> as you take back. The more you have spent, the tighter 'roughly' gets. At the very top you cannot
> take back a single dollar more than you send out, and you cannot even combine two players into
> one trade to make the numbers work."*

`[MODULE RULE]` The module's own bracket, printed as the module's rule and never as the NBA's:
**below the tax, you may take back up to the salary you send out plus $5,000,000; above the first
apron, not one dollar more than you send; above the second apron, additionally, you may not combine
two players into one trade.** The last clause is the one thing here that is uncontested across all
three researchers (§2.3) and it is the best economics in the system.

**What the student manipulates.** Drag contracts from your roster into the OUT tray; the product
computes, live, the maximum salary you may take back and whether that is enough for anything on the
board. Post it. Or browse the posted board and take one.

**What the server computes.** On `take`, re-validate both sides against the *current* state
(another desk may have taken a piece of it), apply atomically or refuse with a reason that names
the binding constraint and, where the constraint is a wall, **the earlier lesson's decision that
drew it**.

**The apron, experienced as confiscation, not as a fee.** When your committed salary crosses
$209,015,000, four items are **struck through, visibly, on your own tool shelf**: sign-and-trade,
the bi-annual exception, the non-taxpayer mid-level, and prior-year trade exceptions (§2.3, cba,
high). Crossing the second apron strikes through two more: any mid-level at all, and **combining
two salaries in one trade**. The confiscation is legible *before* the crossing is committed, and
at least one struck item is one the pair wanted later. This is Phoenix 2024-25 rendered exactly as
the dossier describes it (§5.4).

#### 4.2.2 The exact economic model

```
takeBackMax(c, out) =
    out + 5,000,000              if c.committedAfter < TAX          [MODULE RULE]
    out                          if c.committedAfter ≥ APRON_1      (real shape, §9.1)
    out, and |outContracts| ≤ 1  if c.committedAfter ≥ APRON_2      (real, §2.3, uncontested)

wallCheck(c) : c.wall === null ∨ c.committedAfter ≤ c.wall          (absolute, §2.3)

toolShelf(c) = ALL_TOOLS
             − {sign-and-trade, BAE, NTMLE, prior-year TPE}  if c.committed > APRON_1
             − {every MLE, salary aggregation, cash}          if c.committed > APRON_2
```

**No tax bill is ever rendered as a dollar figure** (§8.2 #5 — the bracket rates come from one
source family, the $24M→$62.5M worked example is single-source, and the Denver ~$132M figure was
never recomputed). What the tax *is* — a price on the marginal dollar rather than a prohibition —
is taught structurally: at REVEAL, the board shows **Boston, summer 2025: about $4.7M of salary
savings produced about $35M of tax savings** (§5.4, cbssports high / foxsports medium, as of
2025-07). The leverage ratio is the lesson; no bill is printed.

**The counterparty must refuse, and refuse legibly.** A club that is not a live desk does not
trade. Live desks refuse by not taking. The refusals that teach are the *system's* refusals:
*"They are hard-capped at $209,015,000 and cannot take back more than they send."*

### LESSON 3 — **THE LINE MOVES**

*The next July. Before the window opens, the class decides how fast the cap should be allowed to
rise. Then they find out what the real rule is, the line moves, everyone gets richer at the same
moment — and they run the window again.*

#### 4.3.1 The signature mechanic — **THE ROOM WRITES THE RULE, THEN LIVES IN IT**

**Stage 1 — THE RULE (≈8 minutes).** The board shows one fact and no others:

> *Last year the league's income went up. The cap is a share of that income, split thirty ways.
> How much should the cap be allowed to go up in one year?*

Each desk commits a number: the largest one-year rise it thinks the rule should allow, chosen from
a bounded dial in **whole dollars, not percentages** (5–6) or with the percentage shown alongside
(7–8). This is a real forecast with a real stake — because the class's own median is then compared
against the real rule, and the *real* rule is what actually runs.

**Stage 2 — THE REVEAL OF THE REAL RULE (≈4 minutes).** Three real years, one screen:

| Season | Cap | One-year change | The rule in force |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016-17 | $94,143,000 | **+~34%** | no growth limit existed |
| 2025-26 | $154,647,000 | **exactly +10.0%** | at the CBA ceiling |
| 2026-27 | $164,961,000 | **+6.7%** | under the ceiling — revenue growth slowed |

(§8.1 #5, official-nba, high. And the 2026-27 figure came in about **$1M below the league's own
projection because local media revenue fell — and the reduction flowed through to the floor, the
tax line and both aprons**, §3.3.)

**Same trigger. Different rule. Different outcome.** That is FL9(b) instantiated, and it is the
answer to *"the cap is a number handed down from nowhere."* Local trouble in thirteen cities moved
the spending limit for all thirty clubs.

**Stage 3 — THE LINE MOVES, AND THE WINDOW RE-RUNS (≈20 minutes).** The ladder's five lines
**slide up, once, in front of the room**, and every desk's chip stays where it was — so every desk
is suddenly further below every line than it was a second ago. Then the second window runs: same
mechanic as L1, same three-day loop, same board discipline, with three differences:

1. **Your books arrived from L1 and L2.** Multi-year deals are still on them. Dead money is still
   on them. Renounced rights are still gone. A job closed with a two-year deal is **open again**.
2. **The pool is the same seven names again**, at the same real asks — *deliberately*, because the
   whole intellectual reveal is a price comparison across the room's own two windows.
3. **Everyone has more room than last time.**

`[MODULE RULE]` **The new cap.** The module does **not** print an NBA figure for a season the league
has not announced. It prints its own arithmetic, labelled, in the module's voice:

> *"Our league's income went up. Under the real rule the cap may never fall and may never rise by
> more than one-tenth of itself — at most $16,496,100 this year. Ours rises by $13,196,880, to
> $178,157,880. That is this room's number, worked out from the real rule. It is not a league
> figure."*

The percent is never load-bearing at 5–6: the ceiling is shown as a dollar amount and the
comparison is a subtraction (§8.2 #21). Registered in §11.3 with its misconception risk. The floor,
tax and aprons move with it by their real published ratios (90%, 121.5%, and the real 2026-27
offsets — printed as *this room's* thresholds, never as NBA truth).

#### 4.3.2 The exact economic model

Identical to L1's, with the thresholds replaced and the books seeded. Plus one new, decisive
quantity, which is the module's actual finale:

```
priceRatio(playerId) = (price he settled at in L3's window)
                     ÷ (price he settled at in L1's window)
```

computed **from the room's own two settled markets**, and rendered as **two dollar figures side by
side, never as a ratio** (§8.2 #21 forbids the ratio at 5–6; 7–8 may see it as a percentage).

**The property this finale rests on, and the only thing L3 tunes:** when every desk's room rises by
the same amount, **the settled prices of the contested players must rise by more than the settled
prices of the uncontested ones.** That is the winner's curse as an emergent property of the room's
own bidding, not an asserted fact — and if it does not emerge, the finale is deleted rather than
narrated. It is asserted by a sweep of the reachable bid space, near where desks actually bid, not
at the extremes (D29).

**No expected value anywhere.** M1 may use uncertainty and bargaining power; EV and negotiation
belong to M4 (D2, contract anti-trespass #1). No probability is computed at either band; at 7–8 the
room may build a long-run frequency from its own pooled results (7.SP.C.6) and nothing more.

## 5. THE CARRY

### 5.1 What moves, in what shape

A **bounded flat record**, not a log. State size is a first-class budget: every session read
deep-clones the whole state through JSON and every write re-serialises the store
(`snapshotRepository.ts:45`, `:222`), so the carry is capped at **one object per desk plus one
class-level object**, with no arrays that grow with actions.

```ts
type Carry = {
  v: 1;
  gradeBand: "5-6" | "7-8";        // §11.2 — requires the D38 amendment
  league: {
    year: "2026-27" | "2027-28";
    thresholds: { cap; floor; tax; apron1; apron2 };   // real in L1/L2; module arithmetic in L3
    settled: { playerId: string; annual: number }[];   // ≤ 8 rows — RAW PRICES, not a claim
  };
  desks: Record<SeatId, {
    club: string;                          // 1. identity — real club id
    books: [number, number, number];       // 2. committed salary, this year + two
    dead: { name: string; annual: number; throughYear: string }[];   // 3. by NAME
    lost: { what: string; why: string }[]; // 4. irreversible losses, each with its reason
    tools: { ntmle: bool; taxMle: bool; roomMle: bool; bae: bool;
             tpe?: { amount: number; expiresYear: string } };        // 5. the tool state
    wall: { line: number; drawnBy: string } | null;                  // 6. one flag, one number
    slots: number;                                                   // 7. the second constraint
    jobs: { role: string; coveredThrough: string | null }[];         // 8. coverage, not quality
  }>;
};
```

Eight fields. Every one is here because it changes a **reachable option set**, not a display.
Field 6 — one flag and one number — is the cheapest and richest carry in the whole dossier.

### 5.2 The falsifiable property

**P1 — named L1 decision, named reachable L3 state.**

> In L1's window, a desk running **Sacramento** re-signs its own expiring free agent using **cap
> room**. To use cap room it must **renounce** its other free agent, which permanently destroys
> Bird rights to him for that free agency (§2.5, cba, high — irrevocable except in a narrow
> offer-sheet-rescission window).
>
> Therefore in L3's window, the action **A = "re-sign that player over the cap at up to 5 years
> with 8% raises"** is **refused**, and the refusal names the L1 decision by day:
> *"You gave up his rights on signing day 1 to make room for Ayton."*
> A desk that filled the same job with the taxpayer mid-level exception instead reaches L3 with
> that same action **legal**.
>
> **Stated as a test.** There exist an L3 action **A** and two L1 histories **H1** (renounced) and
> **H2** (did not) such that A is refused under H1 and applied under H2, and the refusal reason
> names the L1 decision. Asserted by replaying the reducer over both branches. **If no such
> (A, H1, H2) triple exists, the carry is narration.**

**P1b — the same property across the L1→L2 seam, on the wall.** A = "combine two contracts into one
trade." H1 = used the NTMLE on signing day 2 (`wall = 209,015,000`). H2 = filled the job with
minimums. A is refused under H1, applied under H2, and the refusal names signing day 2.

**P1c (7–8 only) — L2→L3.** A = "use any mid-level exception." H1 = closed L2 above the second
apron. H2 = did not.

**P2 — no sludge.** For every field in the carry there exists at least one reachable L3 state that
differs on the basis of **that field alone**. A field with no reachable consequence is deleted from
the carry, not documented. (`league.settled` earns its place by being the only input to L3's
two-windows reveal; if that reveal is cut, the field is cut with it.)

**P3 — recoverability across the carry.** For every reachable seeded L2 and L3 opening —
**including every reachable dead-money position and the honestly-labelled stock franchise** — at
least one legal action satisfies that lesson's stated obligation. Brute-forced over the whole
reachable carry space. Memphis' real position — **$3,926,207 of room against $21,909,021 of dead
money** (§4.1 #9) — is a seat, and it must be playable.

**P4 — attribution, per band.** At 5–6 the causal sentence at the next lesson's opening is
**computed from the carried field**, never authored per scenario: `"You have $${gap} to work with
because you signed ${name} for $${annual} on signing day ${d}."` At 7–8 it is silent — and the
information needed to diagnose it (the books by year, the tool shelf, the wall and who drew it) is
present on the 7–8 surface, or "silent" has become "hidden."

**M11's failure, explicitly forbidden.** A carry may never destroy or blank a pair's franchise
identity. `club` is validated first and independently; if any other field is missing or malformed,
the club survives and the rest is replaced by an **honestly labelled stock position** — *"Parts of
your books could not be read from last lesson. You are running Memphis on a stock cap sheet."*
Never disguised as carried (D18).

### 5.3 What deliberately does NOT carry, and why

| Not carried | Why |
|---|---|
| Every action taken | The runtime already owns the class log (`classEvents`, WHILE YOU WERE AWAY). A seed that is a transcript is a transcript. |
| Per-round intermediates — composed offers, reveal beats, gate calls, UI state | They are the previous room's choreography, not its economics. |
| Any value derivable from the books — totals, room, distance-to-a-line, tools *count* | D15. Live re-derivation once printed a false projector number; the discipline is **carry the facts, freeze the claims within a lesson, never carry a claim across lessons.** A carried claim has no reducer behind it in the receiving lesson and will eventually contradict that lesson's own arithmetic. |
| Any other desk's data, in any form | `studentView` must never leak another seat, and a carried blob is the easiest place for that to happen unnoticed. |
| Outcome labels, strategy classifications, awards, any evaluation of how a lesson went | `classifyStrategy` is on the discard list for a reason. A carried judgement is a progression system (D4). |
| A cross-band carry | Refused or honestly relabelled as stock. See §6.4. |
| Anything the module cannot honestly stand in for | The practical test that settles most arguments: if the module cannot ship an honestly-labelled stock value for a field, that field is not carryable. |

## 6. THE TWO BANDS

### 6.1 What is IDENTICAL, because the economics is the economics

1. **The world.** Same league, same clubs, same five thresholds, same seven people on the board,
   same dates, same real cases.
2. **The mechanism.** One reducer per lesson. Given the same actions from the same state, the two
   bands resolve **identically**. If they diverge, the bands have learned two different economics
   and D22's shared-consequence requirement is false in the only place it matters.
3. **Which concepts are true.** Opportunity cost is opportunity cost. The cap is soft in both rooms.
   Dead money persists in both rooms. **Every false lesson in the contract §4 is false in both
   rooms, and every falsifiable guard binds in both rooms.** No band is exempted from a
   false-lesson guard. Depth is a dial on what a student is asked to *do*, never on what is *true*.
4. **Decision quality ≠ outcome.** Both bands, always. Only *who does the separating* differs.
5. **Irreversibility and recoverability.** Both bands, both properties.
6. **Attribution.** Both bands can name the choice that produced today's state. 5–6 is told; 7–8
   diagnoses.
7. **The real numbers.** Real cap figures stay real in both rooms. What changes is the arithmetic
   the *student* performs.

### 6.3 The 5–6 scaffolded exploration phase, concretely

Problem-solving-before-instruction is **g = +0.50 at grades 6–10 and g = −0.09 at grades 2–5**, and
the moderator splits at exactly this band boundary (§6.3, Sinha & Kapur, high; direction
well-supported, magnitude uncertain per §7.11). The 5–6 band contains grade 5, so it straddles the
moderator. Running the 7–8 front half on a grade-5 room runs the exact condition the meta-analysis
found negative. Four concrete pieces:

1. **DAY ZERO, RUN WITH THE CLASS — on the projector, before day 1 opens.** The teacher runs one
   complete offer for a **stand-in club that is not any desk's** (Portland). The board shows the
   open job, the three people who could fill it, the money, and the one tool. The class votes out
   loud; the teacher commits it on `/teach`; it resolves against two scripted rival offers so the
   room sees **one loss and one win** before anything is at stake. Then the forgone panel for the
   choice they made goes up. Only then does day 1 open. This is a worked example run *by* the class,
   which is what the meta-analysis's own authors recommend, and it costs about four minutes.
   **Day 1 is not day 0 restated:** day 0 is a single uncontested offer; day 1 has rivals and a
   shrinking board. A round that is the same card with new numbers means the room copied a
   demonstration and nothing else happened.
2. **The constraint state is always visible and never scrolls.** The ladder is pinned. The two live
   numbers — distance to the floor, distance to the cap — are always on screen.
3. **An in-product trade-off prompt, computed from this desk's own state, on every composition.**
   Not a tip: an arithmetic sentence. *"Spending $18.0M here leaves $2.4M and one slot — not enough
   for any wing on the board."*
4. **A mandatory teacher-led contrast at CONSEQUENCE.** The director hands the teacher the two
   specific desks to put side by side and the sentence to say. This is not optional pacing; the
   consolidation stage is what both bands keep, and it is the half the evidence supports at grade 5.

One honest caveat the director carries for the teacher: exploration-first costs immediate
performance and buys transfer. A flat quiz on the way out is not evidence of failure.

### 6.4 The cross-band seed

**The economic requirement, not merely the engineering one:** the two bands do not present the same
decision set. A 5–6 room never chose a tool and never triggered a hard cap by its own transaction.
A 7–8 L2 seeded from a 5–6 L1 therefore attributes to the student a constraint **they were never
given the chance to create** — an attribution failure, and attribution is the one thing this module
cannot lose.

So: **a cross-band carry is refused, or accepted and honestly relabelled as stock.** The refusal
requires `gradeBand` in the seed envelope, which the runtime does not currently carry
(`sessionService.ts:320` ships `{lessonModuleId, state}` only). See §11.2.

## 7. THE CLASS REVEAL

**No universal franchise score exists anywhere in the model, the payload or the renderer.** There is
no team quality figure, no OVR, no fan trust, no franchise value, no ten-year outlook, and no rank.
This is a structural commitment, not a policy: the model never computes such a number, so no
renderer can print one and no award can crown one.

What the projector shows instead, **all at once, in one frame**, is five readings of the same room —
each one a **count or a sum of a thing the model actually holds**, each one naming a **different**
club:

| Reading | What it counts | Why it conflicts with the others |
|---|---|---|
| **MOST JOBS CLOSED** | open jobs filled, 0–2 | Costs money and tools. |
| **LONGEST COVERAGE** | Σ years of coverage across closed jobs | Buys years by giving up flexibility. |
| **MOST ROOM LEFT** | dollars between committed salary and the nearest line above | Bought by *not* closing jobs. |
| **MOST TOOLS LEFT** | unused exceptions, 0–4 | Bought by not acting. |
| **CHEAPEST JOB CLOSED** | the smallest dollar amount in the room that closed a job | Almost never the biggest spender. This is the FL1 killer, and it is a real number from the room's own play. |

Plus, beneath them and not ranked: **THE FLOOR TABLE** (who is under $148,465,000 and by how much,
and where the money goes), and — at L2 and L3 — **WHO COULD NOT ACT, AND WHY** (the rule named, the
desk not blamed).

**Two properties the reveal must satisfy, asserted, not assured:**

- **At least three distinct desks appear across the five readings** in the reachable outcome space
  for any room of ≥6 desks. If one desk sweeps, the readings are not conflicting and the reveal is
  a leaderboard wearing five hats.
- **The class evidence contains at least one actual instance, from the room's own play, of a
  cheaper allocation beating a more expensive one on a displayed dimension** (FL1(b)) — not a slide.

**And the reveal explicitly refuses to adjudicate.** The board's own words, verbatim, module-owned:
*"Five clubs. Five different things they are best at. None of these is the answer."*

Ordering matters: **CHEAPEST JOB CLOSED is pressed last**, because it is the one that breaks the
intuition, and because it needs the other four on the wall to be surprising.

## 8. THE FINAL DEFENSE

L3's ARGUE phase, ~9 minutes. Decision quality vs outcome comes **last** because it needs the room's
own accumulated evidence — three sessions of identical choices producing different results — to be
anything other than a slogan.

### 8.1 Grades 5–6 — the product does the separating

The pair does not have to *discover* that outcome ≠ decision. Adults fail at that (Baron & Hershey;
§7.11 notes the developmental literature for 10–14-year-olds is thin), so the product does it for
them and the teacher says the sentence.

1. **THE SAME CALL, TWO ENDINGS.** The board shows two desks that made the **same** decision — both
   signed a BIG at roughly the same price on the same day — and whose L3 positions now differ, with
   the *reason* named and attributable (one's other job re-opened because the deal was two years;
   the other's did not). Identical choice, different result, cause stated. Never a shrug.
2. **THE FRAME.** Each pair gets one screen with three computed blanks and speaks two moves:
   *"We chose ______ because ______."* / *"The class evidence that backs us is ______."*
   The blanks are pre-filled with **their own numbers**, from their own frozen `foregoneAtLock` and
   their own closing books, so the argument is about reasons, not recall.
3. **THE TEACHER'S SENTENCE**, supplied by the director and computed from the room: *"Two desks made
   the same call. One of them ended up better off. That did not make it a better call."*
4. **Convergence is required** (Rule 5). The debrief lands on one nameable mechanism: the thing that
   decided the outcome was the *term* of a contract, not the quality of the decision.

### 8.2 Grades 7–8 — the student does the sorting, and defends it

1. **THE SORT.** Four real cases, on cards, in the room's hands, presented with the **information as
   it stood at the date** and the outcome withheld:
   - Portland, 2007-06-28 — Oden was the accepted pre-draft consensus No. 1 (§5.6). *Most classes
     will also pick Oden. That is the point.*
   - Toronto, 2018-07-18 — trade your most popular player for someone with one year left who is
     reported to prefer Los Angeles (§5.6).
   - Milwaukee twice — Holiday 2020 and Lillard 2023, **the same franchise, the same category of
     aggressive bet, twice**: once a title, once $21,311,053 a year of dead money through 2030-31
     (§5.6, §8.1 #21).
   - Dallas, 2025-02-01 — with Nico Harrison's actual quoted rationale, and **no editorial verdict**
     (§5.6).
   Sort them into good-decision/bad-outcome and bad-decision/good-outcome. Then defend the sort.
   **At least one case where the consensus move was genuinely right and indistinguishable ex ante
   must be in the set** (Milwaukee/Holiday 2020), or the module teaches contrarianism instead of
   outcome-bias resistance.
2. **THE ASSIGNED-SIDE DEBATE**, on the best paired case in the dossier: **Indiana and Milwaukee on
   Myles Turner, one player, one week, July 2025** (§5.8, espn, high). Indiana was reported "fully
   prepared" to enter the tax for the first time since 2005-06, then pulled back after Haliburton's
   Achilles tear. Milwaukee created the room by waiving and stretching Lillard's $113M. One side
   argues Indiana; one side argues Milwaukee. **Indiana's change of mind was rational once its star
   was injured, not cheap** — a room that reaches that sentence on its own has done the work.
3. **THE THIRD MOVE.** Every desk must state the strongest case **against its own club's strategy**,
   and the product hands it the strongest counter-desk's **actual numbers** — computed from the
   room, not authored — so the counterargument has evidence behind it and not just a shape.
4. **Resolution may be a defensible disagreement, adjudicated by evidence** — never "everyone has
   their opinion," which is the multiplist failure mode this exact age is most prone to.

**No score, award, bonus, or reveal language anywhere in the module rewards matching the historical
move.** Grep-assertable, and asserted.

## 10. THE VISUAL WORLD

**The place: the league office, not the front office.** Every competing direction will put the
student in *their own* war room. This one puts them in the room where the same rule is drawn for
everybody — a ruled wall, a ledger, and a board of people. Near-black ground, one brand accent, the
Cap Room register. Real clubs as **typographic wordmarks only**: no logos, no marks, no arena
imagery, no player photographs (§8.2 #22, and no image MIME type is servable anyway —
`http.ts:87-96`).

### 10.1 The hero object per surface — exactly one each

| Surface | Hero object | Composition |
|---|---|---|
| **/board** | **THE LADDER** | One vertical scale filling 70% of the frame height. Five ruled lines at their real dollar values, each labelled with its name and its number in evidence-tier type clearing the 2.6%-of-screen-height back-row floor. THE ROOM bins that, on its own grid, capped at 12 bars, counting **committed decisions only** and claiming movement only against a number the desk chose itself. ### 10.2 What earns motion

Exactly four things move in the whole module. Everything else is still.

1. **A chip rising on the ladder** when a signing settles — 700ms, once, on the REVEAL beat only.
2. **A card leaving the board** when another desk signs that person — the permanent-loss beat, and
   the only motion that happens during PLAY.
3. **The five lines sliding up**, once, in L3 — the single largest motion in the module, and the
   one the whole design is built to earn.
4. **A tool being struck through** on the shelf when a line is crossed.

All four collapse under `prefers-reduced-motion` to an instant state change. Colour is never the
only carrier: every status has a word, every struck tool has a strikethrough *and* a reason line.

### 10.3 Measured drawings (D25)

**The ladder's geometry carries a quantity**, so it is measured, not eyeballed. A browser instrument
asserts that a chip's vertical position is its committed salary's position on the scale, within a
stated relative tolerance, with a no-content baseline subtracted and a minimum measurable share —
and it is **poisoned** (fed a deliberately mis-positioned chip) and must reject it before any pass
is believed. The ladder may only encode quantities the model actually holds: committed salary and
the five thresholds. It never encodes "quality," because no such quantity exists.

### 10.4 Taken from the mockups, and refused

**Taken:** the franchise identity band, carrying a **real** club, a real market fact and a real
financial position; constraints living in a persistent header as several distinct resources rather
than one bar; "potential impact" chips naming which dimensions a choice moves, *without* numbers, as
5–6 scaffolding that 7–8 has withheld; the named-contrasts panel on `/teach`; AT TIME CUT as an
enumerated pre-close checklist; the strategies-compared composition — which is exactly §7's five
readings, and is strictly better than a leaderboard.

**Refused:** Team OVR, Fan Trust, Franchise Value and the ten-year outlook (no such quantity is
computed, so none can be drawn); the single ranked leaderboard and CLASS CHAMPION; XP, levels,
ranks and trophies (D4); "Team 4"; the four generic decision cards, which are the exact menu of
abstractions the founder rules out; the fake "6 Days Left" deadline — TIME CUT is real,
server-clocked and teacher-held; negative cap room rendered as a red failure state, because being
over a line is a legitimate NBA position with consequences and not an error.

### 11.2 Runtime changes actually required

Three, and only three.

1. **`gradeBand` on the seed envelope.** `sessionService.ts:320` ships `{lessonModuleId, state}`.

2. **Nothing else on the server.** The posted-offer board, the shared pool, the ladder, the carry

| S8 | **Open jobs and filled-slot counts per club are the module's**, derived from each club's real narrative tension. | No per-club roster-need ledger exists in the dossier. | A student may think the NBA publishes "open jobs." | Labelled as this room's reading of the club's situation, never as a league fact. |

### 11.4 The riskiest technical unknown

**Not the trades. The desk registry against a shared, conserved pool.**

`initialState` runs **once**, with `seatIds: []` (`sessionService.ts:322`). `join()` never calls the
module and never bumps `session.version`. `closeRound` and teacher hooks reduce with `seatIds: []`.
Every other direction shares this problem; **this design makes it worse in a specific way**, and I
would rather say so than discover it in week six:

> **In a parallel-solitaire module, an empty desk is invisible. In this one, an empty desk is a club
> that occupies the league.** Pool contention, the ladder, the floor table and every one of the five
> readings depend on *how many desks are actually live*. A club claimed at minute 3 and abandoned at
> minute 12 is a club that made offers and then stopped, and the room's economics is different
> because of it.

**The mitigation, decided before any content is written:** clubs are assigned from a **fixed
deterministic order** at first claim; the **live set is frozen at the first entry into PLAY** and
stored in module state; unclaimed clubs are simply **absent from the market** and never make offers
and never appear on the ladder; the pool size is computed **from the frozen live count**; and *"the
pair that joined and never touched anything"* is a named test case on `/play`, `/board`,
`teacherView`, `aggregate`, `round.unresolved` and the close hook, separately.

**Second unknown, smaller but real:** state size. Real books (three years × up to 15 contracts ×
12–16 desks) plus dead money plus the tool state is heavier than M2's state, and every read
deep-clones the whole thing through JSON while every write re-serialises the store and the
checkpoint holds a second copy. The carry is capped at §5.1's flat record for exactly this reason,
and `latency-harness.cjs` and `e2e-full-room.cjs` at 32 seats get run **early**, not at the end.

## 12. HOW THIS DIES

### 12.1 "It is a spreadsheet with basketball names on it."

**The argument.** The hero object is a ruler. The student's verbs are *offer*, *post*, *take*. There
is no game, no court, no ball, no season, no wins. Design B hands a ten-year-old a broken franchise
with Damian Lillard's ghost on the payroll; this hands them a ledger and a line. A room of fifth
graders may find the ladder beautiful for ninety seconds and accounting for the other fifty minutes.

**My answer, and it is a partial one.** The drama in this design is entirely **social**, not
financial: seven real people leave the board one at a time, in front of everybody, and each
departure is somebody else's decision landing on you. That is the loop, and it is the loop children
actually play in every playground game there is. The ladder is the *scoreboard for a rule*, not the
activity. But I concede the risk is real and it is the sharpest thing a Player/Gameplay critic will
say. Three concrete hedges: every settled signing prints as a **transaction sentence with a person's
name and a date**, never as a row in a table; the board's PLAY frame shows **people, not payrolls**;
and the CONSEQUENCE beat that carries the lesson — the floor bill — is a **human** outrage
("you paid twelve million dollars to players on other teams"), not an arithmetic one. If the
prototype reads as accounting, the correct response is to kill this direction, not to add
decoration.

### 12.2 "The conserved pool makes the room chaotic, and the losers learn nothing."

**The argument.** With sixteen desks and seven people, most desks lose most contests. A desk that
loses days 1 and 2 arrives at day 3 with nothing, having *experienced* only failure — and worse, the
failure was caused by other desks, which is the least attributable kind. CLAUDE.md §8 requires the
student to attribute consequences to **their own** choice; "somebody else outbid you" is the exact
opposite.

**My answer.** This is the strongest objection and the design answers it structurally in three
places. **(a)** Losing is not empty: a lost day is a *named* loss with a *named* cause — the
consummated deal publishes, so you know exactly who signed him and for how much, and the honest
lesson "I could have had him for $1M more, and here is what that $1M would have cost me" is
attributable to *your* number, not their luck. **(b)** The minimum market never closes: no desk can
be shut out of the obligation, and the recoverability property (P3) is brute-forced over the whole
reachable space, not spot-checked. **(c)** The tie-break is in-model and economic — the incumbent's
real 5yr/8% advantage, then remaining room — never HTTP arrival time, so "why did they get him?"
never has the answer "their laptop was quicker" (`tradeDeadline.ts:783`, the defect this replaces).
And the contention property is *tuned*, with a stated target of roughly one contested loss per desk
per window — enough that the pool feels alive, not so much that most days are empty.

### 12.3 "L3 prints a cap number the NBA never announced, and the whole finale rests on it."

**The argument.** §8.2 is binding, and the module's biggest beat — the lines sliding up — runs on
**$178,157,880**, which is not a league figure. A Sports Reality critic is entitled to call that the
same class of defect as the discarded module's $130M cap rise. And the two-windows price comparison,
the module's intellectual climax, only exists because that invented number exists.

**My answer, and its limits.** The **rule** is real, official and §8.1-safe (the cap may never fall
and may never rise more than 10%). The **three outcomes** are real, official and dated (+34% / +10.0%
/ +6.7%). What is the module's own is one arithmetic step, and the contract explicitly permits a
lesson to render its own registered arithmetic **labelled as the module's**. It is labelled every
time it appears, it is in the ledger as S6 with its misconception risk, and — the part that matters
— **the class derives it from the rule rather than receiving it**, which converts an invented figure
into a student computation. It never appears on any surface without the sentence *"this room's
number, worked out from the real rule; it is not a league figure."*

What I will not claim: that this is as safe as printing nothing. It is not, and if a reviewer rules
it out, the honest fallback is to run L3's window at the **unchanged** 2026-27 thresholds and lose
FL9(a) — the constraint no longer moves inside the module — which costs this direction its finale
and most of its distinctiveness. I would rather have the argument than pre-emptively surrender the
best beat in the module, but I am recording the fallback so the choice is deliberate.
