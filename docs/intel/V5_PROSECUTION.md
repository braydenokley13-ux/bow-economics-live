# V5 Prosecution: Adversarial Review of BOW Model V5

**Subject:** `BOW_Model_V5_Final.pdf` (120 pages, "Final canonical model," dated August 20, 2026)
**Reviewer posture:** Hostile, independent. This document protects the Track 101 refoundation from "V5 said it, therefore we build it."
**Method:** Full read, all 120 pages, in four ~20-page passes plus the appendix audit.

## How to read this report

V5 is not a neutral input. It is itself a persuasive document that grades its own homework (see Finding 5), sets its own success criteria, and stamps its own recommendations "DECISION" (adopt now) rather than "HYPOTHESIS" (do not scale) wherever doing so favors a bigger build. The findings below are ranked by how much damage they would cause if a later agent treated V5's language and formatting as proof rather than as one interested party's argument.

Two calibration notes before the findings, in fairness to V5: (a) it explicitly disclaims a generalized LMS, an automatic Economics/Financial-Literacy/Challenges merger, and any claim that class data proves causality or mastery (p.3) — these guardrails are real and worth keeping; (b) its "multiplayer is a mechanism, not a quality badge" doctrine (pp.7, 14) matches the founder mandate and should survive. The rest of this report is about where V5 drifts from the mandate given to this refoundation.

## Finding 1 — Commerce/enterprise architecture is scheduled into the first two weeks of build, directly against "no commerce/enterprise features"

**Damage: severe.** Pages 52–74 (Part IV, "Company and business model," and Part V, "Platform and simulation system") construct a full monetization stack: an eight-tier subscription ladder (BOW Free → Educator Plus → Department Launch → School Complete → District pricing, p.57–58), a four-tier sponsorship menu (BOW Access, p.59), a consumer subscription product (BOW League, p.59–60), an enterprise/API pricing schedule with six tiers from Developer Sandbox to Strategic Distribution (p.61), a creator marketplace with a 20% take rate (BOW Exchange, p.62), and an explicit "$10B path" revenue model with per-engine illustrative dollar figures ($180M institutional, $135M League, $70M infrastructure, $60M Exchange, etc., p.55–56).

This is not abstract long-term ambition kept safely out of scope — it is embedded in the concrete build sequence. Page 72, "First 90 days, Days 1–15": *"Declare BOW-WEBSITE the control plane. Add Product, Plan, Entitlement, AccessGrant, SponsorGrant, ExperienceVersion, Session, Participant, UsageRecord, and EvidenceReference designs."* That is a full commercial entitlement/billing data model, scheduled ahead of a single Ramaz lesson shipping. If a later agent inherits V5's build order uncritically, engineering time in week one goes to `SponsorGrant` and `Subscription` schemas instead of the live classroom lesson system the founder actually asked to be rebuilt.

**Internal contradiction:** V5's own doctrine says "Do not charge pilots" and "keep Ramaz/D26 free during the proof year" (pp. 58, 63: "Charge pilots... Do not do"). The build sequence disagrees with the doctrine box on the facing pages. A prosecutor cannot resolve this for the team — see Decision Packet 1.

## Finding 2 — Predetermined simulation/lesson counts violate "no predetermined simulation count"

**Damage: severe.** The founder mandate given to this refoundation is explicit: no predetermined simulation count. V5 repeatedly commits to specific numbers as targets, not as things to be discovered:

- "Eight flagships cover the current tracks" (p.40); "Eight flagship portfolio" is listed as a "WORKING SPEC" on the decision ledger (p.50).
- "Build or refound six to eight extraordinary flagships before expanding the visible shelf" (p.69).
- A "QUALITY DECISION" box: *"Prefer twelve simulations students remember... over 100 simulations that exist only as repository links"* (p.69) — twelve is asserted as the right number with no derivation shown.
- A fixed portfolio-allocation percentage by motif: "sports for approximately 70%... everyday personal finance for approximately 20%... one alternate motif... approximately 10%" (p.69).
- "Six Ramaz Alpha lessons" as the Phase-2 validation set (pp.46–47).

None of these numbers derive from a stated pedagogical need; they read as round numbers chosen for narrative tidiness ("eight," "six," "twelve," "70/20/10"). A team that treats "eight flagships" as canon will build to a headcount instead of building to what the Track 101 economics curriculum actually requires — precisely the failure mode the founder mandate was written to prevent.

## Finding 3 — Track 201 (grades 7–8) and Financial Literacy consume half of the near-term validation plan, diluting the grades 5–6 mandate

**Damage: high.** The mission for this refoundation is Track 101, "roughly grades 5–6." V5 treats Track 201 (explicitly "grades approximately 7–8," p.18) as an equally canonical, equally resourced pillar throughout: comparable page count (pp.24–27 vs. 20–23), a full parallel "Track differentiation" spec (p.42), and — most concretely — the Phase-2 "Ramaz Alpha lesson set" that V5 recommends actually piloting is **half out of the grades-5-6 mandate**: of the six lessons chosen for first live validation, three are Track 101 (101 Salary Cap Basics, 101 Small Markets, 101 Why Draft Isn't Ranking) and three are Track 201 (201 How Teams Bend the Cap, 201 Player Economics, 201 Modeling the Market) (p.47). If this plan is executed as written, half of the founder's scarce pilot-teaching hours go to validating out-of-mandate grade-7-8 content instead of concentrating on Track 101.

Financial Literacy is treated the same way: it is elevated to a "co-equal pillar" with its own program-arm matrix (pp.13–14), its own future architecture template (p.13), and an explicit resourcing target ("20% so Financial Literacy is a true co-equal pillar rather than a promise," p.69) — none of which the Track 101 mandate calls for.

## Finding 4 — Sports-only mandate is undermined by V5's own roadmap to a second motif

**Damage: high.** The founder mandate is "sports-only (basketball/sports business)." V5's own text treats this as temporary: *"Sports remains BOW Economics' flagship motif... It should not become a permanent limit or a decorative skin"* (p.36), and the Motif roadmap names "creator economy" as the "next candidate" (p.36) with a concrete resourcing commitment: *"Use approximately 10% for one alternate motif — creator economy is the leading candidate — to test whether BOW's mechanics travel beyond sports"* (p.69). This is a pre-approved diversion of build capacity away from sports before the sports-only Track 101 spine is even finished. It should be treated as out of scope, not as a queued next step.

## Finding 5 — The "Adversarial product review" is not adversarial; it is V5 rebutting itself

**Damage: moderate-high, because it launders confirmation bias as scrutiny.** Section 28 (p.48) stages nine "critics" (Economist, Game designer, Track 101 student, Architecture critic, Skeptical product critic, etc.) and gives each one a tidy "V5 response" that reaffirms the existing architecture — e.g., "Architecture critic: Shared core can become a universal platform that slows both subjects, both arms, and every format" is answered with "Small contracts, distinct runtimes, optional bridges, and extraction only after a second real need" — an assertion, not a demonstrated mitigation. A document cannot adversarially test itself; every "attack" in this section was written by the same author defending the same plan. Readers should treat Section 28 as V5's list of known objections, not as evidence those objections were resolved. This is the clearest textual instance of V5 biasing the team toward its own proposed solution rather than surfacing real risk (mission's "conflicts where V5 biases the team toward its own proposed solutions").

## Finding 6 — Premature generic platform/control-plane abstraction

**Damage: moderate-high.** Despite explicit disclaimers against "a generalized LMS" (p.3) and "one universal runtime" (p.13), V5's Part V designs exactly that in substance: a "BOW control plane" (p.65) owning a generic identity/organization/membership/product/plan/subscription/entitlement/access-grant/sponsor-grant/usage-record/evidence-reference schema (p.68) meant to unify three runtimes — Live Labs, Decision Challenges, and BOW League — none of which coexist in production today, and two of which (Decision Challenges as a shared runtime, BOW League) do not yet have a single validated classroom user. Building a shared control plane to arbitrate between products that don't yet exist is premature abstraction by definition: the mission explicitly calls out "generic engine/platform ambitions" as a pattern to distrust, and this is the clearest structural instance of it, notwithstanding V5's own "Build constraint" box on page 11 that says the opposite ("Do not build BOW Core as a separate infrastructure program... extract a shared contract only after at least two products need the same behavior").

## Finding 7 — False precision in the $10B narrative and pricing tables

**Damage: moderate.** Pages 54–56 present a revenue model with named engines and specific illustrative dollar amounts (Institutional $180M, BOW League $135M, Infrastructure $70M, BOW Access $50M, BOW Exchange $60M, Studio $75M — "Illustrative total... $570M") and a "SCALE TEST" box computing a 17.5× revenue multiple to reach $10B. V5 itself labels this "not a forecast" (p.55) and its own Appendix D admits the external comparables (Duolingo, Roblox, Stripe, EVERFI, Epic) "do not prove BOW's model" (p.73). Yet the specificity of the district-pricing table (exact figures like $7,500/site, $12,000/year, $25,000/year platform fee, p.58) creates an impression of market-tested diligence that the document explicitly disclaims elsewhere. This is a textbook case of "assumptions dressed as evidence": precise numbers borrow the authority of data without being data.

## Finding 8 — The 3-lessons-per-module resolution is a document-completeness inference stamped as a tested DECISION

**Damage: moderate.** Page 2 states: *"An early outline uses four lessons per module; a later and substantially more complete map uses three lessons per module. V5 treats the later 4-module × 3-lesson structure as the latest coherent working curriculum."* This reasoning — "the later source document is more complete, therefore it is canon" — is an editorial inference about source provenance, not pedagogical evidence, yet it is filed under V5's "DECISION" label ("adopt now unless new evidence overturns it," p.2) alongside genuinely load-bearing architecture calls. The founder mandate says the ~3 lessons per module *may* be redesigned, reordered, merged, or split — implying flexibility, not a number to be locked from a document-comparison heuristic. Downstream builders may treat "three lessons" as fixed when it was never actually decided by evidence.

## Finding 9 — Appendix A conflates real playtest evidence with bare uptime checks

**Damage: moderate.** Page 39 candidly discloses: *"Direct new browser playtesting from this workspace was blocked... V5 therefore uses the Simulation Library's recorded end-to-end playtests and source metadata and does not claim fresh direct verification."* That caveat is honest. But Appendix A (pp.75–106) presents entries with genuinely substantive "Recorded finding" text (e.g., Cap Crash, p.76: specific win/roster numbers from a real run) in the identical table format as entries whose entire "Recorded finding" is a bare HTTP check — e.g., "GitHub Pages returned 200 on 2026-08-14. Reachability only" (Signal Engine, p.92) or "No GitHub Pages deployment (404)... Requires a build to run" (dozens of ARCHIVE/KILL entries, pp.98–106). A reader skimming the audit for "which simulations are validated" can easily mistake a passing uptime check for a passing pedagogy check, because both are printed with the same authority under "V5 decision."

## Finding 10 — No designed mechanism for founder-desired module continuity

**Damage: moderate.** The founder mandate states module continuity is desirable when prior decisions create meaningful future consequences. V5's per-lesson architecture provides a "Second run/comparison" pattern (rerun the *same* lesson with one changed condition, e.g., pp.20–23) and, within a single Track 201 lesson, a two-period design ("Two-Year Cap Planner," p.25). But nowhere in the Lesson blueprint contract (Appendix B.1, p.107), the Class-data schema (p.33–34), or the module maps (pp.20–27) is there a mechanism for a decision made in Module *n*, Lesson 1 to alter the starting state of Lesson 2 or 3 in the same module. Every lesson in the current design starts from a fresh, lesson-scoped state. If later agents build strictly to V5's blueprint, cross-lesson consequence — a founder-fixed value — will not exist unless someone notices the gap and adds it deliberately.

## Finding 11 — Flagship roster defaults almost entirely to GM/front-office roles

**Damage: low-moderate.** The founder mandate notes "not every student plays a GM." Of the nine flagship experiences V5 recommends building or refounding (p.40 table), the great majority cast the student as owner, GM, scout, analyst, or agent: Cap Builders, League Balance Lab, Value & Signal Room, Draft Uncertainty Lab, Cap Crash Control Room, League & Contract Deal Room, Model Room, Draft Asset Market. Only "Your Market" clearly casts students as buyers/sellers rather than front-office decision-makers, and Small Markets/League Balance touches fan/player/owner stakeholder roles. There is no explicit design principle in V5 (comparable to its explicit anti-multiplayer-default and anti-quiz doctrines) that guards against every flagship defaulting to the GM perspective. This is a quiet, structural risk rather than a stated violation.

---

## Finding 12 — "Do not charge pilots" doctrine conflicts with the Day 1–15 commercial-schema build order

**Damage: moderate (restated from Finding 1 for the ledger).** Page 63's "Do now / Do not do" table for the "Proof: now" phase says: *"Do not do: Charge pilots, build public marketplace, promote statewide results, or add consumer subscriptions."* Page 72's Day 1–15 build window nonetheless instructs building the Product/Plan/Subscription/Entitlement/AccessGrant/SponsorGrant schema before any lesson ships. These two passages of the same document disagree about sequencing. A team executing literally will build billing infrastructure it has just been told not to monetize yet.

## What survives review

For balance: V5's core learning-loop doctrine (enter a system → decide → see consequences → revise → explain, p.7), its anti-quiz/anti-leaderboard language (pp.14–15), its shared-core-stays-small build constraint (p.11, even though violated in practice per Finding 6), its "multiplayer is a mechanism not a goal" doctrine, its facilitator-control-room and data-truth rules (pp.31, 34), and its 76-record simulation audit methodology are genuinely useful and largely consistent with the founder mandate. The four-module Track 101 spine is preserved intact. These parts should not be thrown out along with the findings above — only the commerce/platform/count/scope material needs to be quarantined from the near-term Track 101 rebuild.

## Decision packets

### DECISION REQUIRED: Should Parts IV–V (commerce, subscription, control-plane, API/marketplace architecture) be excluded from the near-term Track 101 refoundation scope entirely?

**WHY IT MATTERS:** V5's own build sequence (p.72) schedules commercial-entitlement schema work into the first 15 days, directly conflicting with the founder mandate of "no commerce/enterprise features" and with V5's own "do not charge pilots" doctrine (p.63). If unresolved, engineers may reasonably read V5 as authorizing early billing/entitlement work.

**EVIDENCE:** Findings 1, 6, 7, 12 above (pp.52–74, 52–56, 63, 72).

**OPTION A:** Formally strike Parts IV and V (and Appendix E's Days 1–15 commercial-schema tasks) from the current build mandate. Revisit only after Track 101 has shipped and been taught live.

**OPTION B:** Keep Parts IV–V as background context only ("read for orientation, not for build order") but do not let any later agent cite them as authorization to build entitlement, subscription, or pricing infrastructure.

**OPTION C:** Adopt V5's Parts IV–V as-is on the theory that the identity/session data model is cheap to build now and expensive to retrofit later.

**RECOMMENDATION:** Option A. The founder mandate is unambiguous ("no commerce/enterprise features"), and nothing about teaching Track 101 in a grades 5-6 classroom requires a Plan/Subscription/SponsorGrant schema to exist first.

**CONFIDENCE:** High.

**COST OF BEING WRONG:** Low if Option A is chosen and commerce infrastructure is genuinely needed later — it can be built when needed. High if Option C-style thinking is inherited implicitly — engineering time and design attention get diverted from lesson/simulation quality (the actual product bar) toward billing plumbing for a monetization strategy nobody has validated.

### DECISION REQUIRED: Should the Phase-2 "Ramaz Alpha" pilot set (p.47) be re-scoped to Track 101 (grades 5–6) only, dropping the three Track 201 lessons?

**WHY IT MATTERS:** As written, half of V5's recommended first live-validation set (3 of 6 lessons) targets grades 7–8, outside the current refoundation mandate. Brayden's scarce live-teaching hours during the proof phase are the project's most valuable resource; V5 is currently recommending half of them be spent outside the mandate.

**EVIDENCE:** Finding 3 above (pp.17–18, 24–27, 46–47).

**OPTION A:** Re-scope the Alpha pilot to six Track 101 lessons (drawing from the twelve-lesson Track 101 map on pp.20–23) so all near-term validation directly serves the grades 5–6 mandate.

**OPTION B:** Keep the mixed 101/201 pilot set as V5 recommends, on the theory that Track 201 evidence also stress-tests the shared engine/contracts that Track 101 will reuse.

**RECOMMENDATION:** Option A, unless the founder has separately decided Track 201 is in scope for this refoundation (the CONTEXT provided to this review names only Track 101).

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium — Option B has a legitimate engineering argument (shared kernels benefit from harder test cases), but it should be a deliberate tradeoff the founder makes, not an inherited default.

**COST OF BEING WRONG:** Medium. Wasted pilot capacity on out-of-mandate content is recoverable but not free — it delays the grades 5-6 evidence the founder actually needs to evaluate the refoundation.
