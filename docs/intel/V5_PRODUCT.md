## 2. The Economics product thesis (core, load-bearing)

Stated thesis (p.16, boxed as PRODUCT THESIS): *"BOW Economics turns the classroom into a legible economic system. Students receive roles, incentives, constraints, and information; make decisions that matter; affect one another when the concept requires it; create the data Brayden reveals; change one rule; and leave able to explain the mechanism and its boundary."*

This thesis explicitly covers only "the immediate Economics × Enrichment implementation, not the full future Economics pillar" (p.16) — i.e., it describes Track 101/201 at Ramaz specifically, which is exactly this project's scope.

**What the product is** (p.16): a connected lesson experience (hook → teaching → interaction → live state → data reveal → theory → debrief), not slides plus a separate site; a portfolio of mechanic families under one simulation/lesson contract; a three-surface live system (student device, facilitator control room, class display) *when needed*; a research lab where Ramaz use generates the receipts needed to improve/transfer the product.

**What the product is not** (p.16–17): a portal of 76 equally-promoted links; a quiz library wrapped in sports language; a universal multiplayer engine forced onto every lesson; a substitute for Brayden's teaching or an automated causal claim; a miniature sports-front-office course whose models are treated as universal economics.

**The exceptional-lesson bar** (p.17, six criteria a lesson must hit): Economics truth (agents, incentives, constraints, information, actions, feedback, dynamics, and model boundary all explicit); Decision quality (no highlighted answer — student manipulates the relationship and commits); Social necessity (other students matter only where their actions actually change prices/resources/info/bargaining/collective outcomes — this is a real design constraint, not decoration); Debrief power (system produces patterns/comparisons Brayden can explain from the class's own data); Room operability (one instructor can run it without becoming a sysadmin); Student pull (interaction legible fast, consequences visible, students want another run).

**Doctrine table** (p.14–15): Economics-first-architecture-second; subject ≠ program arm; live only when interdependence matters (multiplayer is a mechanism, not a quality badge); debrief is product (a sim that hides the mechanism is incomplete even if fun); data describes before it explains (class outcomes are teaching material — causality needs the model, comparison, and instructor explanation); economics creates the drama (scarcity/incentives/uncertainty/information/bargaining/collective outcomes replace XP and trivia); no simulation-count goal; no shallow World multiplication (a new World must change role/institution/narrative meaning/mechanic, not just nouns and art); no grading theater (Economics prioritizes reasoning, participation, class data, debrief, engagement — not scored mastery); no LMS expansion.

## 3. Everything V5 says specifically about Track 101

### 3.1 Curriculum structure and grade band
V5 "preserves the latest coherent 24-lesson structure: four modules and three lessons in each track" as the current working curriculum for both tracks, treating an earlier 4-lesson-per-module outline as superseded provenance (p.2, p.17). **Track 101 = grades approximately 5–6** per this task's framing (V5's own table header on p.18 says "grades approximately 5–6"; note the founder brief for this project frames Track 101 as grades 5-6 while V5's broader company sections sometimes say grades 5-8 — this is a minor internal inconsistency worth flagging, not a hard contradiction). Track 201 = grades ~7–8, "decision-first, more quantitative... more comfortable with uncertainty, multi-period consequences, and model disagreement" (p.17) — Track 201 is out of this project's scope but V5 treats it as Track 101's harder sibling, useful for contrast.

**Design intent for Track 101 specifically** (p.20, opening line of the lesson-by-lesson map): *"Track 101 should feel concrete, social, and visually legible. Students can reason deeply without heavy arithmetic. Use fewer variables, short instructions, visible meters, pairs or teams where helpful, and class comparisons that turn intuition into language."* This is V5's single clearest statement of pedagogical register for the grade band this project targets.

### 3.2 The Track 101 four-module spine (p.18, WORKING DECISION — Brayden confirmation needed per p.50)
| Module | L1 | L2 | L3 | Progression arc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 — Caps & Payrolls | Salary Cap Basics | Luxury Tax Basics | Cap Jail / Second Apron | Rule → marginal cost → flexibility lost |
| M2 — Money in Motion | Tickets, TV & Jerseys | Small Markets, Big Money | Why the Cap Exists | Revenue flow → sharing → institution design |
| M3 — The Numbers Game | Moneyball 101 | Stats vs. Scouts | Metrics That Matter | Efficiency → competing evidence → measurement |

This project's brief says the module spine is founder-fixed but the ~3 lessons per module may be redesigned/reordered/merged/split — V5's table is exactly this granularity and gives per-lesson detail below.

### 3.3 Per-lesson design (V5's "V5 classroom form" recommendations, p.20–23)
For each of the 12 Track 101 lessons V5 records a concept target, the source activity (often weak per V5's critique), a proposed "V5 classroom form" (live-system redesign), the class-generated data it produces, and a second-run/comparison idea. Condensed:

- **101.1.1 Salary Cap Basics → "Three Leagues, Three Rules."** Same mini-roster built under hard/soft/no-cap rule cards; rerun changes the rule, not the player pool (p.20).
- **101.1.2 Luxury Tax Basics → "Tax Line."** Live ownership meter (payroll, marginal tax, cost, benefit computed by system); round 2 moves near a bracket edge (p.20).
- **101.1.3 Cap Jail/Second Apron → "Flexibility Trap."** Build first, shock (deadline problem) arrives, students discover which repair tools remain — build→shock→adapt, directly teaches opportunity cost/path dependence (p.20–21).
- **101.2.1 Tickets, TV & Jerseys → "Revenue River."** Fan/media/sponsor/league/team/player roles feed a *live flow map* instead of a static diagram (p.21).
- **101.2.2 Small Markets, Big Money → "League Balance Lab."** Unequal starting markets, class vote on a revenue-sharing rule, then roster-build under the resulting budgets — genuine collective-outcome mechanic (p.21).
- **101.2.3 Why the Cap Exists → "League in a Box refound."** Choose cap+sharing rule+exception, simulate several seasons, defend the objective; "no rule is labeled correct" (p.21–22).
- **101.3.1 Moneyball 101 → "Value Draft."** Fixed-budget draft from a shared pool; leaderboard separates total talent from efficiency (p.22).
- **101.3.2 Stats vs. Scouts → "Signal Room."** Scout and Analyst roles get *different private evidence*, must share/persuade/compromise before signing — an information-asymmetry mechanic unusual for this grade band (p.22).
- **101.3.3 Metrics That Matter → "Metric Builder."** Students weight two measures, watch a ranking reorder live, defend the metric — teaches proxy/measurement distortion (p.22).
- **101.4.1 Why the Draft Isn't a Ranking → "Draft Distribution Lab."** Many parallel drafts of the same strategy; display plots the outcome *distribution*, defeating the "one draft = proof" fallacy (p.23). The audited prototype already shows this by replaying one strategy 20 times (scores 40–93) — V5's highest-confidence Track-101 asset (p.79).
- **101.4.2 Floor, Ceiling & Prospect Pyramid → "Prospect Portfolio."** Allocate three picks across safe/balanced/high-variance profiles; rerun flips contender↔rebuilding context on the same board (p.23).
- **101.4.3 Fit vs. Best Player Available → "Draft Room Fit vs. BPA."** Source material has "no dominant heuristic" — rebuilders/contenders reward different choices; V5 says preserve that property rather than resolve it (p.23).

### 3.4 Lesson architecture library (applies to Track 101, p.28)
V5 rejects one fixed lesson template in favor of six named, reusable lesson *forms*, each of which "protects three moments: a student commits before the answer is known; the system produces interpretable feedback; the lesson closes with explanation":
1. Predict → system → reveal → theory (best for emergent patterns/misconceptions)
2. Teach a little → run → teach → rerun (a rule students can feel before formalizing)
3. Role briefing → negotiation → debrief (private information, bargaining, coalition)
4. Build → shock → adapt (cap, portfolio, operations, path dependence)
5. Competing models → decision → new signal (analytics, uncertainty, evidence)
6. Institution design → simulate → defend (policy, revenue sharing, caps)

This is a WORKING SPEC-level design vocabulary, not a mandate that every Track 101 lesson use all six.

### 3.5 Default classroom rhythm (WORKING SPEC, p.28–29)
A default **55-minute** Ramaz rhythm (explicitly "not a stopwatch," a default only): 0–5 hook+prediction; 5–12 compact teaching; 12–15 join+role; 15–27 round 1; 27–34 reveal; 34–40 theory; 40–49 change/round 2; 49–55 debrief+exit. Part II's section header separately describes lessons as "50–60 minute Ramaz lessons" (p.16) — treat 50–60 as the operative range and 55 as the illustrative default.

### 3.6 Track differentiation in visual/interaction design (p.42)
Track 101 direction: "Broadcast clarity: larger type, fewer simultaneous variables, concrete icons, explicit state changes, warm energy, pair-friendly interaction." (Track 201 direction, for contrast: "Front-office depth: denser comparison, ranges and uncertainty, private information, multi-period state, model disagreement — without spreadsheet clutter.") Shared: same feedback grammar, accessibility, session states, control semantics, and public/private/estimated/final visual distinction.

### 3.7 Curriculum-change recommendations for Track 101/201 (p.18–19, WORKING SPEC)
- Preserve the 4×3 structure and most lesson titles for Ramaz continuity.
- Move every fact likely to go stale (cap thresholds, tax brackets, media deals, contract examples) into *dated instructor notes*, not the durable concept spine — durable teaching content should not depend on real-world sports facts changing year to year.
- Add explicit model-boundary language to every sports example so students know what the simplified system omits (this is a recurring V5 doctrine, restated at p.19, p.30, p.116: label what the model leaves out).
- Create a separate "Future Curriculum Opportunities" shelf (market formation, specialization/trade, public goods/commons, competition/market power, externalities, coordination) and explicitly **do not insert these into Track 101/201 until the current sequence is taught and reviewed** (p.19; full detail in Appendix C, p.109). V5 picks "The Ownership Equation" as current but says this "must be confirmed in the formal curriculum registry before slide redesign" (p.18) — a live open question, not resolved evidence.

## 4. Lesson-system and simulation design principles (subject-general, apply to Track 101)

- **Two invariants for every BOW learning experience** (p.7–8): (1) it must end in *use* — decide, construct, negotiate, allocate, explain, or revise, never merely finish content; (2) it must emit evidence appropriate to its job — for Economics that's usually "class patterns and reasoning," not individual mastery scores.
- **What the identity does *not* mean** (p.8): not every lesson is multiplayer; not every consequence is a win/loss cartoon — some are distributions, opportunity costs, options lost, counterpart reactions, or revealed uncertainty. Supports the founder constraint that multiplayer is a mechanic, not a goal.
- **Mechanic-first selection process** (p.36): (1) name the concept and misconception students must experience; (2) choose the mechanism that makes it manipulable/consequential/observable; (3) choose the motif whose real institutions make it easiest to understand; (4) write the model boundary, strip false-economics story elements; (5) only then design art/terminology/variants. Sports is a *motif*, not the definition of Economics (p.11); a flavor/case-variant/motif/World hierarchy (p.36) governs how much "sameness" different sports reskins share.
- **Economics mechanics taxonomy** (p.35): 12 mechanic families (allocation under constraint, continuous market, auction, bargaining & coalition, trade & exchange, production, competition & entry, public goods & commons, information & signaling, forecasting & model risk, shock & adaptation, portfolio/intertemporal, coordination & network, institution design). V5's own coverage judgment: **"BOW can teach the current sports-business curriculum well, but it does not yet demonstrate the breadth of an Economics simulation system"** — rich in roster allocation/drafting/front-office, thin in market formation, production, public goods, labor, competition, externalities (p.35) — a self-assessed gap, not classroom-tested.
- **Event→debrief chain** (p.33): capture events → calculate only declared metrics → detect patterns against transparent thresholds → render 1–3 graphs → *show what happened before the model that may explain it* → offer comparison/counterfactual only when interpretable → close with student explanation. Guardrail: "the system may describe what happened; the teacher and model explain why" (p.33) — the platform must not manufacture causal certainty.
- **Data truth rules** (p.34): show sample size and invalid actions; separate observed result / model interpretation / instructor hypothesis visually; never compare groups with different rules without naming the difference; never treat one stochastic outcome as proof; never calculate false precision; never retain student names needlessly.
- **Controlled productive chaos** (p.30): explicit before/during/freeze/recover/after states so one instructor can "freeze the economy and reclaim attention immediately."
- **Anti-cockpit rules** (p.31–32): ≤7 primary live controls; no raw event stream/DB vocabulary on the teaching surface; no shock button without an explanation and data-reset preview; no AI interpretation shown as fact without citing the pattern; no control that overrides a committed choice without a visible audit record.
- **Simulation factory / flagship gate** (p.70–71): 10-step process (name the learning job → smallest honest mechanic → prototype decision loop → run design-time model for degenerate strategies → one vertical slice → pass truth gates → test with students → **test non-founder transfer** → promote or archive → extract kernel only after 2+ reuses) with 8 gates (Decision, System, Learning, Fun/pull, Facilitation, Truth, Safety/access, Technical, Commercial). Transfer step explicitly: "a non-founder facilitator prepares, launches, runs, recovers, and debriefs without Brayden silently rescuing the session" (p.70) — V5's check against founder-dependence, a named top risk (p.49).

## 5. Classroom/product quality standards and success metrics

**Success metrics are session-level, not point-accumulation** (p.47, non-negotiable): "V5 measures whether the product creates a teachable system, not whether students accumulated points. Metrics are session-level unless a validated study supports a stronger claim." Named targets (WORKING SPEC, unvalidated): median join <60s, 95% ready within 2 min, no account required; first consequential action within 4 min of join, ≤1 rules rescue per team; freeze acknowledged <2s, restore/reassign <30s; no unrecovered session loss; declared metrics within 10s of close, meaningful reveal startable within 90s; "most students can state the mechanism, use one piece of class evidence, and name one boundary or changed condition" (V5's closest Track-101 learning-outcome bar); voluntary strategy debate/replay as a student-pull signal; three non-BOW adults prep in ≤30 min and would run the lesson again (teacher-transfer bar).

**Adversarial review (p.48)** stress-tests the model from ten angles, e.g. "Track 101 student" (risk: rules/math/dashboards bury the fun and the decision; response: fewer variables, system-computed arithmetic, larger feedback, fast action, concrete consequences) and "Curriculum critic" (risk: platform ambition pulls away from the supplied 101/201 curriculum; response: preserve 4×3 structure, map every flagship to a lesson job). This is V5 self-critiquing, not external validation.

**Unresolved risks (p.49):** founder dependence (test: non-founder facilitator trial); real-time complexity (test: 3 vertical slices, load/reconnect tests, strict "live only when needed"); student sample (one school may not generalize; test: record grade/device/motif response, later cross-school test); sports staleness (test: dated instructor notes, versioned data packs); false evidence (dashboards can imply causality without support; test: session-bound claim language, explicit data-truth states).

## 6. Grade level, pacing, and teacher operation

- Track 101 ≈ grades 5–6; Track 201 ≈ grades 7–8 (p.18). Company-level sections elsewhere describe the broader addressable band as "grades 5–8" (p.4, p.16, p.51–52) — this is the umbrella product-market framing, not a contradiction of the Track 101 grade band, but worth noting the document isn't perfectly consistent on whether "5–6" or "5–8" is the precise Track 101 audience.
- Lesson length: 50–60 minutes typical Ramaz period (p.16), with a 55-minute default internal rhythm breakdown (p.28–29, explicitly "a default, not a template prison" — a negotiation may need one long run, a draft-distribution lab may need several short runs).
- Room assumptions (p.29): one Brayden-equivalent facilitator, a shared display, one device per student or team; accounts off by default (short session code + safe alias/role); network/device/student failure is treated as a first-class design requirement, not an ops footnote; "controlled productive chaos" must be freezable and recoverable in seconds.
- Student join flow (p.30): open URL/QR → enter 4–6 character class code → enter name/initials/alias per privacy setting → receive role+private info+one-sentence goal → readiness check → target median join time under 60 seconds.
- Grouping rules (p.30): individual best for forecasting/measurement/personal strategy where peers don't change state; pairs best where "reasoning benefits from talk and one device... useful in Track 101 and negotiation teams"; teams where organizational tradeoffs and role division matter; whole-class network where trades/prices/public goods/coalitions make interdependence the actual concept — avoid whole-class structure when interaction is "only simultaneous single-player."
- Teacher-transfer is treated as an unproven hypothesis with a concrete test: "three non-BOW adults prepare in ≤30 minutes and would run the lesson again" (p.47, also p.49 "Founder dependence" risk and p.70 transfer-test step in the flagship gate). No teacher-transfer evidence exists yet per V5's own admission.

## 7. What this means for the current mission (synthesis, not new claims)

V5's Track 101 material is unusually actionable and directly on-point for this project: it already proposes a full 4×3 lesson-by-lesson redesign with named classroom mechanics, class-generated data fields, and rerun/comparison ideas for every one of the 12 current Track 101 lessons (Section 3.3 above). It also supplies a reusable lesson-form vocabulary, a facilitator-control model, a debrief-recipe contract, and explicit quality gates that any later Track 101 redesign work should be checked against. However, **every one of these Track 101 proposals is unvalidated** — V5 repeatedly and explicitly flags that no student-run or non-founder-facilitator evidence exists yet (p.2, p.39, p.47, p.49), that the existing curriculum PDF contains "outdated or contradictory sports facts" that durable architecture should not depend on (p.111), and that even its strongest-audited Track 101 assets (Cap Crash, Draft Room Fit vs BPA, Why the Draft Isn't a Ranking) are "KEEP... provisional until live classroom validation" (p.75, p.38).

No consequential ambiguity in this reading rose to the level of requiring a DECISION PACKET for the CEO — V5 already names its own open questions (the L3 title ambiguity at p.18, the grade-band 5-6-vs-5-8 framing, and the ~9 "provisional KEEP" simulations) as things for Brayden/BOW to confirm before further build, and this report surfaces them for downstream agents rather than adjudicating them.
